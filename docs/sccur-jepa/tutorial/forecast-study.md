# What can the last pose tell us about the next step?

Imagine pausing a walking video as the left heel rises. Could you mark where the left ankle will appear a fraction of a second later? Would a wrong guess tell you whether the person has a gait abnormality? Notebooks 20–26 turn these into two separately testable questions. You can follow the trail below with a spreadsheet and the linked files, even if you cannot run pose estimation.

These seven notebooks form the later **reference-controlled benchmark**. Earlier notebooks in this project trained a skeletal joint embedding predictive architecture (S-JEPA) to predict learned representations. Here, the researchers first tested how much they could explain with a simpler model that predicts **coordinates**. Throughout this lesson, the 40.8% result belongs to the model trained on current posture. The initial S-JEPA probe experiments used a different protocol. ## 20 · Count recordings before counting predictions

A single uploaded video can contain several annotated walks. Each walk can yield multiple overlapping clips. In this study, a **recording** is the original video, a **sequence** is an annotated walk and a **window** is a short segment with a visible past and a future to be predicted. If two windows contain the very same steps, putting one in training and the other in a test would make the test misleadingly familiar. The group split keeps all related windows together. In fact, 17 pairs of retained sequence IDs share some original recording frames. A new sequence ID alone cannot guarantee new footage or a new person. [Notebook 20](../../../experiments/aaai-worldmodels/20_cohort_identity_and_coverage.ipynb) · [Sequence audit](../../../experiments/aaai-worldmodels/docs/draft03/reviewer_support/sequence_audit.md)

These are the walks with sufficient observations in the selected five categories of the Gait Abnormality Video Dataset (GAVD). The full annotation catalog holds 1,874 sequences. Visibility requirements exclude some walks, so these are results about the eligible sample. The names in the first column come from the dataset. A recording count describes videos, and a participant can potentially appear in more than one. [Cohort table](../../../experiments/aaai-worldmodels/docs/draft03/cohort.csv) · [Coverage and exclusions](../../../experiments/aaai-worldmodels/docs/reference_v2/data_summary.md)

A pose estimator places landmarks on the body in each frame. The team selected 16 joints, subtracted the hip midpoint and expressed distances in units of the apparent torso length estimated from past frames. This lets a video of a large figure and a video of a small figure share a comparable coordinate system. Camera angle still matters. Inputs cover 16 samples at nominally 10 observations per second, or 1.5 seconds between the first and last input. The model forecasts both knees, ankles, heels and front-of-foot landmarks at eight offsets, 0.1 through 0.8 seconds later. Actual frame times are retained. Only sufficiently confident observations contribute to error. These measurements describe projected, body-relative motion in a video. ## 21 · Build a reference for what counts as surprising

First try **persistence**. Predict that each joint will stay at its latest observed position. If the leg barely moves, this forecast can do well even when the walk is visibly restricted. This is why the study also scores **low movement**. It assigns a higher anomaly score when the eventual positions stay closer to their starting points. It needs the future video to calculate this score.

The more complex score asks whether a recording was unusually easy **or** unusually hard to forecast relative to normal walking with similar past motion and missingness. The researchers fit the forecaster using normal recordings, estimated the expected error from motion and missingness using predictions for examples held out of the respective fits, then calibrated the residual error against a separate group of normal recordings. An outer test recording contributed to none of these fits. Calibration used only seven or eight normal recordings at a time. Averaging three rotations reduces dependence on one assignment but still gives a coarse reference. The resulting scores can rank recordings; a clinical alarm threshold would need additional evidence. [Notebook 21](../../../experiments/aaai-worldmodels/21_reference_scores_and_calibration.ipynb) · [Evaluation contract](../../../experiments/aaai-worldmodels/docs/reference_v2/evaluation_contract.md)

## 22 · Hide evidence without revealing the answer

One **token** here is one joint's two image coordinates at one time. During training the study deliberately hid 40% of eligible past tokens. A joint that was already missing could not be selected. Observed shoulders and hips used for normalization and each joint’s latest anchor were protected. At evaluation, each model got its full naturally available inputs. The experiment scored every method on the same observed future targets. The notebook also performs a fresh pose estimate on a decoded frame and displays how a real mask changes the input. [Notebook 22](../../../experiments/aaai-worldmodels/22_structured_masking_laboratory.ipynb)

Would it help to hide connected limb joints for several frames, rather than choose positions randomly? The team compared uniform random masks, time blocks, connected limb tubes, matched random joint groups, motion-balanced masks and motion-focused masks, plus a model trained without masks. The backbone, training population and budget stayed fixed. One safeguard erases hidden coordinates **before** filling the input features: otherwise a later filled value could secretly copy the hidden answer. Try the notebook's test that modifies a hidden coordinate while holding the mask fixed. The visible features must stay identical. [Model contract](../../../experiments/aaai-worldmodels/docs/reference_v2/model_contract.md)

## 23 · Fit the simplest model that can learn motion

Persistence cannot learn. A **ridge regression** can learn that a particular posture tends to precede a particular displacement, while penalizing very large coefficients. It predicts that displacement relative to the latest observed positions. These coefficients are the weights the model gives each input when combining positions into a prediction. Other models received one leg’s history, both legs’ histories, or current posture plus recent velocity and reliability information. A small neural network predicted future coordinates in the masking comparison. It had a different objective from predicting teacher embeddings in S-JEPA.

The notebook fits a small teaching model on real normal examples. Its *training* error merely checks that the fitting procedure runs. For the study results, five outer folds held out whole recording groups, and three inner normal-recording folds chose model settings. Settings were chosen before using the outer test results. A useful contamination check changes every future coordinate and its availability while leaving the past untouched. Predictions must stay the same. After the future arrives, availability determines which predicted coordinates can be graded. ## 24 · Grade the forecasts, then grade the rankings

**Worked example.** Suppose the last observed ankle x-coordinate is 0.20 torso lengths, the future measurement is 0.50 and the learned model predicts 0.40. Persistence incurs squared error (0.50 − 0.20)² = 0.09. The learned forecast incurs (0.50 − 0.40)² = 0.01. These invented coordinates illustrate one calculation. In the real study, error was averaged over observed coordinates within each leg and then over legs, windows, sequences and recordings. This prevents a single long video from dominating the primary mean. [Notebook 24](../../../experiments/aaai-worldmodels/24_source_and_external_evaluation.ipynb)

On the **29 held-out normal recording groups**, persistence had mean squared error (MSE) 0.12126 and current posture had MSE 0.07174. Each normal recording was tested in its own held-out fold. Relative reduction is (0.12126 − 0.07174) / 0.12126 ≈ **40.8%**. The paired 95% recording-bootstrap interval is **33.3%–47.4%**. A bootstrap repeatedly resamples whole recordings and compares both models on each resample. Its interval describes uncertainty conditional on the saved predictions. It does not cover every effect of retraining, unknown repeated participants, or a new camera setting. After examining the original results, the researchers asked whether giving each of the 141 normal sequences equal weight would change the finding. This later, or **post hoc**, check gave a **41.6%** reduction. These are two weightings of the same observations. [Forecast table](../../../experiments/aaai-worldmodels/docs/draft03/forecasts.csv) · [Weighting checks and intervals](../../../experiments/aaai-worldmodels/docs/draft03/sequence_weighting_results.json)

In the mask comparison, MSE multiplied by 1,000 was **77.61** for uniform random, **79.62** for connected limbs and **79.16** for motion-balanced masks. Smaller is better. These values use the predictions averaged over three training seeds before scoring. The structured policies tested here did not improve the uniform benchmark. This was an exploratory comparison of a compact coordinate predictor. [Complete forecast results](../../../experiments/aaai-worldmodels/docs/draft03/complete_forecasts.csv)

Now ask a different question. Draw one abnormal-annotated recording and one normal-annotated recording from the same test fold. How often does the abnormal one receive the higher anomaly score? **AUROC** measures this probability, with half credit for ties and 0.5 indicating chance ranking. The researchers combined folds according to their number of eligible pairs. On GAVD the conditional forecast score reached **0.462**, versus **0.811** for low observed future movement. Thus the low-movement score ranked about 81 out of 100 pairs correctly on an expected-frequency scale. That is not the percentage of patients correctly diagnosed. Their paired difference, forecast minus low movement, was **−0.348 [−0.506, −0.191]**. The earlier bilateral excess-error score had pooled AUROC 0.793; it uses a different scoring rule and also compares pairs from different folds. [Ranking definitions and data](../../../experiments/aaai-worldmodels/docs/draft03/rankings.csv) · [Paired comparisons](../../../experiments/aaai-worldmodels/docs/draft03/figures/ranking_comparison_data.csv)

**External transfer.** The team froze the models, scaling, normal references and score directions, then applied them to a separate knee osteoarthritis/Parkinson’s/normal video dataset. Here the evaluation unit was a *coded participant*. There were 773 windows from 168 recordings of 90 participants, including 27 normal, 49 osteoarthritis and 14 Parkinson’s. The forecast score reached AUROC **0.591** and low movement **0.762**. Their paired difference **−0.172 [−0.357, 0.009]** includes zero, so this dataset leaves the size and even the direction of the population difference uncertain. It supplies no demonstrated forecasting-score advantage. ## 25 · Look for explanations other than physiology

If a camera records a person from the front instead of the side, projected ankle displacement can change even if the walk is identical. The notebook explores recording view, lost landmarks, coordinate noise, timing shifts and external acquisition differences. These tests modify observations; they cannot simulate muscle activation or disease progression. Matching recordings on available view, past movement and missingness reduced the GAVD sample from 91 to 40, with only 38 contributing to the matched ranking comparison. Conclusions about that restricted subset should not be extended automatically to everyone.

The external data also differ in age, native frame rate, markers, clothing and person-selection conditions. In one Parkinson’s participant’s recording an assistant overlapped the person, making identity continuity uncertain. A sensitivity check excluding that participant yielded forecast/low-movement AUROCs of 0.597/0.759 on 89 people; this exclusion also removed the only retained severe-Parkinson’s participant. Record what you lose when you impose a quality gate. ## Check your reasoning

1. **A friend randomly splits the 2,815 windows and calls the test independent. What should change?** Assign the original recording groups first and put all related sequences and windows in their recording’s fold. A window split can repeat nearly identical steps across training and test.
2. **If the future ankle is obscured, should a predictor omit its output?** No. Make a prediction for that ankle, but leave it out of the error calculation if it cannot be observed. A **query** is a request for one joint at a specified future time. During **inference**, the trained model answers each query using only the past. Letting future visibility change the inputs would leak information.
3. **Why test persistence if it is easy to beat on average?** It establishes how much error comes from movement since the last measurement. Restricted movement can yield very low persistence error and a strong low-movement ranking score.
4. **Can we call 41.6% a second replication of the 40.8% result?** Both estimates reuse identical saved predictions. The second answers a worthwhile sensitivity question about how to weight long versus short recordings.
5. **Why does 0.591 versus 0.762 not settle the external comparison?** The paired participant-bootstrap interval includes zero. Those observed values favor low movement, but the data leave meaningful uncertainty.
