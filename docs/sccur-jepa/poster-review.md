# Review of the SCCUR gait poster

The [previous poster](SCCUR-JEPA-v6.pptx) contained three useful experiments. A visitor had to move between columns to work out which model produced a number, which recordings it saw, and what comparison would answer the research question. The [revised PowerPoint](SCCUR-JEPA.pptx) gives the two classification tests the central column. The right column follows a separate forecasting model from its prediction to its test as an anomaly detector.

| Section reviewed | What a visitor could miss | Change in the revised poster |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Title and opening | The title identified the topic, but the reader had to search the abstract for the question. | A shorter masthead leaves space for a concrete motivation and a single research question. The navy masthead, typography, logos and underlined presenter follow the conference template. |
| Abstract | The summary gave the three headline results before readers could tell which samples belonged together. | The abstract names the dataset, explains what S-JEPA learns, identifies the new-recording result and explicitly introduces the separate forecasting test. A three-row table maps each task to its sample and recording counts. |
| Methods | Training and inference were split between two columns. The 33- and 16-landmark setups looked like stages in one experiment. | One workflow tracks a filmed walk, shows which landmarks each study used, illustrates the teacher and student viewing the same knee, and follows each classifier to its output. |
| Five-label recognition | Percentages drew attention away from the small denominator. The text mentioned shared videos, but omitted the test clips already seen in pretraining. | Each dot represents one of the 21 test clips. A split diagram shows how clips from the same source can reach both sides. The caption reports nine shared recordings, three exposed test clips, and a separate source-identification check using missing joints. |
| Recognition on unseen recordings | Readers could mistake 61.9% and 81.8% for successive scores on the same task. “Untrained encoder” could also sound like an untrained classifier. | The second panel states the binary endpoint and whole-recording split. It shows how six normal and eleven abnormal recordings contribute to balanced accuracy. The plot identifies the fitted classifier using random features. Its tie with pretrained features appears in the interpretation. |
| Forecasting | The ankle example, population improvement and anomaly claim were close together, but the size of an error and its value as a detector answer different questions. | A labeled trajectory shows a real example where the forecast misses a peak. The 40.8% reduction and its interval sit below it with the denominator of 29 normal recordings. A second graph compares a separate posture-and-velocity anomaly score with low movement across all 91 recordings. Its cohort table is in the same column. |
| Conclusions and next steps | The older action list gave limited guidance on what would resolve the competing explanations. | The final panel ties each proposal to a measured limitation. Holding out verified participants would test transfer across people. Marking foot contacts or knee motion and matching walking speeds would test condition-specific information beyond movement amount. |
| References, credit and print layout | Separate reference and acknowledgment bars competed with result headings. | A footer carries both references, the department and ASDRP acknowledgment. It keeps the center column available for evidence. The charts, workflows and tables are editable PowerPoint objects. |

The earlier gait-cycle phase comparison remains in the slide notes. It was measured on the 12 normal training clips and addressed a different outcome. The printed poster gives that space to the held-out trajectory and the low-movement comparison.

The numerical review used the [Study A overlap audit](../../experiments/penny/docs/faq/evidence/original/leakage_audit.csv), [Study B classifier release](../../experiments/aaai-worldmodels/docs/research_results.json), and the [forecasting benchmark](../../experiments/aaai-worldmodels/docs/draft03/README.md). The final print proof was checked as a complete page and as individual panels. The PDF contains one 48 × 34 inch landscape page.
