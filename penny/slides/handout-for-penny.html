How your neurological findings drive Gait-JEPA

A one-page guide to where your feature-to-condition mapping plugs into the new self-supervised gait model.

What you built

You went through all 82 gait features and, for Parkinson's and stroke separately, graded each one H, M, L, or NA by how directly it traces to the neurological mechanism of that condition. For every feature you recorded a neurological reason, a significance threshold, and a cited source. For example:

  • stride_time_cv is H for PD, because basal-ganglia rhythm failure makes stride timing vary a lot step to step.
  • knee_asymmetry is H for stroke, because a weakened side produces stiff-knee gait, a knee-flexion difference of at least 17 degrees.
  • walking_speed_ms is H for stroke, about 0.5 m/s versus about 1.4 normal, from reduced corticospinal drive.
  • stride_length_si is H for both, a symmetry index above about 16 percent is suspicious.

That grading is not a side note. It is the map that tells the model where to look.

The one thing to hold onto Your thresholds and grades are never used as training labels. The model first learns from lots of unlabeled walking video. Your mapping is used to aim that learning and then to grade what it learned. It shows up at three distinct stages.
Stage 1

Training aims the task

The model learns by hiding part of a walking clip and predicting it. Your H-priority list decides what we hide.

Hiding a whole limb over time forces it to encode the asymmetry features you flagged H (stride_length_si, hip / knee / ankle symmetry).

Hiding a whole time window forces it to encode the PD rhythm features (stride_time_cv) and the stroke slowing features (walking_speed_ms).

Your mapping is why the masking is mechanism-aware instead of random.

Stage 2

Evaluation grades it

We freeze the model and ask: did it learn your mechanisms without ever being told them?

Tiny probes predict each of your H-priority scalars from the model's representation, and we report how well (research question RQ3).

Your documented thresholds (symmetry index above 16 percent, stride_time_cv above 0.06, knee-flexion difference at least 17 degrees) are the pass or fail checks: does the probe land on the clinically correct side over 70 percent of the time?

Your CSV is literally the answer key for this stage.

Stage 3

Inference reads it out

For a brand-new walking clip, the model does not just output "stroke."

Using your mapping, it also reports named clinical scores: an asymmetry score, a rhythm-variability score, a postural-stability score, and a slowing score.

Each score is compared to your threshold to flag which mechanism looks abnormal.

The output is interpretable, not a black box.

Why this matters for the project

Your Parkinson's-versus-stroke distinction is built into the design as a testable hypothesis: both conditions share an asymmetry axis and a postural-instability axis, but Parkinson's keeps a distinct rhythm-and-variability axis while stroke keeps a distinct spatiotemporal-slowing axis. The probes in Stage 2 are how we check whether the model kept them separable, exactly as your research predicts they should be.

In one sentence: your mapping is the throughline of Gait-JEPA. It aims the masking in training, it is the answer key and threshold set in evaluation, and it turns a raw prediction into a clinical readout at inference.

Where your research could go next

The evaluation task has 5 conditions, but only 2 of them (Parkinson's and stroke) have your mapping so far. The highest-value next steps, in order:

  1. Cerebral palsy and myopathic first. They are already in the 5-class task, so mapping them makes the clinical-probe story cover the whole task instead of 2 of 5 conditions.
  2. Deepen Parkinson's and stroke where a source did not give a specific number. Firmer thresholds sharpen the Stage 2 threshold-agreement check.
  3. Skip for now the catch-all or non-clinical conditions (abnormal, style, exercise, inebriated) unless the evaluation set grows to include them.

A simple rule: map a condition when it is (a) in the evaluation set and (b) has a clear neurological mechanism. That points straight at cerebral palsy and myopathic next.
