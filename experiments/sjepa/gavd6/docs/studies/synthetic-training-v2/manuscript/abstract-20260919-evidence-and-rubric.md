# Evidence and fixed rubric for the abstract revisions

19 September 2026. This record supports seven manuscript drafts. It preserves the earlier studies and distinguishes writing improvements from changes that need additional evidence. No neural model was fitted, HAIC job submitted, sealed test set opened, or pending diagnostic result supplied by this revision exercise.

## Paper scope and conference relevance

The paper asks whether paired synthetic supervision can improve **offline 2D pose trajectories while preserving movement amplitude, timing and left–right relationships**, and whether predicting learned target features adds value beyond predicting coordinates from the same supervision. Offline means that a method can use the full observed window; this is not a forecasting or action-conditioned world-model experiment.

ICLR's relevant focus is representation learning and scientific understanding of what representations retain. The current call does not specify a narrower annual technical theme. Its reviewer guidance permits meaningful contributions without state-of-the-art performance, but the contribution still needs convincing evidence. The opportunity is a controlled explanation of restoration gains; an implementation plus an inconclusive pilot is not automatically a significant negative result. [Official 2027 call](https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2027/CallForPapers), [reviewer guidance](https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2027/ReviewerGuidelines).

The predecessor studies motivate the question rather than validate the new restorer. Laterality makes the consequences of changing joint identities explicit. Original synthetic training shows why an apparent gain needs an appropriate simpler control. Their populations, metrics and interventions are different, and their denominators cannot be pooled.

## Interpretation and literature boundary

Same-camera renders create aligned observations and reference projections, but the target joints approximate a landmark convention. A body-model joint center and an image-annotated landmark need not coincide. Hedlin and colleagues demonstrate a joint-regressor evaluation problem in a different SMPL/Human3.6M mapping; that supports a hypothesis and control, not a diagnosis of this SMPL-H/COCO experiment. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.00076), [official code](https://github.com/ubc-vision/joint-regressor-refinement).

S-JEPA establishes skeleton latent prediction for action recognition. SmoothNet studies temporal refinement, while PoseBERT and MotionBERT already learn motion representations using corrupted observations and motion-capture supervision. The novelty claim therefore cannot be “synthetic denoising,” “the first skeleton JEPA,” or “estimator-independent refinement.” The present design tests the incremental value of aligned target features under matched supervision and evaluates preservation separately. JEPA does not inherently force invariance to every anatomical distinction. [S-JEPA](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2024/papers_ECCV/papers/04755.pdf), [SmoothNet](https://github.com/cure-lab/SmoothNet), [PoseBERT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.10211), [MotionBERT](https://motionbert.github.io/).

“Left–right relationships” is the broad objective. The current preservation measurement is much narrower: signed horizontal ankle separation in the image plane and quantities derived from it. The laterality study's unanchored global-sign ambiguity is conditional on its symmetric observation setup; it is not a universal impossibility theorem for every video system. Independently verified anatomical anchors would be needed for an anatomical-side claim.

## Fixed scoring rubric

Each dimension receives a score from 0 to 10. **5 means a substantial unresolved weakness; 10 means no material weakness within the stated scope.** Scores below 5 indicate more severe or missing support; 6–7 indicate useful but limited evidence or presentation; 8–9 indicate strong execution with explicit remaining weaknesses. Narrow wording cannot make a two-person pilot statistically adequate for a broad preservation claim.

| Dimension | Weight | What is assessed | What could improve through revision? |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Conference relevance and contribution | 20% | A consequential question about representation learning and an identifiable contribution. | Explain the matched objective comparison; no credit for unsupported novelty or success. |
| Claim accuracy and evidence support | 20% | Alignment of every claim with its source, scope, baseline and evidence class. | Repair ambiguous baselines, label proxies and summaries, avoid causal overreach. |
| Evaluation and statistical rigor | 15% | Independent sampling, meaningful controls, supported metrics, seed uncertainty and transfer evaluation. | Explain limitations more clearly; **score stays 3.5** because the evidence does not change. |
| Scientific insight and related-work positioning | 15% | What the findings distinguish, and how the question differs from verified prior work. | Separate latent prediction, coordinate supervision and calibration without pretending the mechanism is settled. |
| Reproducibility | 10% | Available data, predictions, provenance, executable methods and reconstructable results. | Make provenance easier to inspect; **score stays 5.5** because prediction artifacts remain missing. |
| Clarity and narrative | 10% | A readable question, method, informative result and bounded interpretation. | Remove distracting details and explain necessary jargon. |
| Figures | 5% | Correct, readable visual explanation with appropriate evidence labels. | Common pipeline scored **7.5 in every version**; improved prose cannot create an empirical trajectory plot. |
| Submission fit | 5% | Appropriate scope, informative abstract, anonymous prose and compatibility with verified submission guidance. | Use a substantive abstract and defensible title. This dimension is not an acceptance prediction. |

The total is `sum(weight_percent × score / 10)`, out of 100. Component changes require reasons. Half-point judgments are not precise measurements; totals retain their arithmetic precision only to make the weighting checkable. The final version may tie an earlier version. These are authoring judgments informed by independent reviews, not ICLR reviewer scores or estimates of acceptance probability.

## What writing can fix, and what still needs work

**Revision now:** foreground the control comparison; distinguish paired supervision from JEPA's choice of prediction space; label synthetic projections; specify the timing denominator; connect the predecessor findings without conflating their cohorts; omit irrelevant real-video classification numbers and retrospective oracles.

**Analysis still to complete:** repair source verification and execute the three existing CPU checks. Fit pooled offsets and affine/ridge corrections only on training people and training extractors. Test reference coordinates against themselves before interpreting absent timing support. Inspect fixed, preselected trajectories and convergence separately for clean and corrupted conditions. A constant per-joint pixel offset preserves raw displacement, but frame-dependent reference scaling can change normalized ankle-separation quantities; calibration must therefore be evaluated on motion too.

**New empirical evidence:** increase independent people and motions; repeat the strongest comparisons across seeds; verify optimization and total compute; review anatomy independently; collect dense real temporal references before a transfer claim. If longer windows or additional camera views are needed for visible motion events, declare a new development experiment. Neither the old laterality test split nor a newly chosen favorable subset should be used to manufacture confirmation.

For execution, keep using the [single Slurm guide](../../../../slurm/synthetic-training-v2/README.md#repair-extraction-status-verification). These manuscript records add no alternative run instructions.
