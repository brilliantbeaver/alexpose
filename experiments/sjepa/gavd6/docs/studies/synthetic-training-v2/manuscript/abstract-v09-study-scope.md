# Abstract version 09 — study scope and planned evaluation

19 September 2026. Current working draft after independent scientific, contribution and writing reviews. The implemented method is central; the completed small experiment supplies preliminary evidence, and the expanded study is explicitly planned.

**Title:** Predictive Feature Learning for Pose Restoration: Position Accuracy and Motion Preservation

**Abstract**

Pose estimators convert video into sequences of joint positions used to measure movement, making it important to preserve motion as position errors are corrected. We investigate whether predicting learned features of reference poses improves 2D pose restoration beyond learning joint coordinates directly. We render videos from recorded 3D human movement and pair estimated poses with the body model's joint positions projected into the same images, including versions with blur and obstruction. We train a joint-embedding predictive architecture (JEPA) on these pairs to predict numerical features describing the reference poses from partially hidden estimated poses. The encoder, which converts the estimated poses into features, is then held fixed while a separate output network learns coordinate corrections. Comparisons use the same training pairs and include coordinate-based learning, an untrained fixed encoder with a trained output network, and simple joint-position corrections fitted only on training data. Preliminary evaluation on two people excluded from training, with one training run per neural method, finds that simple joint shifts achieve similar or larger reductions in visible-joint error than paired JEPA, while paired JEPA increases error in joint displacement over 0.20 seconds relative to uncorrected tracks. The planned main study will include more people and motions, repeated training runs, and evaluation on a pose-estimator family and combinations of blur and obstruction excluded from fitting. We will assess joint displacement and the amplitude and peak timing of horizontal ankle separation in the image, after checking whether references support each measurement and how coordinate scaling affects it. These comparisons will test whether feature prediction provides reproducible benefits beyond spatial correction and coordinate learning, with uncertainty estimated across people.

**Notes for the authors, outside the abstract**

The initial results are descriptive comparisons from two development people and one neural training seed, 17. The three pose estimators repeatedly measure the same movements. “Similar or larger” describes the observed reductions; it does not assert statistical equivalence. Simple joint shifts refer to the 24-parameter offset control, fitted on training people and training extractors, then applied without refitting. The [downloaded-results analysis](../results/postrun-analysis-20260919/README.md) retains the numerical evidence and its limitations. Version 08 remains the [detailed diagnostic abstract](abstract-v08-calibration-motion.md).

Projection means converting the synthetic body's 3D joint positions into 2D locations using the rendering camera. These references describe the model's joints; they do not establish anatomical or clinical accuracy. Joint displacement measures the change in a joint's position between two times. The ankle-separation measurements describe the horizontal left–right relationship in the image, with timing defined by peaks in that signal. Such peaks are not verified heel strikes. Checking reference support means determining which sequences have the valid and visible joint references needed for each measurement; scaling checks determine whether the normalization itself changes the signal.

The study asks whether feature prediction helps beyond the same coordinate supervision and simple spatial corrections. A novel and substantive result would require a well-supported answer that generalizes across independent people and motions, adequate training comparisons, and movement measurements whose behavior is understood. Additional runs will estimate effect sizes and uncertainty; they cannot be assumed to produce a statistically significant JEPA advantage. Real-video transfer remains a separate uncompleted evaluation.
