**Suggested title:** Spatial Calibration and Motion Preservation in Paired Synthetic Pose Restoration

**Abstract**

We investigate whether latent prediction improves 2D pose restoration beyond spatial calibration and coordinate-supervised learning while preserving movement. Controlled renders pair pose-estimator trajectories with projected synthetic joint references under blur and obstruction. A joint-embedding predictive architecture (JEPA) learns reference features, followed by a coordinate readout fitted to its frozen encoder. Comparisons include coordinate pretraining, direct restoration, a trained readout on an initialized encoder, and training-only spatial calibration. In an exploratory pilot with two development people and one seed, a 24-parameter joint-offset correction reduces visible-landmark error by 43.9–45.2% across three extractors, approximately matching or exceeding paired JEPA's gains. A 72-parameter affine correction improves coordinate and displacement errors for every extractor in this comparison, including the family excluded from fitting. Paired JEPA increases displacement error by 1.2–4.4% relative to unchanged tracks. Reference checks find that only 25% of records satisfy visible-ankle timing requirements, and frame-varying scale normalization alters reference peak counts. These findings motivate an evaluation that separates spatial correction from movement preservation and measures the contribution of latent prediction against matched controls. Broader generalization and real-video transfer remain unestablished.

The ranges above use [independently audited full-precision diagnostic tables](../results/postrun-analysis-20260919/README.md). The affine comparison is limited to the seven methods in that diagnostic report. Three extractors are repeated measurements on the same two development people; they are not independent cohorts. This abstract does not claim a new calibration algorithm, a novel generic pose-refinement task, successful motion preservation, or a statistically established JEPA benefit.
