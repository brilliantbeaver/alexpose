# Abstract version 10 — expanded results

19 September 2026. Revised after three independent adversarial reviews using the same weighted rubric across two rounds. This draft incorporates the completed eight-person, seed-17 development evaluation and preserves the distinction between initial findings and the planned broader study.

**Title:** Predictive Feature Learning for Pose Restoration: Position Accuracy and Motion Preservation

**Abstract**

Pose estimators turn video into sequences of joint positions used to measure movement, so correcting position errors should also preserve motion. We investigate whether predicting learned numerical features of reference poses improves two-dimensional pose restoration beyond direct training on joint coordinates. We render videos from recorded human movement and pair estimated poses with body-model joints projected into the images, including versions with blur and obstruction. A joint-embedding predictive architecture (JEPA) predicts reference features from partially hidden estimated poses; its feature extractor is then fixed while an output network learns coordinate corrections. An initial development evaluation of treadmill walking includes eight people excluded from training and three pose estimators, with one training run per neural method. Direct training reduces position error and error in position changes over 0.20 seconds relative to simple spatial corrections fitted on training data, although smoothing across neighboring frames sometimes achieves lower movement error. Paired JEPA adds little over controls with untrained features or reference sequences from different time windows. Exploratory evaluation including synthetic joints hidden from view shows amplified variation in horizontal ankle separation on average, with extra predicted peaks despite improved coordinate accuracy. Planned experiments will assess consistency across training runs and budgets, with uncertainty estimated across people.

**Evidence notes, outside the abstract**

The abstract is 203 words by whitespace-separated counting. It replaces version 09's two-person findings with the completed development evaluation on eight people, using seed 17 and 2,000 updates per pretraining/readout phase. Direct training uses 4,000 supervised updates; equal total update counts do not imply equal compute or equal coordinate-training budgets. The 24 training people are separate from the eight development people. The three pose estimators observe the same movements, so they do not triple the independent sample size. The [complete analysis](../results/seed17-complete-analysis-20260919/README.md) records the numerical comparisons, training histories and evidence limits.

Simple spatial corrections are joint shifts and per-joint affine corrections fitted using training people and training estimators. An affine correction can rescale and mix a joint's two coordinates as well as shift them. Direct training reduces mean visible-position error by 7.15–8.51% and 0.20-second displacement error by 6.17–8.31% relative to the affine control across the three estimators. The filtering qualification matters: the five-frame filter has lower displacement error for HRNet overall and for all three estimators under combined blur and obstruction. These are descriptive comparisons from one neural training seed, with exploratory person-level intervals in the analysis; the abstract makes no confirmatory significance claim.

The initialized control uses an untrained, fixed feature extractor with a trained coordinate-output network. The shuffled control uses reference windows from a different time within the same training person and rendering/extractor group; repeated treadmill motion can make those windows similar. “Adds little” summarizes the observed small differences and does not assert statistical equivalence or a general limitation of JEPA. The tested method restores observed two-dimensional sequences; it does not demonstrate future-motion forecasting.

The exploratory movement analysis includes valid synthetic joints that are hidden from view and uses one reference scale per window. The original visible-reference amplitude aggregate remains unsupported. “Amplified variation” describes mean per-window amplitude ratios above one, not amplification in every example. Horizontal ankle separation is the signed difference between the two ankle positions across the image, and its peaks are signal maxima rather than verified heel strikes. These body-model references do not establish anatomical or clinical accuracy, and real-video transfer remains untested.

The planned repetitions and budget comparisons will test whether these findings persist; they cannot be assumed to produce a significant JEPA advantage. Independent reference review and broader held evaluation remain necessary for stronger conclusions. Local checks reconstructed saved aggregates and verified file/code hashes; the original neural prediction arrays are absent from the download, so a new local replay of all predictions is not claimed.
