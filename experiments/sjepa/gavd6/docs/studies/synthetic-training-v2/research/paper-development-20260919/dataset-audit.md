# Dataset and reference-quality audit

19 September 2026. This memo combines the [completed local seed-17 analysis](../../results/seed17-complete-analysis-20260919/README.md) with primary dataset publications and official documentation checked for this task. Recommendations are proposed study design, not completed experiments. No data were downloaded, accounts created, access requests sent, or cluster jobs launched.

## Recommended decision

Keep AMASS as the controlled synthetic development source, and pursue **BioMotionLab MoVi as the main real-video evaluation** if access and synchronization checks pass promptly. MoVi offers a plausible route to substantially more independent people, while retaining synchronized reference motion. Human3.6M is a useful established replication benchmark if it is already accessible, but its usual two-person evaluation cannot establish broad population reliability. GAVD is valuable for the eventual clinical application, but it cannot currently supply the dense independent joint references needed for the restoration claim.

The fresh experiment should answer whether a chosen restoration method improves both joint positions and movement on reserved people and recordings, under a declared joint convention. It should not select whichever dataset yields a favorable JEPA result. The already inspected eight people remain development data.

## Dataset choices and limitations

| Dataset | Verified reference and population facts | Recommended role and main limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AMASS | The original release unifies optical-marker captures through MoSh++ body-model fitting; it provides motion/body parameters and visualization tools, with over 300 people reported in the original publication. Registration is required. [Official project](https://amass.is.tue.mpg.de/) | Synthetic training and controlled tests with known cameras and corruptions. These are model-derived reference joints, not independent manual anatomical labels or synchronized natural video from every constituent corpus. Do not count rendered views or textures as new people. |
| BioMotionLab MoVi | The paper reports 90 people and 21 actions, including walking. Capture round F has full-marker optical motion capture; calibrated stationary PG1/PG2 video is synchronized at 30 Hz with 120 Hz mocap. Phone cameras are neither calibrated nor synchronized. S1/S2 provide natural clothing but sparse optical references at head, wrists and ankles. [Original paper](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0253157) | Preferred real-video confirmation, starting with F/PG cameras. S1/S2 can later test ankle restoration under natural clothing; they do not provide full-body optical truth. Confirm actual eligible people and usable walking duration before fixing a sample-size promise. |
| Human3.6M | The original collection has 11 actors, calibrated four-view 50 Hz video and synchronized mocap. [Official description](https://vision.imar.ro/human3.6m/description.php) The widely used protocol trains on S1/S5/S6/S7/S8 and tests S9/S11. [Primary protocol example, Table 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1611.09813) Its skeletal positions derive from Vicon skeleton fitting and forward kinematics. [Dataset technical report](https://vision.imar.ro/human3.6m/human36m_techrep.pdf) | Strong controlled real-image replication with walking and other actions. Two standard test people limit person-level inference despite many frames. Use native joint definitions and camera projection; do not substitute COCO names and assume anatomical equivalence. |
| 3DPW | The original dataset has 60 sequences and **seven actors**, with 18 clothing styles; its reference poses combine video, IMUs and SMPL fitting. [Original paper, §5.3](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_ECCV_2018/papers/Timo_von_Marcard_Recovering_Accurate_3D_ECCV_2018_paper.pdf) The official release has train/validation/test folders and several explicitly defined protocols. [Evaluation page](https://virtualhumans.mpi-inf.mpg.de/3DPW/evaluation.html) | Useful optional outdoor/moving-camera stress test. Seven people and estimated reference poses limit population power and claims about small anatomical errors. Eighteen clothed models are not eighteen independent people. Do not assume the sequence split is a subject-disjoint split without inspecting identity metadata. |
| MPI-INF-3DHP | Its references come from multiview markerless capture, and its test scenes include indoor/outdoor activity. The authors revised TS3/TS4 annotations in 2018 and TS6 in 2019. [Official project and errata](https://vcai.mpi-inf.mpg.de/3dhp-dataset/) The original training protocol uses eight subjects. [Paper, supplement §4.2.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1611.09813) | Optional replication if already available. Record release/hash and preserve valid-frame masks and original timestamps. Verify consecutive valid reference coverage before any derivative or peak metric; a benchmark's available scored frames do not automatically form dense trajectories. |
| PoseTrack | The original benchmark has manually annotated 2D tracks, 30 consecutive annotated central frames, and additional validation/test labels every fourth frame. Unreliably localized joints can be omitted. The original release has 550 videos with a 292/50/208 split. [Original paper, §3](https://pages.iai.uni-bonn.de/gall_juergen/download/jgall_posetrack_cvpr18.pdf) | Useful independent 2D localization and short-displacement check; weaker for long gait waveforms or hidden joints. These counts describe the original release, not every later PoseTrack version. Do not infer global person identity from a track ID or fill annotation gaps and score them as observed truth. |
| RICH | The original paper reports 22 people, 134 multiview videos split 57/27/50, with seven unseen test subjects. References are fitted SMPL-X bodies; the paper calls them pseudo-ground truth. [Original paper, §§5–6](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/papers/Huang_Capturing_and_Inferring_Dense_Full-Body_Human-Scene_Contact_CVPR_2022_paper.pdf) | Useful later human-scene interaction extension, especially contact/occlusion. Less direct for a one-week walking study and still requires distinguishing fitted references from anatomical observation. |
| GAVD | Official files provide gait labels, person boxes, views, video links and limited gait-event annotations; raw videos are not distributed. Dense reference joint trajectories are not among the documented fields. [Official repository](https://github.com/Rahmyyy/GAVD) | Qualitative transfer and eventual clinically motivated evaluation. Independent dense joint/event annotation would be a new data-collection effort. An abnormality label does not validate a corrected ankle trajectory, and another estimator's output cannot serve as independent truth. |

The local study currently uses normal treadmill motion from one AMASS corpus, one camera, and eight evaluated people. This is a restricted selection and reference protocol, rather than evidence that AMASS as a whole is low quality. The observed coverage failure came from requiring reference visibility throughout each window. Valid hidden synthetic coordinates remained available in that run.

## MoVi: a practical access and preparation route

The [official access tutorial](https://www.biomotionlab.ca/Data/Tutorials/DataverseTutorialBMLmovi.pdf), dated March 2020, requires an institutional Dataverse account and a request for at least one file before access is granted. It supports selecting individual files. Its inventory lists 90 F/PG2 recordings and 89 F/PG1 recordings, plus camera parameters and F-round V3D/AMASS archives. It describes a 5 GB bundled-download limit, roughly ten videos per bundle. **Those are historical inventory and interface details, not a verified current total size.** The current Dataverse landing page returned HTTP 403 through the research tool, so approval latency, total bytes and current file completeness remain unverified.

For an access-approved initial check, obtain the camera archive, the relevant F-round V3D archive, and the F/PG2 video for a few reserved development people. Inspect one walking action and one less repetitive action before requesting a large batch. Downloading selected people and one calibrated view first avoids paying the integration cost of all cameras and sensor modalities. The F/PG2 choice is operational, based on the historical inventory; a camera's viewpoint still needs visual confirmation rather than inference from its number.

The [authors' toolbox](https://github.com/saeed1262/MoVi-Toolbox) supplies camera parameters, import/visualization tutorials and motion-trimming tools. Videos contain a whole capture round; `flags30` and `flags120` identify action boundaries at video and mocap rates. Use those boundaries and the documented synchronization convention rather than equating array indices. Retain the original video frame number, mocap sample/time and camera ID for every scored frame. At 30 Hz, a 0.20-second displacement spans six video frames; recompute this from timestamps if frames are missing. The existing 25 Hz synthetic convention must not be copied unchanged. Either declare timestamp-aware resampling to the training rate, with an explicit interpolation/anti-alias policy, or evaluate at the native rate and report that a 64-frame input now covers a different duration.

Use **Visual3D and projected MoSh++ references as separately named reference conventions** in a sensitivity analysis. Visual3D estimates biomechanical joints from local markers and reports missing joints when markers are unavailable; MoSh++ regresses joints from fitted body surfaces and can infer through marker occlusion, with possible error propagation. The source preprocessing also fills mocap gaps of up to five frames using a cubic polynomial. [MoVi paper, §§2.4–3.2](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0253157) Neither convention is a manual COCO keypoint annotation. A disagreement between them is a reference-quality diagnostic, not automatically a failure by the restoration model. Retain available gap/preprocessing indicators when assessing fine motion or comparing against smoothing.

The paper's open-access publication license does not determine the data license. MoVi's [data site](https://www.biomotionlab.ca/movi/) and [toolbox license notes](https://github.com/saeed1262/MoVi-Toolbox) specify noncommercial research use and require AMASS terms for its AMASS-derived portion. AMASS separately restricts redistribution. [AMASS license](https://amass.is.tue.mpg.de/license.html) Keep review videos local unless the relevant data terms permit redistribution; publish code, provenance and allowable identifiers instead of presuming permission to redistribute videos.

## Prevent overlap before counting independent people

AMASS explicitly includes BMLmovi; its news page records the synchronized MoVi release. [AMASS news](https://amass.is.tue.mpg.de/news.html) Reserving real MoVi videos while training on their AMASS motion would leak the evaluation motion. Exclude the entire BMLmovi collection from the restoration training and all learned priors used in the claimed synthetic-only experiment.

**No verified public identity crosswalk was found between MoVi and BioMotionLab_NTroje/BMLrub.** Excluding BMLmovi alone does not establish that nobody appeared in another BioMotionLab collection. The strongest conservative design uses a fresh synthetic training roster excluding all potentially overlapping BioMotionLab collections, while checking the remaining source provenance. CMU, KIT and ACCAD are candidate origins, not a guarantee of person disjointness merely from their folder names. If the identity issue cannot be resolved, describe the external test as a dataset/recording holdout and retain the person-overlap limitation explicitly.

Create one manifest with source corpus, original participant identifier, canonical person, original recording, derived-window start/end, split and any known duplicate/alias. Split before rendering, window extraction or augmentation. Hash original motion files and compare normalized motion fingerprints to catch exact and near duplicates; review flagged pairs without seeing model rankings. Keep ambiguous same-person records in one group or exclude them under a declared rule.

An illustrative MoVi design reserves 20–30 eligible people for integration and reference checks and up to 60 for the untouched test, if available after those checks. This is a **proposed split**, not an official benchmark protocol or a power calculation. Fix the assignment and intended sample size before comparing methods on the test set. Repeated actions, views, corruption variants and seeds do not increase the independent person count.

Audit each frozen pose estimator's actual checkpoint provenance: config, checkpoint hash, training datasets, fine-tuning datasets, and external pretraining. The names “HRNet,” “RTMPose” and “ViTPose” are insufficient to establish nonexposure. Record unknown exposure as unknown. Use this study's existing COCO-specific artifacts only after verifying their receipts/configs; do not replace them with a mixed-dataset checkpoint without revisiting the external-test claim. Learned motion priors require the same audit; an official AMASS preparation example includes HumanEva and TotalCapture in its data lists. [AMASS authors' preparation code](https://github.com/nghorbani/amass/blob/master/src/amass/data/prepare_data.py)

## Reference and image checks before a large run

The following are proposed acceptance checks, chosen to detect failures that matter for this experiment rather than to remove difficult model examples.

1. **Verify identity and time.** Reject duplicate split assignments, unexplained frame-count changes, nonmonotonic timestamps and mismatched action boundaries. Inspect synchronization over a complete motion, including quick changes in direction; a correct first frame is insufficient. Keep dropped-frame and reference-gap records.
2. **Verify projection and anatomy.** Overlay reference joints on original full frames and on the exact resized crop given to each estimator. Label left and right joints and display the joint names. Test camera rotation/translation, distortion, image orientation, units, and crop-to-image inversion with numerical round trips. Freeze a mapping for the shared joints before scoring; do not learn a test-specific mapping to improve reported error.
3. **Separate three kinds of missingness.** Store reference validity, image visibility and model-output availability separately. A valid synthetic or marker-derived joint can be hidden in RGB; a visible limb can lack valid mocap; a detector can fail despite valid reference. Use the same reference-derived scoring masks for all methods, and report model failures rather than silently dropping them.
4. **Make reference quality visible.** For each candidate person/view, play at least two metadata-selected short clips with reference overlays, plus every automatically flagged segment. Include walking and a motion with reversals or stops. Reviewers should not see method rankings during data acceptance. Record accept/reject/uncertain, the reason, reviewer and timestamp. A second reviewer adjudicates questionable references and a prespecified random sample of accepted clips. Systematic errors trigger correction and a versioned manifest, not ad hoc exclusions.
5. **Distinguish real detection from privileged crops.** The current synthetic pipeline uses renderer foreground boxes. A real-video result using reference-derived person boxes tests restoration conditional on accurate localization. For a practical pipeline claim, additionally run one frozen detector/tracker and evaluate its complete attempted population, including missed detections and identity switches. Keep an oracle-box diagnostic separate so that crop failures and restoration failures can be understood.
6. **Inspect the measured signal.** Show reference and predicted coordinate traces, displacement, ankle separation, missingness, scale and peak matches with one shared cursor tied to video. Preserve a fixed physical-time axis and the same scale across methods. Check reference amplitude and noise before using ratios or peak timing; do not call ankle-separation maxima heel strikes without an independently validated event definition.

A review page should contain the original video, the actual estimator crop, and toggles for reference/input/restored poses, with a split/person/recording identifier always visible. The user should be able to choose examples by metadata or a seeded random list, filter automatic quality flags, and export the review decision. Save representative clean/corrupted videos and reference arrays alongside their hashes. Three still frames from the first few windows cannot reveal timing drift or establish coverage across the population.

## A one-week boundary that protects the result

Start access checks immediately, but set a first-day decision point. If MoVi access and a small verified import are ready, proceed with the reserved MoVi protocol and a bounded set of methods. If approval or reference integration remains blocked, complete a fresh, reviewed AMASS test panel and repeated seeds using the existing pipeline, with a real-video replication only on a dataset already available and verified. State that the main result is synthetic if no external test is finished.

Human3.6M requires academic registration with affiliation/advisor information, so a new application has an uncertain schedule. [Official registration](https://vision.imar.ro/human3.6m/register.php) 3DPW requires acceptance of its [research license](https://virtualhumans.mpi-inf.mpg.de/3DPW/license.html). RICH requires registration and includes subject-specific restrictions. [Registration](https://rich.is.tue.mpg.de/register.php), [license](https://rich.is.tue.mpg.de/license.html) No access approval or download completion for these datasets was verified here.

The strongest feasible paper can combine a controlled synthetic mechanism experiment with one credible real-video replication and a transparent visual audit. More cameras, rendering variants or frames can improve coverage, but only newly reserved people increase the person-level sample size. A well-known dataset supports reproducibility; it does not by itself guarantee statistical significance, reference validity or a benefit from JEPA.
