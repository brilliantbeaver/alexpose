# Visual review workflow

Use this for every conceptual figure in the Latent Laterality study.

## Design constraints

- One figure answers one question. Split a figure whenever it needs two independent safeguards, comparisons, or conclusions.
- Use the surrounding prose for definitions, caveats, metrics, and evidence. A figure shows the relationship only.
- Use one reading direction and no more than three visual stages.
- Keep the title under seven words and the total visible figure text under 30 words.
- Use colour as a role, never as decoration: teal and orange distinguish the two label conventions; gray is neutral structure.
- Do not draw an outer frame, shadow, or a card around a stage unless the container itself has scientific meaning.
- Use arrows only for direction or construction. Lines must never cross; merge converging paths before the next stage.
- Build on a roughly 560-pixel-wide canvas with labels that remain at least 11 pixels high when shown at 375 pixels wide.

## Adversarial review before publishing

1. Hide the caption and ask whether the figure’s one point is still clear.
2. Delete every word, box, arrow, or colour that does not change that point.
3. Render it at normal and narrow widths. Check that text does not touch a line, arrow, or edge, and that an arrow has only one possible destination.
4. Check semantic consistency: a colour, marker, or gap must mean the same thing everywhere it appears.
5. Confirm that the report names the image as conceptual, an implementation check, or an experimental result. Numerical claims stay in the linked output artifacts.

Prefer a small, quiet figure that leaves one idea memorable over a complete diagram that asks the reader to decode several ideas at once.
