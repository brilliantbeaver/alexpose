**Disposition: revise before delivery.** The evidence narrative is substantially sound. The principal blocker is the page limit. One protocol sentence needs correction, and the proposed experiment needs a more explicit target quantity. No new experiments or extensive extra literature survey are required to resolve these issues.

## Must fix

1. **The PDF currently violates the 2–3-page body limit.** The main body occupies five pages, followed by one source/reference page. Both bottom floats spill onto independent float pages: the external JEPA concept is alone on page 2, and the v08 result plot is alone on page 4. The prose sits on pages 1, 3, and 5. The `[b]` placements followed by `\clearpage` at original manuscript lines 32–42 and 53–60 cause the visible failure. Integrate the figures into the actual three-page body, then rebuild and inspect all pages at reading scale. Do not report three pages merely because the prose was planned as three blocks. Keep original figure proportions and readable labels. The “Source and scope” paragraph contains administrative material that could move to the README/notes; the user's extra-page exception is for references, so this should not become a fourth page of substantive discussion.

2. **“There is no untouched confirmation cohort” is not the supported statement** (original line 48). The study reserved a locked confirmation assignment but did not complete its evaluation. v08 §3.3 p.4, Appendix A p.12, and §5 p.9 consistently distinguish this from absence of a cohort. Replace with “No untouched confirmation evaluation was completed,” or equivalent. The surrounding statements about repeatedly inspected development people, person-disjoint fitting, and unknown aliases are appropriate.

3. **Define the proposed per-leg change before asserting its success criteria** (original lines 71–75). The existing result concerns projected 2D knee-angle excursion. The proposed experiment introduces calibrated video, fixed body dimensions, and independent marker-based references, but “each leg's reference-measured change” does not say whether it remains a projected 2D excursion or becomes an anatomically defined 3D knee measure. The future-direction notes correctly recognize this distinction; the short manuscript loses it. One concise phrase can resolve it: specify either changes in each leg's 3D knee-angle excursion under a fixed anatomical convention, or changes in the same projected measure using independently projected marker references. If using 3D, explicitly identify it as the new proposed endpoint rather than silently carrying over the 2D metric. Keep reference-repeatability tolerances and prospective language.

## Recommended improvements within the same length budget

4. **Make ambiguity search operational in one sentence.** “Search plausible alternatives” could mean sampling the existing ensemble, which would not explain the proposed difference from the ensemble-disagreement baseline. Specify that the search seeks trajectories with similar agreement with observed joints but maximally different per-leg change estimates, subject to stated anatomical/temporal constraints. This makes the hypothesis falsifiable and distinguishes measurement-targeted search from generic variation. Do not call the resulting range certified, exhaustive, or physically correct; the existing caution about failed local search is good.

5. **Give the closest uncertainty precedent priority over a broad reconstruction example.** The brief appropriately avoids a first-ever claim, and its notes cover the literature. Still, its future-work sentence cites monocular reconstruction and JEPA gait estimation while the recommended contribution is a reliability diagnostic. Consider citing Pace et al., [*Uncertainty-Aware Mapping from 3D Keypoints to Anatomical Landmarks for Markerless Biomechanics*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26844), in that sentence, potentially replacing the broader OpenCap example. This makes clear that uncertainty-based selection in biomechanics already exists. Retain the narrower question of per-leg change validity, observation-only controls, and additional benefit over simpler rules. A three-page brief does not need the entire precedent catalogue.

6. **Clarify why the large angular penalties appear.** The 720°/180° numbers are correct, but a reader could mistake them for observed motion or clinically meaningful error cutoffs. Add “fixed worst-case failure costs” or “worst-case accounting costs” when introducing them. This is a few words, not an invitation to reproduce the appendix's denominator derivation. Likewise, “pretrained encoders” instead of “encoders” in the 2,000+2,000-update sentence avoids implying that initialized-feature controls received pretraining.
