# Future-direction and external-figure audit

Checked 28 September 2026. This is a bounded editorial/research assessment for the new two-to-three-page brief. It incorporates the previous [research-direction novelty audit](../../ambient-jepa-writeup/reviews/research-direction-novelty-audit.md) and [search record](../../ambient-jepa-writeup/reviews/RESEARCH-SEARCH.md), with fresh checks of the primary sources linked below. No experiment was run. The recommendation is a proposal, and novelty is not established by a search failing to find an exact match.

## Recommended focus

**Preserving independently measured changes in each leg under occlusion, and detecting when the observation supports conflicting interpretations.** This is the strongest continuation of the current evidence. Direct coordinate supervision improved restoration, but the zero-response control remained stronger on pooled asymmetry-response error. Occlusion and anatomical side assignment therefore deserve priority over a larger model or a new generative interface. JEPA should remain a controlled candidate representation, not a presumed solution.

The next study can have one specific technical hypothesis: **searching for alternative observation-consistent lower-limb trajectories will identify misleading per-leg change reports better than visibility thresholds or conventional uncertainty signals, at the same proportion of complete comparisons retained.** The ambiguity search should be small and offline initially. It need not be coupled to a new foundation model, a simulator, or an RL controller.

The minimal convincing experiment is a crossed movement/observation design. Acquire or locate calibrated, synchronized video and an independent marker-based reference for two prespecified walking trials per participant. Use both original video and digitally occluded copies of each trial. A same-trial clear/occluded comparison has no true motion change; a between-trial comparison has the reference-measured change. Evaluate left and right knee excursions separately, alongside complete knee trajectories and wrong-side assignment. Derive 3D anatomical angle conventions with the biomechanics team; the v08 projected 2D measurement cannot silently become a 3D clinical quantity.

Begin with a fixed contemporary reconstruction pipeline and a supervised temporal refiner. Where compatible, compare a matched refiner with JEPA pretraining under identical supervised adaptation and loss-weight selection. Apply visibility, calibrated measurement residual, ensemble-disagreement, and ambiguity-search selection to the same underlying estimates. Keep zero-response and interpolation controls. A matched direct-supervision model is essential because the current study supplies little evidence that JEPA is preferable. [OpenCap Monocular](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24733) is a relevant external reconstruction comparator, subject to compatible data, licensing, and model assumptions.

Search alternatives jointly across trial pairs, initially fixing camera calibration and independently measured body dimensions. Use transparent, permissive anatomical ranges and temporal constraints, without enforcing symmetry or a healthy-gait template. A found alternative is a witness of ambiguity under those assumptions. A failed local search does **not** prove identifiability, physical validity, or safety. Do not describe the explored range as a certified uncertainty bound.

Use separate participants for fitting, calibration, and locked final evaluation. Success requires preservation of reference-measured per-leg change, followed by a reduction in false changes, missed changes, and wrong-side reports at matched whole-pair retention. Evaluate the actual measurement over its complete support; removing low-confidence peak-flexion frames would change the outcome. Estimate uncertainty by resampling people, and audit retention and errors in occluded and atypical strata. Choose numerical margins using pilot repeatability and reference uncertainty before final testing, not a convenient invented clinical threshold.

The principal risk is that plausible anatomical priors suppress the unusual motion the system is intended to measure, while a local optimizer misses valid alternatives. A second risk is that simple visibility rules perform equally well; that would remove the case for the search cost. An informative initial result could therefore be a diagnostic benchmark and failure analysis rather than a more accurate estimator.

For Landay's lab, this creates a concrete basis for studying how an ambient system reports a supported change, an inconclusive comparison, or a request for another observation without burdening residents unnecessarily. For Delp's lab, it tests whether restoration preserves a reference-defined biomechanical contrast, with measurement conventions and model uncertainty explicit. Neither contribution should be described as demonstrated clinical benefit or fall-risk prediction.

For the short manuscript, keep this recommendation to roughly 150–220 words and avoid trying to fit the entire pipeline. The essential elements are the per-leg change question, crossed test, independent reference, direct-supervision baseline, matched-retention criterion, prior-bias risk, and the two labs' respective value. The ambiguity search is the specific method to test; it should not displace the basic measurement-preservation question.

## Comparison of the seven branches

The original discussion separates learning physical principles from evaluating physical plausibility, giving seven branches. Their priority here is relative to this study's immediate evidence.

| Branch | Scientific value and required evidence | Feasibility and main risk | Recommendation for the next study |
|---|---|---|---|
| Learning physical principles in a vision model | Potentially valuable for movement under changed support, contact, or loading. A convincing test needs true future-state or intervention outcomes, independent references, and a causal input pipeline. | Much broader than full-window restoration. Robotic manipulation success does not establish human balance understanding; [V-JEPA 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09985) also distinguishes video pretraining from subsequent action-conditioned robot modeling. | Defer future-state modeling until movement measurement is reliable. Do not rename the present model a world model. |
| Measuring physical plausibility | Explicit segment consistency, anatomical conventions, floor/contact assumptions, and reference agreement can reveal errors. Dynamic claims require loads and inertial parameters. | Limited kinematic checks are feasible now; a realistic-looking or anatomically admissible trajectory may still be the wrong movement. | Include transparent constraints and audit whether they exclude real atypical motion; use them to investigate ambiguity rather than certify a reconstruction. |
| S-JEPA discrimination and generation | A frozen or adapted discriminative readout is a natural representation evaluation. Generation would require a separate output model and tests of edit fidelity, diversity, anatomy, and contact. | [S-JEPA](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2024/papers_ECCV/papers/04755.pdf) is demonstrated for skeletal action recognition. [SMPL](https://smpl.is.tue.mpg.de/) parameterizes a body surface using pose and shape; its joints are derived outputs, not a complete parameterization given arbitrary keypoints. | Compare representations for the measurement task first. A generator has not earned its complexity from v08 results. |
| Robotics/OpenSim constraints or loss signals | Useful for testing whether biomechanical assumptions improve measurements; matched ablations would isolate their effect. | [OpenSim inverse dynamics](https://opensimconfluence.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OpenSim/pages/53090063) needs inertial parameters and all relevant measured or modeled external forces for accurate torques. Outputs from uncertain pose and assumed loads are not new ground truth. [OpenCap Monocular](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24733) already combines monocular motion refinement and biomechanical modeling. | Start with an offline assumption audit and limited kinematics. Add dynamics-based losses only with independent reference supervision and force/contact checks. |
| Depth combined with motion features | An extra view or calibrated depth can test whether additional evidence resolves a particular ambiguous comparison. This has clear ambient-sensing relevance. | A normalized 2D embedding cannot uniquely supply metric scale or depth. [Depth Anything V2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09414) has separately fine-tuned metric variants. [WHAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.07531) combines 2D motion, image features, estimated camera motion, and contact-aware refinement. | High-priority enabling work, initially with calibrated cameras. Judge added depth by independent 3D change preservation, not smoother output or agreement between related models. |
| Qwen tool agent trained with RL | Eventually useful for deciding whether another tool or observation improves a measurement enough to justify cost. Reward could reflect reference-measured errors and participant effort. | A [Qwen2.5-VL](https://qwenlm.github.io/blog/qwen2.5-vl/) controller has documented visual-agent and structured-output capabilities; the current [Qwen3-VL repository](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-VL) is a later candidate to benchmark. These do not demonstrate biomechanical judgment. [SAM 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00714) segments images/video; masks do not establish anatomical identity, metric depth, or physical contact. | Defer RL until a fixed pipeline and rule-based acquisition baseline show useful tool choices and an independently scored reward. Do not let abstaining on every difficult case optimize the reward. |
| Coordinated human–object generation | Relevant to chairs, walkers, and assistance; needs synchronized body/object geometry, contacts, and meaningful interaction targets. | [CHOIS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03913) already jointly generates human/object motion with geometry and contact conditioning, and [InterDiff](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.16905) uses physics-informed interaction correction. [PhysDiff](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.02500) is also precedent for physics-guided human generation. | A valuable later branch with a bounded chair interaction; it is not the clearest immediate response to the current measurement failures. |

## Novelty boundaries that must survive compression

The proposal should not claim the first gait-change estimator, the first JEPA gait model, or the first uncertainty-aware biomechanics method. [Stenum et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pdig.0000467) already compare within-person speed-related gait changes with motion-capture references. [Pace et al. (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26844) study uncertainty-aware keypoint-to-anatomical-landmark mapping and selective retention. These are direct reasons to use stringent baselines.

[SynthGait-19K (September 2026 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.08108v1) is especially close: GaitXFormer uses V-JEPA2 initialization, Appendix F.4 evaluates controlled occlusion, and Appendix E.2 tests within-patient directional changes anchored to clinical severity. The narrower opportunity is a measurement-specific ambiguity diagnostic, evaluated on independently measured per-leg changes and observation-only nulls together. Its benefit over simpler uncertainty methods is unknown. The earlier audit also identifies biomechanical posterior reconstruction and task-level uncertainty sets as precedents; the brief need not recite all of them, but should avoid broad originality claims.

## Recommended external concept illustration

**Use Figure 2 from Bardes et al., *Revisiting Feature Prediction for Learning Visual Representations from Video* (2024), arXiv:2404.08471v1.** It is a generic JEPA concept diagram showing two encoders, a predictor conditioned on a variable, and a comparison between predicted and target representations. It contains no empirical gait result or patient imagery.

The original diagram's aspect ratio is approximately 1.88:1. It should therefore occupy about 2.6 × 1.38 inches, possibly beside its explanatory caption. It cannot fill a 6 × 1 inch box without distortion; forcing one-inch height would shrink already small mathematical subscripts. Figure 3 from the same paper has a wider ratio, but its token/shape labels are much denser and it needs approximately 6 × 1.8 inches. I recommend Figure 2 as the more economical concept explanation. Render the final embedded figure at its actual printed size before accepting the layout.

Attribution should include the original authors/year, **original Figure 2**, a linked CC BY 4.0 credit, and the fact that it is reproduced unchanged. The caption must explicitly call this an external conceptual illustration and explain that v08 instead uses skeleton sequences and clean synthetic reference targets. Define the diagram's input, feature, and conditioning symbols briefly; do not imply that its original video architecture is the exact model used by v08. The original figure license permits reuse with attribution, while preserving author credit and avoiding an implication of endorsement.

I also inspected two alternatives. The I-JEPA arXiv paper (2301.08243v3) exposes the arXiv non-exclusive distribution license, which does not itself supply a public adaptation license; I did not rely on its repository's code license for figure reuse. S-JEPA's accessible project page and ECVA PDF did not provide an explicit figure-reuse license during this check. V-JEPA 2 Figure 2 has an explicit CC BY 4.0 article license, but the left-panel portrait aspect and embedded frame images make it less suitable here. These alternatives were not needed once the clean licensed generic diagram was found.
