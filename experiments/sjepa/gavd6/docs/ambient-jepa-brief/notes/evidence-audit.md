# Independent evidence audit for the compact research brief

Audited 28 September 2026. The source of truth is `docs/iclr/versions/v08/paper-v08.pdf`, read in full (16 pages, including references and Appendices A–E), together with its TeX source and the completed-study exports. Page numbers below are the printed PDF pages. No models were trained and no manuscript or source evidence was changed.

## Overall finding

The PDF's main numerical claims agree with the original participant-level exports at the reported precision. Independent read-only calculations reproduced all four saved core contrasts, all four saved feature-response contrasts, and all eight saved repair contrasts, including their original interval procedures, to absolute tolerance `1e-10`. The strongest contribution for a short brief is a controlled **measurement-fidelity evaluation** of JEPA-inspired restoration: improved coordinates, a smaller scalar response error, and correct anatomical assignment are separable requirements. The results establish practical failure modes and unresolved representation benefits, not a validated gait world model or clinical system.

## What was trained and measured

- The student receives noisy estimated **2D poses**; a teacher receives privileged clean projected references during training. A readout maps student features to restored coordinates. This is paired synthetic supervision inspired by JEPA, not a purely label-free reproduction of S-JEPA. S-JEPA uses its target encoder downstream; this adaptation deploys the student (PDF §2 p.2).
- The task restores each **complete observed window independently**. Pairing defines training losses and the response calculation; it does not make the model a future-state predictor (PDF §1 p.2 and Figure 1 p.3).
- With per-leg projected knee-angle excursion `q = P95(theta) − P5(theta)`, asymmetry is `A = q_R − q_L` and response is `Delta A = A_b − A_a` (PDF Equation 1 p.2). This scalar can hide which leg changed and can hide trajectory/timing errors. The nominal 3D edit is not the projected 2D response.
- Original response scoring **excludes explicit `no_change` pairs**: `src/gavd6_sjepa/research_directions/gait_fidelity/evaluation.py:329` filters `movement_state.ne('no_change')`. Therefore the 5.81° zero-response baseline must not be explained as resulting from including many zero-edit trials. Small projected responses, inaccurate inferred changes, and failures affect the comparison even among the scored nonzero edits.
- Frozen encoders use 2,000 pretraining plus 2,000 readout updates; direct fitting jointly updates encoder/readout for 4,000 updates. Equal update totals do not equalize encoder adaptation or coordinate-supervised exposure (Appendix B p.13).
- “Change” adds **both** scalar-response and minimum-segment geometry penalties to coordinate supervision. Its comparison with coordinate-only training cannot identify which added penalty caused deterioration. Low-scalar repair changes the scalar weight to 0.1; dense repair matches leg- and timestamp-specific angular changes, with its initial gradient calibrated to low scalar (PDF §3.2 p.4; Appendix B p.13).
- Means aggregate conditions → windows → motions → people → seeds. Nonheld/held weights are 2:1 for responses and 4:1 for endpoint metrics. Failures stay in reference-eligible denominators at **720° response** and **180° waveform** costs. These are bounded worst-case accounting costs, not typical movement errors or clinical thresholds (PDF pp.5,12,15).

## Scope and novelty wording

The strongest defensible novelty is the study's **controlled, paired test of whether JEPA-inspired motion features retain side-sensitive gait measurements through pose restoration**, using an original/edited motion contrast, separate waveform and naming checks, and simple task baselines. This distinguishes measurement fidelity from feature predictability or action-recognition accuracy. The paper's comparison with S-JEPA is an adaptation analysis, not a reproduced S-JEPA benchmark or proof of superiority over published pose refiners, none of which was fitted here (§2 p.2).

For HCI and biomechanics, the evidence supports the need to validate the movement information on which a resident-facing report or balance assessment would depend. It does not establish that an older adult's gait changed, that asymmetry indicates fall risk, that reconstructed motion obeys physical dynamics, or that a clinical intervention is indicated. Sequoias and laboratory balance participants are prospective beneficiaries, not the tested cohort. Repeated development inspection and the absence of untouched confirmation limit any ranking. No equivalence or noninferiority margin was set, so an interval spanning zero cannot establish preserved accuracy.

The artifact audit found no unresolved numerical contradiction in the recommended claims. Remaining limitations are provenance and interpretation: final development motion count absent; training subset counts recoverable only from diagnostics; compact exports lack per-pair true magnitudes, complete reconstructed poses, and checkpoints needed to reproduce training. The brief should not manufacture individual pose examples, response-magnitude bins, clinical affected-side labels, or a universal “first-ever” novelty claim from these materials.
