# Plain-language evidence checklist for v08

This is a read-only audit of the original exports, calibration receipt, and relevant notebooks. No manuscript, data, analysis outputs, or fitted models were changed. The numbers below describe the same 14 development people and three fitted seeds. Rewriting them more simply must not turn this reused development sample into independent confirmation.

**Define the task before reporting its scores.** The model restores a complete observed sequence of joint positions. For each leg, knee excursion measures the range of knee angles during that sequence. The “response” is the change in right-minus-left excursion between an original movement and its synthetically edited counterpart. It is not the prediction of a future frame. “Waveform error” means frame-by-frame knee-angle error. The feature extractor produces internal vectors; the readout converts those vectors back into joint coordinates.

1. **Zero response is a demanding benchmark for this particular measurement.** It always predicts that the paired response is zero; it does not set joint coordinates to zero or reconstruct a motionless person. Its pooled error is **5.810830°**, below all **16 learned variants** in the response export. Direct coordinate training scores **7.544155°**, giving zero prediction a paired advantage of **1.733325° [0.528417, 3.149885]**. I independently reproduced this interval from the original person-by-seed rows using 2,000 paired crossed-bootstrap draws, seed 731. A safe explanation is: “Direct coordinate training improved noisy joint estimates, but its average movement-change error remained larger than simply predicting no change.” Retain the original failure scoring and pooled scope. Direct beats zero under clear observations, so avoid “zero prediction is always better” or “the models learned no movement information.”

   Source: `outputs/iclr/jepa-response/evaluation/per-person.csv`, fields `response_error` and `zero_response_error`. The unchanged/direct comparison is also in `outputs/iclr/walking-core/evaluation/per-person.csv`. Notebook `gait_fidelity/07_completed_study_walkthrough.ipynb`, cells 21–24, explains the benchmark, failure accounting, and paired uncertainty.

2. **Predicting feature differences has no established advantage over predicting feature endpoints.** With the original change-trained readout, delta and endpoint score **9.988035°** and **10.361088°**. Delta's mean advantage is **0.373054° [−1.110272, 1.760337]**. A safe explanation is: “The small average difference is too uncertain to show which of these two procedures is better.” With coordinate-only readouts, their means reverse: delta **10.944775°**, endpoint **10.232657°**. This reversal concerns point estimates; the readout-interaction interval also crosses zero, **1.085171° [−0.651744, 2.847543]**. Avoid “delta wins,” “endpoint wins,” “the methods are equivalent,” or “JEPA does not work.”

   Sources: the response `per-person.csv`, `comparisons.json`, and `readout-control-comparisons.json`. Notebook `gait_fidelity/experiments/F_jepa_response.ipynb`, cells 5–7 and 19–22, explains the objectives and interpretation. The two variants retain a common base feature loss; their auxiliary difference is not a new downstream outcome metric.

3. **The same coefficient did not give the two auxiliary losses equal initial influence.** The shared coefficient is **0.012646811176583523**. At initialization, the weighted endpoint auxiliary's gradient RMS is **10%** of the base objective's, while delta's is **0.001303714%**. A safe explanation is: “Before training, the added endpoint objective supplied a much larger parameter-gradient signal than the added difference objective, despite using the same coefficient.” These measurements use 32 training batches and precede clipping. They do not show the relative influence throughout training or explain the result causally. The six new feature pretrains clip their combined gradients on **11,999/12,000** updates, which is a separate recorded fact. Avoid “delta was never trained,” “the gradient ratio is the ratio of actual updates,” or “clipping caused the outcome.”

   Sources: `outputs/iclr/jepa-response/diagnostics/loss-calibration.json` and `ledger.json`. Notebook F, cells 13–17, explicitly distinguishes the shared coefficient, initial gradient measurement, and subsequent combined-gradient clipping.

4. **The original change-training package worsened knee-angle trajectories across all eight families.** Compared with coordinate-only training, every family has a larger mean waveform error; the increases range from **4.387841° to 7.207555°**. This is eight families with two output-training objectives, not 16 independent replications. A safe explanation is: “Adding the original change-training package made the restored knee-angle trajectories less accurate in every tested family.” The package adds both the scalar response loss and a short-segment geometry penalty. Avoid attributing the whole effect to the scalar term alone or generalizing to all change supervision.

   Source: response `per-person.csv`, pairing each family's `-base` and `-paired_change` rows. Notebook 07, cells 14–15, and notebook G, cells 9 and 15, state which terms change. The completed core contains five families; the response export adds three, giving the eight-family statement.

5. **Reducing scalar weight improves waveform accuracy, without proving better response accuracy.** The repair keeps the two pretrained feature extractors frozen and changes their readout training. On ViTPose, delta waveform error falls from **23.170916°** to **19.473519°** when scalar weight falls from 1 to 0.1: a gain of **3.697397° [2.584569, 4.810225]**. Endpoint waveform error falls from **23.331389°** to **19.635038°**, a gain of **3.696352° [2.161066, 5.231637]**. These are exploratory paired-person intervals after averaging seeds, conditional on the three fits. Say “lowered frame-by-frame knee-angle error,” rather than broadly “improved the models.” Delta's response-error mean rises from **11.927949° to 13.107513°** in this comparison, with uncertain change.

   Dense supervision improves delta waveform error only another **0.278856° [−0.194914, 0.752625]** over low scalar—the unresolved repair primary. The “93% recovered” number is a descriptive ratio of mean waveform gains, not an explanation of how the improvement arose. Sources: `outputs/iclr/readout-repair/development/evaluation/per-person-by-extractor.csv` and `comparisons.json`. Notebook `gait_fidelity/experiments/G_readout_repair.ipynb`, cells 9–19, derives the fixed terms, calibration, and primary comparison; notebook 07, cells 18–24, connects them to saved results.

**Keep the notebook evidence boundaries explicit.** Gait-fidelity notebook 07 reads the completed `outputs/iclr` runs; F and G combine small constructed calculations with optional saved-receipt inspection. Their teaching arrays, illustrative traces, and scratch updates are not additional experimental results. The retained tutorial receipt reports targeted checks and a results walkthrough, while full fixture execution was blocked by disk exhaustion and remote validation was not completed (`docs/studies/gait-fidelity/records/tutorial-refresh-20260925.json`). Do not simplify this to “all notebooks were run successfully.”

The `future_innovation` notebooks concern a **separate experiment**: past RGB/skeleton inputs from 32 frames predict contextual future RGB-teacher features, evaluated using R² and recording-source folds. See `00_question_and_worked_example.ipynb`, `02_teacher_features_and_validity.ipynb`, `03_matched_predictors_and_controls.ipynb`, and `04_results_and_next_decision.ipynb`. Their default teaching mode uses generated examples; inspect mode reads that other study's saved artifacts. They can inform clear explanation of controls and evidence status, but they are not sources for v08's response errors, person counts, 128-frame/12-joint tensors, or primary intervals. Do not import their future-target task, RGB baseline, decision thresholds, source-video units, or deterministic-fit protocol into this paper.

**A faithful simple conclusion:** “In this controlled restoration study, a lower movement-change error did not guarantee more accurate knee-angle trajectories or anatomical naming. The choice of readout supervision mattered substantially, while the added benefit of the tested feature-difference objective remained uncertain.” Keep clinical usefulness, natural-video transfer, future-state prediction, and general claims about world models outside the completed findings.

The two new abstract summaries also match the implementation. In `src/gavd6_sjepa/research_directions/gait_fidelity/measurements.py`, `measurement_loss` adds the squared normalized asymmetry-change error and a short-segment term of the form `relu((min_segment_px - length) / min_segment_px)^2`, averaged on fixed reference support. The executed `outputs/iclr/{walking-core,jepa-response,readout-repair}/config.json` files all set `min_segment_px` to **2.0**. “Penalties for asymmetry-change errors and very short predicted limb segments” therefore describes the actual compound objective; it makes no claim of broader anatomical regularization. `repair_objectives.py` separates scalar, dense, and geometry terms, and the follow-up retains coordinate and geometry supervision while reducing the scalar coefficient from 1 to 0.1. “Giving less weight to the change penalty reduces trajectory error” is accurate within that stated follow-up scope and does not assert improved response accuracy.

The zero-response comparison remains a pooled comparison with fixed failure penalties, and the main text preserves the clear-observation counterexample and the absence of a reconstructed zero-baseline trajectory. The rounded means, intervals, uncertain delta-versus-endpoint ranking, independently evolving teachers, and lack of independent confirmation retain their meaning. The final clarification that frozen weights are held fixed, the specification of summed Euclidean assignment distances, and the reference to the dense angular term's gradients are also faithful. **No unresolved material semantic misstatement was found in this revised main text.** No manuscript, figure, data, or fitted-model files were changed by this review.
