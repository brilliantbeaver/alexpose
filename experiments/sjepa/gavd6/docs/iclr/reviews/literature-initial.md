# Literature, contribution scope, and submission constraints

Independent scope and literature review, 25 September 2026. The manuscript title is fixed: **Evaluating Feature Prediction for 2D Pose Trajectory Restoration with Paired Synthetic Supervision**. This review consulted `docs/studies/gait-fidelity/full-writeup.md`, `research-overview.md`, `methods/evaluation.md`, and the earlier manuscript draft; it checks the interpretation of their evidence rather than independently recomputing numerical results. The evidence and statistics reviewer is responsible for the latter.

## Defensible contribution

The useful scientific question is whether predicting features of a reference trajectory adds to restoration of a signed, leg-identified movement response when coordinate approaches receive the same synthetic reference information. Its motivation is specific: methods optimized for useful action representations need not retain the information a quantitative movement measurement requires. That is a task-dependent question, not an implication that JEPA inherently removes clinically relevant variation.

The narrow contribution is a controlled **evaluation** of predictive targets and readout objectives under paired synthetic interventions, including coordinate and measurement outcomes, initialized and shuffled-reference controls, and explicit treatment of failed measurements. The completed experiments diagnose a scalar-supervision/trajectory tradeoff and show that the apparent feature-prediction comparison depends on the downstream objective and scoring rule. They leave the hypothesized incremental feature-prediction benefit unresolved. The abstract should report at least the principal uncertain feature contrast, the trajectory tradeoff, the loss-weight control, and the zero-response result. Reporting only improvement over noisy input would hide the decisive measurement baseline.

A concise positioning paragraph could read:

> We study whether feature prediction improves restoration of a defined movement response when paired synthetic trajectories provide reference supervision. Existing masked skeleton methods motivate predictive features, while motion-capture pretraining and temporal refinement supply established alternatives. Our contribution is an evaluation that follows those features through a coordinate readout to a signed bilateral measurement, using paired movement edits, observation perturbations, and failure-aware scoring. Under the tested schedule, the readout objective has a consistent trajectory cost, whereas the primary comparisons do not establish an incremental benefit from the proposed feature or dense-response supervision.

This paragraph deliberately says neither that the objective effect is universal nor that the architectures are equivalent. An interval spanning zero fails to establish superiority; it does not establish no effect or equivalence.

## Narrative and figure guidance

The paper should proceed from the measurement requirement to the controlled comparison, then the results and their limits. A reader should understand the scientific object before encountering the training ledger. The three studies have distinct purposes, but repeating their procedural histories through the abstract, introduction, methods, and discussion will make the argument harder to follow. One table can state their models, readouts, primary outcomes, estimator population, and independent sample.

The opening diagram should answer: *What must remain correct when the observation changes, and what must change when the movement changes?* A small paired example can define a right-minus-left excursion response and contrast physical side exchange with erroneous input naming. The architecture figure should answer: *Which targets are available only during training?* The empirical plots should answer: *Does the feature comparison beat its control, does scalar supervision damage trajectories, and how much repair is explained by lower weight?* Separate panels or explicit labels are necessary when pooled estimators and ViTPose-only results appear together. Failure decomposition must use the same population weighting as total scores, and the zero-response benchmark must not be given a fabricated waveform score.

Captions should give units, lower/higher-is-better direction, sample size, aggregation, exact uncertainty, and whether a plot is descriptive. Sign-based arrows cannot substitute for a definition of anatomical side. Reuse a small vocabulary and an accessible palette; avoid turning every paragraph into a polished aphorism or a contrast of “not X, but Y.”

## ICLR 2027 requirements checked on 25 September 2026

The [official author guidelines](https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2027/AuthorGuidelines) require anonymous main and supplementary material and at most nine main-text pages at submission. References and appendices are excluded; appendices follow the bibliography and may be ignored by reviewers. Use the [official 2027 LaTeX files](https://media.iclr.cc/Conferences/ICLR2027/iclr-2027-style-files.zip). A mandatory AI-use statement is excluded from the limit. Optional ethics and reproducibility statements are also excluded; the ethics statement may occupy at most one page. Code and links must preserve anonymity.

The [call for papers](https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2027/CallForPapers) gives 18 September 2026 AoE for a genuine abstract and 25 September 2026 at 23:59 AoE for the full paper. The abstract deadline has passed on this review date; this writing task does not verify whether a qualifying abstract was submitted. The guidelines fix the author set at the abstract deadline. Formatting a paper does not establish submission eligibility or completion, and no external submission is authorized by this task.

The [author AI policy](https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2027/AIPolicyForAuthors) requires disclosure in the manuscript and submission form. This workflow includes interpretation of results, feedback on methodology/hypotheses, and manuscript/figure/code assistance, so a vague “grammar assistance” disclosure would be inaccurate. State the known tasks. Do not assert that human authors checked every claim, approved the artifacts, or accept responsibility until they actually do so. The final manuscript can distinguish completed AI-assisted artifact checks from the human verification still required before submission.

These rules are submission constraints, not evidence of scientific merit. A nine-page anonymous rendering with no unsupported statement may still have a modest contribution and incomplete external validation. Scores must retain those limitations rather than rise automatically with version number.
