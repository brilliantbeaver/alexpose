# Independent novelty audit: preserving movement change under uncertain observation

Reviewed 28 September 2026. Scope: the proposed continuation of v08 and PLAN.md, with an additional review of measurement-specific ambiguity search. This is an adversarial assessment of the direction, not an endorsement of an implemented method. The existing overview was not edited.

## Verdict

**The problem is consequential, but the broad proposed novelty is already substantially occupied.** Published work measures within-person gait changes against independent motion capture; newer methods reconstruct atypical gait, quantify biomechanical uncertainty, and evaluate asymmetry in clinical populations. “JEPA + 3D + physics + uncertainty” is not a defensible contribution statement.

A stronger, narrower continuation is to test **whether a side-specific change remains supported when both the movement and the observation conditions vary**, and to expose alternative admissible reconstructions that would change the interpretation. The possible contribution is the combination of a demanding measurement protocol and an effective method for detecting unsupported change reports. Its novelty and significance depend on comparison with existing reconstruction, uncertainty, and simple rejection methods. Do not promise that this combination is unprecedented or already highly novel.

## Closest prior work and what it displaces

The search covered primary journal/conference papers and preprints through the review date, using query families for pathological gait reconstruction, occlusion recovery, asymmetry, within-person change, selective prediction, conformal human reconstruction, biomechanical confidence intervals, and inverse-problem ambiguity. Searches included backward leads from the closest papers. This was a targeted audit, not a systematic review or exhaustive absence search. Preprints below are explicitly identified.

| Prior work | Direct overlap and consequence |
|---|---|
| [Mehraban et al., SynthGait-19K, September 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.08108v1) | Uses controlled camera/appearance variation of the same motion; its GaitXFormer is initialized from V-JEPA2. Appendix F.4 tests persistent/transient occlusion, and §6.7/E.2 evaluates within-patient directional change against clinical-score expectations. Better body reconstruction does not necessarily improve gait estimates. Thus JEPA-based gait regression, synthetic nuisance tests and occlusion robustness are already occupied. The clinical anchors are not independent per-leg biomechanical change references. |
| [Stenum et al., PLOS Digital Health, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pdig.0000467), *Clinical gait analysis using video-based pose estimation: Multiple perspectives, clinical populations, and measuring change* | Measures preferred-to-fast within-participant gait changes in stroke and Parkinson's disease against 3D motion capture, using frontal/sagittal videos. Also evaluates side-specific kinematics/asymmetry. Independent-reference change measurement alone is therefore not novel. |
| [Cotton and Sinz, EMBC 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13188178/), *Biomechanical Reconstruction with Confidence Intervals from Multiview Markerless Motion Capture* | Fits distributions over biomechanical trajectories, propagates observation uncertainty, and illustrates asymmetric stroke gait and occlusion. Crucially, it explicitly lacks ground-truth poses; calibration uses detected keypoints, including highly confident detections as pseudo-ground truth. A useful distinction is independent calibration of a paired biomechanical measurement, not merely uncertainty over reconstructed joints. |
| [Horsak et al., Journal of Biomechanics, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbiomech.2025.112986), *Validity and reliability of monocular 3D markerless gait analysis in simulated pathological gait: A comparative study with OpenCap* | CameraHMR/body-model/OpenSim reconstruction is evaluated against marker-based measurements in instructed pathological gait; a separate repeated-session dataset addresses reliability. These are healthy volunteers imitating gait patterns, not proof of performance in clinical populations. The tool chain and basic validation design already exist. |
| [Shin et al., DeepGaitLab, July 2026 preprint](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13419581/) | Synthetic-data-trained multiview reconstruction is evaluated across 80 participants, including stroke and mild cognitive impairment. Split-belt testing evaluates marker-referenced step-length asymmetry and subtracts individual baseline asymmetry. This is a particularly close threat to “preserving within-person asymmetry.” It should inform the baseline/data search, not be omitted because it is recent. |
| [RecovGait, Sensors, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12656669/) | Restores missing Parkinsonian gait keypoints using gated initialization and unscented tracking. Its central targets are 2D reconstruction and Parkinson's classification, rather than independent 3D measurement changes. It nevertheless defeats a broad claim to originate occlusion recovery for pathological gait. |
| [Pace et al., March 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26844), *Uncertainty-Aware Mapping from 3D Keypoints to Anatomical Landmarks for Markerless Biomechanics* | Combines heteroscedastic prediction and MC dropout, with frame/joint uncertainty, risk–coverage analysis and outlier detection on AMASS-derived data. Its uncertainty is principally evaluated as an error-ranking signal; absolute uncertainty magnitudes are not interpreted. Adding an uncertainty score or selective filtering to biomechanics is insufficient novelty. |
| [Zhang and Carlone, CUPS, ICML 2025](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/zhang25g.html) | Conformalizes uncertainty for human pose/shape reconstruction, explicitly considering dependence in video data. Conformal prediction for reconstructed human motion is existing work; a generic conformal wrapper is a baseline, not a standalone method contribution. |
| [Gilon et al., OpenCap Monocular, March 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24733) | Refines monocular reconstruction with biomechanical structure and evaluates kinematics/dynamics against laboratory references. A WHAM-to-biomechanics pipeline should be treated as a contemporary comparator rather than the innovation. |
| [Wen et al., ECCV 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18527), *Task-Driven Uncertainty Quantification in Inverse Problems via Conformal Prediction* | Calibrates uncertainty directly for downstream task outputs following inverse reconstruction, including adaptive intervals and acquisition until uncertainty is acceptable. Thus “calibrate the gait measurement rather than pose error” is a valuable application-level choice, but not a new general uncertainty principle. |
| [Shaikewitz, Georgiou and Carlone, SLUE, IEEE Transactions on Robotics, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1109/TRO.2026.3717258), *Uncertainty Quantification for Visual Object Pose Estimation: S-Lemma Ellipsoidal Bounds* ([author manuscript v2, August 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.21666v2)) | Propagates conformal 2D constraints into nonconvex object-pose sets and computes outer ellipsoidal bounds using optimization relaxations. It concerns rigid object pose rather than articulated gait; nevertheless, uncertainty sets and optimization-based bounds have direct methodological precedent. |

Additional context includes [GloPro, IROS 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.10369), which combines visual/motion information with uncertainty under occlusion, and [Pemasiri et al., March 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02499), which combines 3D reconstruction, anatomical markers and OpenSim for gait measurements. Neither leaves “physics-informed video gait reconstruction” as an open novelty claim.

## Priority objections that should change the proposed study

**P1 — Distinguish a real gap from a conjunction of existing features.** The useful unresolved question is whether current systems can simultaneously avoid inventing changes under altered visibility and retain genuine, anatomically localized changes under the same visibility shift. Evaluate that conjunction explicitly. SynthGait-19K already tests controlled occlusion and within-patient directional associations, so neither should be presented as absent from the literature. A paper showing that a new network has lower pooled joint error, or that uncertainty rises with occlusion, would not answer the proposed question.

**P1 — Do not let abstention change the measurement.** Removing uncertain frames can remove peak knee flexion, the affected leg, or a difficult gait phase. Percentile excursion over the remaining frames is a different target. Initially reject or retain an entire prespecified trial pair, or construct measurement intervals over its complete fixed support. Report retention separately by gait condition, affected side and visibility; equal overall coverage can hide systematic exclusion of the people the system is intended to help.

**P1 — “Physically plausible” needs operational limits.** Joint limits, consistent bone lengths and smoothness establish restricted kinematic admissibility. They do not demonstrate correct muscle forces, balance, contact, assistance or causal dynamics. A learned normal-motion prior can remove pathology while satisfying every chosen regularizer. Bilateral bone-shape constraints and bilateral motion symmetry are also different assumptions. Do not impose the latter; audit even the former when anatomy varies.

**P1 — Reference independence is conditional.** Marker-based motion capture provides external evidence, but joint angles still depend on calibration, soft-tissue artifacts, marker placement and the biomechanical model. Shared body-model assumptions can create agreement without correctness. Quantify repeatability, state angle conventions, and avoid treating another video reconstruction or the same simulator residual as independent truth. Force-based validation is unnecessary for the first kinematic study unless dynamic claims are retained.

**P1 — v08 gives no basis to require a JEPA win.** All sixteen trained variants lost to the zero-response baseline on the pooled response outcome; the three primary gains remained unresolved. Frozen versus adapted encoders and unequal auxiliary-gradient influence complicate attribution. Include a direct temporal reconstruction baseline with matched adaptation and supervision. Treat JEPA as an ablation and possible initialization, not the scientific premise. A negative JEPA result should leave a useful measurement study.

**P2 — Do not turn marginal calibration into a guarantee for every resident or gait.** Person/session dependence, camera shifts, pathology shifts and selecting which estimates to report all matter. Split by person; keep threshold selection separate from final evaluation; report empirical conditional performance for the prespecified difficult strata. A nominal conformal interval does not automatically provide coverage after selection or under new populations.

## The proposed ambiguity-envelope method

The stronger proposal is to search for alternative 3D trajectories that fit the observations and permitted nuisance parameters while minimizing or maximizing each leg's measured change. This is feasible as an **offline adversarial witness search** at small scope. It is not yet a feasible promise of certified whole-body bounds under unknown camera, body shape, contact and dynamics.

The terminology matters. A local optimizer returns feasible examples, so the range it discovers is an **inner explored range of possible measurements**. Finding two admissible reconstructions supporting materially different conclusions demonstrates ambiguity under the stated constraints. Failure to find a competing reconstruction does not establish identifiability or justify a safe-to-report guarantee. Multiple initializations reduce this weakness but do not remove it. Genuine outer bounds would require additional mathematical structure or certified optimization.

There is also no prior-free construction here. The selected anatomical model, angle limits, temporal variation limits, camera intervals and contact assumptions determine what counts as an alternative explanation. Training a motion prior into the feasible set and then using that set to certify that the prior preserved unusual motion would be circular. Start with transparent permissive constraints, and report how conclusions change as each assumption is added.

For a first implementation, fix camera calibration and independently measured subject morphology, use a short observed lower-limb sequence, and optimize both trials jointly for side-specific knee-excursion change. Shared camera/morphology uncertainty should be shared across a pair when the acquisition warrants it; independent optimization can otherwise manufacture changes from incompatible nuisance choices. Conversely, assuming shared nuisance parameters across separately calibrated sessions can produce falsely narrow ranges. Recompute the exact percentile measurement after optimization; a differentiable surrogate must not silently redefine it.

A useful first claim would be: **the search finds observation-consistent counterexamples that reveal when a reconstruction's reported movement change is driven by its prior, and these flags predict independent-reference errors better than simpler uncertainty signals.** Calibration can convert its outputs into an empirical decision rule on a specified distribution, but it cannot transform local search into global bounds. Compare with ensemble/posterior dispersion, calibrated measurement residuals, simple visibility thresholds and unconstrained perturbation search. If these already perform as well, the elaborate search has not earned its cost.

## A narrow, defensible first experiment

1. **Verify data access before promising a cohort.** Seek synchronized videos and independent marker references with actual within-person condition differences. Stenum and DeepGaitLab are promising leads; paper statements about shared data do not establish that the required identifiable RGB videos, calibrations, pairings and reuse rights are available. An available dataset can establish technical feasibility without establishing transport to Sequoias residents. Do not substitute additional synthetic views for additional participants.

2. **Construct a crossed observation/movement test.** For each reference-measured movement state A and B, retain clear input and create specified visibility degradations. Comparing two observations of the same recorded motion supplies an exact observation-only null; comparing A with B supplies a real-change target measured from references. Use masked video as a controlled intervention on visual evidence, not as a complete model of natural occlusion. Hold out natural occluders, camera layouts or recordings for a separate external check when those data exist. Do not call a nonrandom A–B contrast a treatment effect.

3. **Use one primary measurement family.** Start with left and right 3D knee excursions and their trial-to-trial changes, plus the complete angle trajectories. The left/right difference is secondary because errors can cancel. Predefine the measurement convention, trial support, pair selection and what magnitude counts as resolvable relative to reference repeatability. Keep wrong-side reports and outright reconstruction failures visible.

4. **Test the method before expanding the architecture.** Compare a contemporary fixed reconstruction pipeline, a simple supervised temporal refiner, and a matched JEPA-informed refiner. Apply the same rejection/calibration alternatives to each. Add transparent geometry and ambiguity search as separate ablations. Reserve learned dynamics, full OpenSim training losses, generative models and Qwen/RL routing for a demonstrated remaining failure that they can address.

5. **Make success falsifiable.** At matched whole-pair retention, measure false-change reports on the observation-only null, missed real changes, wrong-side reports, change/trajectory error and interval coverage/width. Report retention and errors across the prespecified atypical/occluded strata, with participant-level uncertainty. The proposed method must improve these decisions beyond simple uncertainty and visibility rules without achieving its score by withholding difficult people. Audit reference exclusion by the assumed constraint set, optimizer failures, sensitivity to initialization and computational cost.

The initial stop condition is informative: if current tools preserve measured changes once visibility is controlled, or a simple calibrated rejection rule is equally effective, the expensive reconstruction/ambiguity architecture is not justified. If the new search detects failures but cannot support narrow valid estimates, the contribution is a diagnostic benchmark and warning mechanism. It should be described that way.

## Suggested claim for the appended section

> The next study would test whether video-based reconstruction can preserve a change in each leg's movement while resisting changes caused only by visibility. Existing work already measures gait changes, reconstructs atypical movement and estimates uncertainty. The proposed extension would evaluate these requirements together against independent motion references, and investigate whether searching for alternative observation-consistent movements exposes measurements that depend too strongly on a reconstruction prior. JEPA features and biomechanical constraints would be compared with simpler alternatives rather than assumed to improve the result.

This is a plausible research direction with an identifiable experiment. Its empirical benefits, algorithmic novelty and relevance to residents' decisions remain to be demonstrated. HCI work can later test whether presenting uncertainty or requesting a repeat observation improves interpretation; it should not be used to imply an already validated clinical capability.

## Audit limits

The review used v08/PLAN facts established in the earlier independent reviews and inspected the primary sources linked above. The parent researcher supplied the SynthGait-19K lead; this reviewer independently checked its full text, including the occlusion and within-patient analyses, before adding it. The review did not run reconstruction systems, download/reconcile clinical datasets, verify every available license, prove mathematical bounds, perform a sample-size calculation, or test deployment with residents. Source summaries distinguish published studies from preprints. No claim of exhaustive novelty clearance is made.
