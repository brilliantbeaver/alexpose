# Recent pose-video bridges

Full primary texts and linked release surfaces fetched 2026-09-03.

## VideoMDM: Towards 3D Human Motion Generation From 2D Supervision

**Problem/method.** Learn text-conditioned 3D motion from monocular video with accurate 2D pose but no 3D labels. VideoMDM warms MDM on lifter pseudo-3D for 400K batches of 64, then runs 200K batches with depth-weighted reprojection, 2D velocity, camera-ray representation targets, and low-noise multistep losses; Fit3D transfer takes 20K batches. One RTX 4090 required about 200 h lifter preprocessing and 46 h training, excluding a 1,120 GPU-h sweep (Section 4, Appendix C.6 Table 4). **Numbers.** HumanML3D FID was 0.876 versus lift-only MDM's 1.671 and 3D-supervised MDM's 0.544 (Table 1); Fit3D MPJPE was 111.24 mm versus WHAM's 228.47, but 185.81 with PnP cameras (Table 2). **Bridge/limits/access.** Warm on AMASS, then adapt the prior on GAVD 2D tracks. Camera estimation, occlusion, and lifter dependence remain weak points. MIT code and three checkpoints are public; processing code and processed data are not. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13364v1), [code](https://github.com/Amir-Mann/VideoMDM_release), [weights](https://huggingface.co/AmirMann/VideoMDM).

## Controllable Complex Human Motion Video Generation via Text-to-Skeleton Cascades

**Problem/method.** Text underspecifies acrobatics, while hand-authored poses are costly. An 18-layer autoregressive Transformer uses frozen Stable Diffusion v2 CLIP and 256-bin joint tokens; rank-64 LoRA adapts frozen Wan2.1-I2V-14B with DINO-ALF appearance attention and an eight-layer 3D-CNN pose encoder. Skeleton training used one A100, batch 8, with steps/time unreported; video training used four A100s for 40K steps on 1,800 clips (Sections III-IV). **Numbers.** Skeleton FID was 255.19 versus HumanDreamer's 322.16 (Table I). Video FVD was 471.3 versus VACE's 498.7 using ground-truth poses (Table II); predicted-pose jitter/dropout/time-shift augmentation improved 574.7 to 492.5 (Table VI). **Bridge/limits/access.** Project AMASS to 2D, render identity-conditioned clips, and train for GAVD-like pose noise. It is single-person and blurs fingers/faces in fast rotations. The public repo contains only the Blender generator; model code, checkpoints, and the 2K-video dataset remain “coming soon.” [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.08028v1), [project](https://ashkantaghipour.github.io/kangaroo/), [repo](https://github.com/AshkanTaghipour/complex-motion-video-generation).

## PoseAnything

**Problem/method.** Human-specific controls generalize poorly and appearance drifts. Starting from Wan2.2-TI2V-5B, it VAE-encodes arbitrary skeleton rasters, concatenates pose channels, adds part-matched cross-frame attention, and decouples subject/camera guidance through CFG. Training uses XPose's 50K non-human pairs plus 15K internal human videos: 3K human warm-up and 8K frozen-backbone PTCM steps, batch 32 on eight unspecified GPUs; mixed-stage steps are unreported (Sections 3-5). **Numbers.** FVD was 133.95 versus Animate-X's 139.01 on TikTok and 99.97 versus ATI's 101.44 on the 51-video XPose benchmark (Tables 1-2). At only 2.5% pose-frame injection, FVD remained 99.23 (Appendix Table 5). **Bridge/limits/access.** PTCM can preserve limb identity across AMASS-to-GAVD rendering and sparse/missing poses, while camera CFG separates viewpoint motion. Internal human data and full PTCM training code are absent. Public code, MIT 6B BF16 checkpoint, and MIT XPose data exist. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.13465v1), [code](https://github.com/Ryan-w2024/PoseAnything), [weights](https://huggingface.co/Ryan241005/PoseAnything), [data](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Ryan241005/XPose).

## Unified Dense Prediction of Video Diffusion

**Problem/method.** Video diffusion lacks explicit geometry and aligned dense outputs. UDPDiff fully fine-tunes CogVideoX-5B, duplicating 16-channel input/output weights to 32 channels so RGB and randomized-color Pixelplanes segmentation/depth latents denoise jointly, selected by learned task embeddings. Panda-Dense has about 300K pseudo-labeled Panda-70M clips. Training was 40K steps, batch 1 per GPU, learning rate 1e-5 on eight A100-80Gs; duration is unreported (Section 4.1; Supplement Section 2). **Numbers.** Multi-depth FVD/subject consistency/motion smoothness were 302.55/97.07/99.23 versus CogVideoX's 343.92/94.57/97.67 (Table 2). A 49-frame, 50-step sample took 205.81 s versus 204.46 s on one A100-80G (Table 4). **Bridge/limits/access.** Co-denoise AMASS-derived silhouettes/depth with GAVD RGB, then extend the channel vocabulary to pose. Labels and depth evaluation are teacher-derived; depth accuracy trails Depth Anything V2 (Table 3). The official paper and author page link no code, Panda-Dense download, or checkpoint. [CVF paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/papers/Yang_Unified_Dense_Prediction_of_Video_Diffusion_CVPR_2025_paper.pdf), [supplement](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/supplemental/Yang_Unified_Dense_Prediction_CVPR_2025_supplemental.zip), [author page](https://bio.lehanyang.info/).
