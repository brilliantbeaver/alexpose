Abstract
Predictive movement representations are plausible inputs to future ambient health
1
assistants, but evidence for a representation does not automatically support de-
2
cisions about a person. We argue that source transfer, functional retention, fu-
3
ture prediction, and clinical utility require distinct evaluations. A retrospective
4
audit of a skeleton joint-embedding predictive architecture illustrates why. A lo-
5
cal ﬁve-category GAVD subset contracts from 666 annotated sequences in 103
6
source videos to 639 pose-eligible sequences in 97 videos. In one source-held-
7
out split, a raw-kinematic readout achieves macro-F1 0.441, compared with 0.292
8
for the learned representation and 0.251 for missingness alone. Separately, the
9
same 64 normal-validation clips yield cross-checkpoint embedding cosine 0.889
10
under equal clip weighting but 0.701 under equal source weighting; one upload
11
contributes 60 clips. These observations support an evaluation position, not a clin-
12
ical or architecture-wide performance claim. We specify the additional evidence
13
needed before masked representation prediction can support longitudinal monitor-
14
ing, forecasting, or agentic care.
15
1
Position: evidence must follow the claim
16
A future ambient health assistant might use movement observations to help a clinician review
17
changes in a person’s mobility. Its perception component would need to distinguish changes in
18
movement from changes in camera, visibility, and the model itself. A representation that predicts
19
masked joints or separates dataset annotations has not yet demonstrated that distinction. If a down-
20
stream language model turns such features into a conﬁdent clinical narrative, the uncertainty in the
21
sensing pipeline remains unresolved.
22
Our position is that evaluations of predictive health representations should make four claims sep-
23
arately: transfer across data sources, retention of useful function after model updates, prediction
24
beyond the observed time interval, and utility in a deﬁned clinical workﬂow. Each concerns a dif-
25
ferent target and requires different evidence. When movement features enter a larger health system,
26
their evidence scope should travel with them: the source population, model version, measured task,
27
and unresolved failure modes.
28
Joint-embedding predictive architectures (JEPAs) make this distinction especially relevant. I-JEPA
29
and S-JEPA learn by predicting target representations, rather than reconstructing every input value
30
[2, 1]. This is not by itself a generative clinical system. Video work such as V-JEPA 2 studies
31
additional prediction and action-conditioned planning capabilities [3]; those capabilities cannot be
32
inferred from the name of a training objective. Our connection to generative and agentic health AI
33
is the evidence required of a possible perception component. We do not evaluate a language model,
34
agent, or care intervention.

An auditable, bounded gait case study
43
Population and evaluation unit.
We audit a project-speciﬁc ﬁve-category subset of the Gait Ab-
44
normality Video Dataset (GAVD) [8]. Its annotations are normal, Parkinson’s, stroke, myopathic,
45
and cerebral palsy. These are the dataset’s movement/condition annotations; this project does not
46
independently verify diagnoses. The local inventory, not the full GAVD dataset, contains 666 se-
47
quences from 103 source videos. A September 4, 2026 metadata snapshot retains 657/100 (se-
48
quences/videos); the measured decoding audit retains 655/98; and a ﬁxed 0.50 coverage gate over
49
12 selected landmarks retains 639/97. Metadata availability, successful decoding, and pose eligibil-
50
ity are separate conditions. Appendix A reports category counts and acquisition limitations.
51
A local registry deﬁnes ﬁve source-grouped outer folds, but only fold 0 and model seed 42 have the
52
current traced training and evaluation artifacts. Source roles are assigned at the metadata-public gate
53
and retained through later attrition. The resulting fold has 377 sequences from 59 training sources,
54
131 from 18 validation sources, and 131 from 20 test sources. No source ID overlaps these roles.
55
A source ID is a grouping key, not a veriﬁed person identiﬁer: the same person could appear in
56
different uploads.
57
Actual representation learner.
The executed model is a small skeleton JEPA adaptation, not a
58
reproduction of S-JEPA. It uses 33 MediaPipe landmarks [6], pelvis centering, within-clip body
59
scaling, and resizing to 64 frames. Each joint’s four-frame coordinate mean becomes a token. A
60
two-layer transformer with 64-dimensional tokens contextualizes the resulting 16 × 33 tokens. A
61
pooled-context MLP predicts masked target features from an exponential-moving-average (EMA)
62
encoder. Prediction targets are sampled from valid shoulder, hip, knee, ankle, heel, and foot-tip
63
tokens. The implemented objective is
64

65

66

67
introduced groups remain available. This is an annotation-informed schedule, not a simulation of
68
disease progression. No current supervised-loss ablation is reported.

What the audit can establish.
The checkpoint hashes, role lists, and training access log agree
70
and record no test tensors in encoder ﬁtting. They establish consistency of the inspected artifacts,
71
not complete historical secrecy of test results. Notebook 04 selects each stage using validation loss;
72
later diagnostics also access test data, and no global mechanism prevents repeated inspection. We
73
therefore present a retrospective case study of the saved run. Training history records 20 epochs
74
per stage. Some runtime settings were not serialized, and all inspected pose caches use legacy crop
75
geometry. These limits, along with precise methods, are retained in Appendix B.

4
Evidence requirements for an ambient health component
109
The case suggests a practical rule: state the intended claim, its unit of evaluation, and the obser-
110
vation that could refute it. The following requirements are proposals motivated by the audit, not
111
experiments completed here.
112
Transfer beyond an upload.
An assertion of useful movement representation should survive raw-
113
kinematic, missingness, and untrained-encoder comparisons on the same groups, with consistent
114
validation and test aggregation. Full fold-local retraining across prespeciﬁed seeds and source-
115
cluster uncertainty would characterize variability. These requirements address source transfer only.
116
Person-level or cross-setting claims need reliable identity grouping or an external cohort; source-ID
117
separation cannot substitute for either.
118
Retention after an update.
A system intended to compare a person’s mobility over time should
119
hold the observed data ﬁxed while auditing changes in the representation model. It should also eval-
120
uate a ﬁxed clinical or movement task across model versions. Matched continued-normal and joint-
121
training controls, alternate condition orders, and alignment-aware comparisons would help separate
122
general optimization effects from curriculum effects. The present experiment has no longitudinal
123
patient observations and does not measure patient deterioration.
124
Prediction beyond observations.
The current objective performs masked representation predic-
125
tion using context throughout a clip. No veriﬁed future-trained checkpoint or current surprise result
126
exists. Moreover, the exploratory sufﬁx-mask code hides only the 12 selected future joints and
127
leaves other future joints visible; its copy-last baseline obtains past tokens from a noncausal full-
128
clip encoder. Such access would prevent a past-only forecasting interpretation even if the missing
129
checkpoint were supplied. A forecasting study must restrict every context input and baseline to the
130
observed preﬁx, make preprocessing preﬁx-causal, retain meaningful time horizons, and compare
131
against persistence and simple motion predictors. Future targets may be encoded for scoring; their
132
information must not enter the predictor’s context. Action-conditioned planning and intervention
133
effects require further evidence beyond observational forecasting.
134
Use in care.
A proposed assistant should carry acquisition quality, model version, and the scope
135
of validated claims alongside a movement summary. A future evaluation could test whether these
136
records help clinicians distinguish a sensor failure, model update, and genuine mobility change. That
137
is a testable human-AI collaboration proposal, not an implemented interface. Clinical utility would
138
require adjudicated endpoints, subgroup evaluation, calibrated uncertainty or abstention, workﬂow-
139
speciﬁc error costs, and prospective evaluation. None is measured here. A ﬂuent explanation cannot
140
supply a missing validation study.
141
5
Counterarguments, limitations, and responsible use
142
This may be a weak model, not a weakness of predictive learning.
We agree. The model
143
is small, the sample is opportunistic, and the current experiment omits untrained-encoder, joint-
144
training, continued-normal, order, and multi-seed controls. The readout aggregation mismatch also
145
limits interpretation. The negative comparison cannot adjudicate the value of an architecture family.
146
It does show why an applied claim needs a matched simpler baseline before representation learning
147
receives credit. Our position does not depend on assuming that larger JEPAs would fail.
148
Source weighting is not always the right estimand.
Also true: a random-clip target and a
149
random-source target answer different questions. Equal source weighting is appropriate for our
150
4

stated upload-transfer audit; it is not an approximation to equal patient weighting without veriﬁed
151
identity. The contribution is making the difference visible. Similarly, missingness can reﬂect true
152
movement difﬁculty as well as occlusion or detector failure. Its association with labels warrants
153
investigation, not automatic removal or a causal claim about shortcut use.
154
Is this relevant to generative health AI?
The evaluated model is non-generative and no health
155
agent is demonstrated. The relevance is upstream: a care-oriented generative system that consumes
156
movement features would inherit their evidence limits. We advocate proportionate requirements.
157
An exploratory representation study need not conduct a clinical trial, but it should not use clinical
158
language to claim evidence that its exploratory endpoints cannot provide.
159
Data use and scope.
Public-video retrieval produces a changing convenience sample. Viewpoint,
160
compression, demonstrators, mobility aids, and visibility may correlate with annotations; demo-
161
graphic balance and cross-upload identity are unresolved. Legacy crop geometry further limits con-
162
clusions about physical kinematics. GAVD distributes annotations and identiﬁers, not unrestricted
163
rights to videos [5]. Public accessibility does not establish consent for every reuse. We do not report
164
a project-speciﬁc institutional ethics determination because none is documented in the reviewed ma-
165
terials. No new participants or clinical interventions were studied. This manuscript contains aggre-
166
gate results; decisions about releasing videos or individual trajectories need a documented data-use
167
review, access and retention rules, and a takedown process. Removing faces does not guarantee
168
that trajectories are non-identifying. Diagnosis, treatment selection, and deployment are outside the
169
evidence presented.

6
Conclusion
171
An auditable movement representation is a useful starting point for ambient health AI, but its claims
172
must remain tied to the task and evaluation unit actually studied. In this case, a simpler sensor-
173
derived readout has the higher observed score, while one upload dominates a clip-weighted retention
174
summary. These ﬁndings motivate explicit boundaries between source transfer, functional retention,
175
future prediction, and clinical utility. Progress toward health agents should test those boundaries
176
rather than assume that one predictive objective crosses them.

Executed method and reproducibility limits
218
Preprocessing and architecture.
The training/readout path marks a landmark valid when visibil-
219
ity is at least 0.45 and its three coordinates are ﬁnite. It subtracts the per-frame mean hip position,
220
with a within-clip median fallback, and divides by the within-clip median of the larger shoulder/hip
221
width in the image plane. Nonﬁnite coordinates are zero-ﬁlled and trajectories are linearly resized to
222
64 frames. The normal-anchor and temporal diagnostics instead interpolate short internal gaps of at
223
most four frames before centering and scaling, while retaining the original validity mask. This dif-
224
fers from training/readout preprocessing, but does not affect the same-vector weighting comparison.
225
These full-clip operations should not be reused unchanged for online forecasting.
226
Each four-frame mean of one joint is projected from three coordinates to 64 dimensions. Learned
227
joint and segment positions are added. Two pre-normalized transformer blocks use four attention
228
heads, feed-forward width 128, GELU, and zero dropout. Hidden positions remain as learned mask
229
tokens. Invalid tokens are excluded from target selection and pooling, but there is no key-padding
230
mask preventing their contextual inﬂuence in attention. The encoder has 70,528 parameters. The
231
predictor pools visible valid contextual tokens, adds a learned target position, and applies an MLP
232
64 →128 →64 (50,368 parameters). The regularization projector is 64 →64 →64 (8,320
233
parameters). The optimized primary components total 129,216 parameters; the EMA encoder is an
234
additional non-gradient copy.
235
Target eligibility is restricted to landmark IDs 11, 12, 23–32. A target patch must be valid in all
236
four frames. The nominal mask fraction is 0.60, but the common target count in a batch is computed
237
from its minimum eligible-token count, with at least one eligible token left visible. It is therefore not
238
60% of all 528 tokens or necessarily 60% of each sample’s eligible set. Prediction uses smooth-L1
239
7

distance to detached EMA features. For projected batch features q, the variance penalty averages
240
max(0, 1 −
√
Var(qj) + 10−4) over dimensions; the covariance penalty sums squared off-diagonal
241
sample covariances and divides by dimension. With batch size B, variance uses denominator B and
242
covariance uses B −1. The optional group head would add 0.10 times condition-classiﬁcation cross-
243
entropy, but is disabled in the reported checkpoint. Neither the tutorial’s centered cross-entropy
244
JEPA nor the older within-label compactness loss describes this run.

Training and recorded settings.
The sampler draws a source uniformly and then a sequence
246
uniformly within that source. Each cumulative stage retains all previously introduced annotations.
247
The 100-row history conﬁrms 20 epochs at each of ﬁve stages. The notebook defaults specify 100
248
updates per epoch, batch size 32, AdamW learning rate 10−3, weight decay 10−4, gradient clipping
249
at 1.0, and EMA decay 0.996. These settings are not all serialized in the checkpoint; environment
250
overrides cannot be reconstructed independently from the retained contract. They are reported as
251
code defaults, not certiﬁed runtime values. The saved output identiﬁes CPU execution.
252
Validation selects the smallest average of per-batch objective values, not an equal-source validation
253
loss. Masks change with the epoch-speciﬁc validation seed. Zero-based selected epochs are 7, 0, 0,
254
4, and 0, with validation objectives 0.15101, 0.12981, 0.12237, 0.11426, and 0.12551. These values
255
are not directly comparable task scores because the evaluated annotation set changes by stage. After
256
stage selection, model parameters rewind to the selected epoch but optimizer moments do not. These
257
details should be corrected or frozen explicitly in a subsequent conﬁrmatory run.
258
Feature and readout deﬁnitions.
The latent readout concatenates means and standard deviations
259
of EMA tokens over all valid joints and over the 12-joint subset, giving 4 × 64 = 256 features. The
260
normal-anchor audit instead uses a 64-dimensional validity-weighted EMA vector pooled over the
261
12-joint subset. Raw kinematics concatenate per-coordinate temporal means, standard deviations,
262
mean absolute frame differences, and standard deviations of differences over 12 joints, giving 4 ×
263
12×3 = 144 features. They are normalized-coordinate summaries, not calibrated physical velocities.
264
Missingness concatenates mean validity per joint and per resized frame, giving 33 + 64 = 97
265
features.
266
For each readout, StandardScaler and balanced-class logistic regression ﬁt on one mean feature vec-
267
tor per training source. Validation chooses C ∈{0.1, 1, 10} by macro-F1, breaking ties toward
268
smaller C. Chosen values are 1, 1, and 10 for latent, missingness, and raw features, respectively.
269
Each scaler and classiﬁer is then reﬁt on 77 training-plus-validation sources. At test, clip probabili-
270
ties are averaged within each source, followed by argmax. Because softmax(W ¯x) and the mean of
271
softmax(Wxi) differ, validation and test use inconsistent aggregation. A future experiment should
272
select with the same prediction rule used for testing and record both rules in the contract.
