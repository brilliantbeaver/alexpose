Abstract
We argue that predictive movement representations proposed for ambient health
1
AI require separate evidence for source transfer, functional retention, future pre-
2
diction, and clinical utility. An audit of one skeleton JEPA run makes these bound-
3
aries concrete. On 20 source-held-out videos, raw kinematics achieve macro-F1
4
0.441, compared with 0.292 for learned features and 0.251 for missingness alone.
5
The same stored normal-validation embeddings yield cross-checkpoint embedding
6
cosine 0.889 under equal clip weighting and 0.701 under equal source weighting:
7
one upload contributes 60 of 64 clips. These are bounded empirical observations,
8
not clinical validation or evidence against an architecture family. They motivate
9
an explicit account of what a perception component can support before its outputs
10
inform a generative health assistant.

Position and relevance
12
A future health assistant could combine movement summaries with other observations to help a
13
clinician review mobility. Its conclusions would still depend on whether those summaries reﬂect
14
transferable movement, acquisition effects, or a model update. A ﬂuent clinical narrative cannot
15
resolve that ambiguity. We argue that source transfer, useful function after adaptation, past-only
16
forecasting, and beneﬁt in care must be tested separately.
17
JEPAs predict target representations rather than reconstructing every observed value [2, 1]. The
18
small skeleton model audited here is non-generative and does not implement an agent or clinical
19
workﬂow. Its relevance to GenAI4Health is the evidence boundary of a possible perception input.
20
Grouped evaluation and leakage auditing are prior practice [3]; our contribution is an auditable case
21
showing how baseline choice and aggregation change the claims the evidence can sustain.
22

Case study and veriﬁed observations
23
We examine a local ﬁve-category subset of GAVD [4], whose annotations are normal, Parkinson’s,
24
stroke, myopathic, and cerebral palsy. These are not diagnoses independently established by this
25
project. The local inventory contains 666 sequences from 103 source videos. A September 4, 2026
26
metadata audit retains 657/100, measured decoding retains 655/98, and pose-quality ﬁltering retains
27
639/97. Source roles are assigned at the metadata-public gate and maintained through attrition. Fold
28
0/seed 42 contains 377 sequences from 59 training sources, 131 from 18 validation sources, and 131
29
from 20 test sources. Other folds in the local registry lack the current completed evaluation. Source
30
separation does not establish separation of people across uploads.

53

Implications and counterarguments
56
The ﬁndings support three practical requirements. First, specify the evaluation unit and compare
57
learned features with raw, missingness, and untrained controls using the same aggregation for se-
58
lection and testing. Second, assess model updates with alignment-aware comparisons and ﬁxed
59
functional endpoints, not cosine alone. Third, require genuinely preﬁx-only inputs, preprocessing,
60
and baselines before calling masked prediction forecasting. The exploratory future-mask path leaves
61
unmasked future joints visible and its copy-last latent uses full-clip context; no qualifying current
62
forecasting result is reported.
63
A larger or better-tuned JEPA might outperform our baseline. Equal source weighting also answers
64
a different question from equal patient weighting, and missingness may partly reﬂect real movement
65
difﬁculty. These counterarguments narrow our claims rather than remove the need to disclose them.
66
The position concerns evidence proportional to an intended use, not a demand that every exploratory
67
model complete clinical validation.
68
No longitudinal patient outcomes, calibrated clinical uncertainty, action-conditioned planning, or
69
clinician-trust study is available. Future care-facing evaluation needs a speciﬁed workﬂow, adjudi-
70
cated outcomes, external validation, and subgroup and error-cost analyses. Public video access does
71
not settle consent or reuse rights; no project-speciﬁc institutional ethics determination is documented
72
in the reviewed materials. This abstract reports aggregate retrospective evidence. It supports careful
73
evaluation of future ambient health components, not diagnosis, treatment, or deployment.
