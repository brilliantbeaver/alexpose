# Literature evidence for the S-JEPA improvement plan

Reviewed on **2026-09-15**. This is a targeted primary-source review, not a claim to have exhaustively searched every publication. It supports [47-improvement-plan.md](47-improvement-plan.md). Dates below distinguish first arXiv posting, inspected version, and verified publication status. A preprint's reported result is evidence to test against, not a replicated fact about GAVD.

The central distinction is between recognizing a person or action and preserving the magnitude, timing, and side of a movement. Literature supporting the former does not establish the latter. The research opportunity is a controlled study of that distinction under noisy video-derived observations.

## How the literature changes the proposed experiments

These are research judgments based on the papers and the existing [local research assessment](../neurips-laterality/RESEARCH_DIRECTIONS.md), rather than reported findings from the papers.

### 1. Prioritize the information-preservation mechanism

The local diagnostic already suggests disagreement between the original target measurement and the same formula applied to resized inputs. The first valuable experiment therefore asks where a specific physical-time quantity becomes hard to recover: observation, resampling, normalization, encoder, or readout.

Use the same eligible full walking bouts to create (a) the historical normalized-time input, (b) fixed-duration windows sampled on a documented physical-time grid, and (c) the same fixed-duration input with timestamps deliberately removed or perturbed as a diagnostic control. Keeping the same windows is essential; otherwise longer/new clips and a new normalization rule change together. Record raw timestamps, valid-joint masks, native video frame rate, selected indices, gaps, normalization references, and the amount of actual observed footage. A resampled frame is not a new observation.

Evaluate direct measurement recovery, a matched random encoder, simple pose/velocity regression, and learned encoders using pooled and temporal readouts. Use observable coordinates or bone-relative quantities whose units and reference frame are explicit. Select a small set of timescales in development; measure whether high-frequency pose jitter dominates velocity targets. Estimate derivatives using actual elapsed time and reject unsupported long gaps. Treat any confidence-weighted loss as an unvalidated adaptation to noisy poses.

This experiment is feasible before large pretraining and can produce an informative negative result: if preprocessing preserves the signal but a particular encoder/readout does not, the next change has a clear target. If direct observables are already unreliable, additional latent losses cannot turn those labels into clinical truth.

### 2. Make future prediction observable and past-only

The strongest temporal follow-up is a prefix-to-future task on full walking sequences. Use fixed horizons in seconds, with all context preprocessing fitted from the observed prefix. Altering future coordinates, confidence, or timestamps that remain after the boundary must leave the context tensor unchanged. The teacher may encode a separate future target during pretraining; the predictor and context encoder must not receive that future data, its normalization statistics, or a mask selected using future motion.

Compare persistence, constant velocity, a periodic/autoregressive baseline fitted only from the prefix, supervised past-pose regression, a random encoder with the same readout, coordinate/motion pretraining, and future-latent pretraining. A mismatched-future control must retain the same source-level sampling, horizon, optimizer exposure, and target marginal distribution as far as feasible. Track both decoding from frozen context features and decoding from the predicted latent; the former alone does not validate the JEPA predictor or rollout.

Score future displacement and/or bone-direction error, preserving signed left–right outcomes when their geometry is trustworthy. Report per-horizon skill relative to the strongest simple baseline and confidence intervals clustered by independent source/participant groups. Do not equate an observation-only information boundary with causal inference about an intervention. Phase-normalized gait cycles are a useful secondary representation, but they must not erase physical cadence or use a future-detected event to construct a past-only input.

### 3. Test dense and deep supervision only after the first two checks

Use a small factorial experiment: base latent objective; visible-token loss only; intermediate-layer loss only; both. Keep token budget, model, masking, random initializations, training sources, and updates matched. Include enough low-level observables and a higher-level endpoint to reveal a trade-off rather than selecting the metric favored by each method. Check variance/effective rank as failure diagnostics, not success metrics.

For a skeleton adaptation, define distance on the joint/time token grid, explicitly choose how spatial graph distance and temporal distance are scaled, and normalize the visible-token term separately from the masked term. Warm up its weight and use development-only choices. Confidence weighting, anatomical weighting, and an auxiliary motion objective should be subsequent single-factor ablations; adding all at once would obscure the mechanism.

The alternative low-cost reference is frozen pretrained RGB-video features extracted from timestamp-aligned person crops across the full walking bouts. Compare pooled and temporal readouts. Test still/repeated-frame, shuffled-frame, person-crop, and background-only controls on the same examples. Those controls determine whether useful video features depend on motion and the person rather than source context. This is a benchmark of a pretrained system, not an equal-data proof that video JEPA is a better learning objective. Distillation from RGB into skeleton features should wait for evidence that this teacher adds relevant motion information.

## Study boundaries that the literature cannot resolve for us

1. **Independent units.** Multiple clips, windows, mirrors, and seeds from one source are not independent subjects. Full-GAVD training increases observed coverage; it does not automatically increase clinical sample size. Split and bootstrap at the strongest verified grouping available.
2. **Pretrained overlap.** Internet-trained RGB encoders may have seen public videos or near duplicates. Known overlap can be checked against disclosed training sources; complete absence cannot be asserted when those lists are unavailable. Record model/checkpoint/version and flag unknown overlap. A clean later-held-out GAVD fold does not erase an external encoder's unknown pretraining history.
3. **Task-defined symmetry.** An augmentation may preserve identity while changing a signed asymmetry target. Specify the expected transformation separately for each observable. Horizontal image reflection, anatomical left/right exchange, 3D reflection, and camera viewpoint are distinct operations.
4. **Clinical meaning.** Predicting a formula derived from the same pose estimator measures representation of that proxy. It does not independently establish affected side, disease severity, or a response biomarker. A clinical claim requires an independently labeled or measured endpoint and appropriate participant/domain separation.
5. **Novelty.** Motion masking, forecasting pretraining, invariant/equivariant splitting, and dense JEPA losses already exist. The defensible contribution is a reproducible account of when an S-JEPA-inspired representation preserves useful signed temporal information under noisy observation, together with a controlled remedy if one works.
