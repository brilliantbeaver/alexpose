# Title options for the Physical World AI paper

The workshop explicitly invites geometry-aware world models, articulated-scene understanding, and evaluation protocols. The paper's title should therefore name geometry, predictive representations, and gait while avoiding claims of clinical validation or a demonstrated general-purpose dynamics model.

Selected title: **Bilateral Geometry for Evaluating Predictive Representations of Human Gait**.

This variation is more specific than “geometry-aware”: it identifies the left–right structure that supplies the paper's reflection rule and signed endpoint. “Evaluating” accurately describes the contribution, while “predictive representations” keeps the connection to JEPA without implying that the study demonstrates real-world forecasting.

| Option | Title | Best use and tradeoff |
|:--|:--|:--|
| 1 | Bilateral Geometry for Evaluating Predictive Representations of Human Gait | Selected. Concrete about the geometry and accurate about the paper's contribution; it omits JEPA from the title but leaves room for a broader representation-learning audience. |
| 2 | Geometry-Aware Evaluation of Predictive Representations for Human Gait | The user's preferred formulation. Cleanest and broadest workshop framing, although “geometry-aware” does not identify the bilateral transformation used in the study. |
| 3 | Evaluating Predictive Representations of Human Gait through Bilateral Geometry | Natural and explanatory; slightly longer and less contribution-forward than option 1. |
| 4 | Geometry-Aware Evaluation of Skeleton JEPA for Human Gait | Names the architecture and modality succinctly, but gives less prominence to laterality and the feature-correspondence result. |
| 5 | From Feature Prediction to Gait Laterality: A Geometry-Aware Evaluation | Captures the paper's intellectual progression and the gap between its internal objective and observable endpoint; “predictive representations” is only implicit. |
| 6 | Bilateral Geometry and Gait Laterality in Skeleton Predictive Representations | Best when the anatomical result should dominate; it sounds more descriptive and less explicitly methodological. |
| 7 | Do Predictive Representations Preserve the Bilateral Geometry of Human Gait? | A readable scientific question that suits a careful negative-result paper, but question titles can sound less definitive in a workshop program. |

If architecture recognition is important for search and reviewer routing, option 4 is the strongest compact choice. If the title should communicate the paper's methodological contribution and remain useful beyond one implementation, option 1 is preferable. Option 2 remains entirely suitable and is the least technical.

Avoid titles claiming that the representation is a validated clinical gait model, that bilateral geometry is learned successfully, or that the experiments establish a real forecasting world model. The abstract can identify Skeleton JEPA, laterality, and the trained-versus-initial comparison without forcing those details into the title.
