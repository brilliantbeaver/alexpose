\begin{abstract}
The human body has an approximate left--right structure: reflecting a pose and exchanging anatomically corresponding left and right landmarks produces another valid pose representation. We use this structure to ask whether a self-supervised skeleton model learns a representation that behaves predictably under reflection. The study uses pose sequences derived from the Gait Abnormality in Video Dataset (GAVD) and a Skeleton Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (S-JEPA). Its target is a coordinate-derived comparison of left-side and right-side motion, not a diagnosis or validated clinical measurement.

The quality-controlled cohort contains 625 pose sequences from 93 source videos. Five outer cross-validation folds are created at the source-video level, so all sequences from one video remain together. In each fold, the encoder is trained from initialization on approximately 80\% of the source videos and tested on the remaining approximately 20\%. Four inner folds, built only from the outer-training sources, select the ridge read-out penalty. The outer-test sources are not used for model training, preprocessing, read-out fitting, or parameter selection. This protocol supports a narrow claim about post-development performance on held-out GAVD source videos; it does not establish generalization to unseen people or clinical validity. The registered paper run and governance reviews are incomplete, so this draft reports the method but no v2.1 empirical conclusion.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A world model learns useful structure by predicting or reconstructing aspects of its input without relying on the final task label. For a model of an articulated body, geometry supplies a natural test of what the representation has learned. We focus on bilateral reflection: the transformation that flips a skeleton horizontally and exchanges every named left body landmark with its right partner.

The central question is deliberately limited. We ask whether a representation trained without the laterality target contains useful information about a signed left-versus-right motion contrast and whether that information changes correctly when the input is mirrored. We separate three statements. A mathematical wrapper can force a read-out to change sign. A trained encoder followed by a probe can exhibit useful sign-changing behavior. The encoder tokens themselves can satisfy a direct reflection-equivariance test. The first statement is a construction; the latter two require empirical evidence.

Laterality v2.1 uses fold-local training. A fresh encoder is trained for each outer fold, and the corresponding outer-test videos remain unavailable until evaluation. This replaces the prior transductive design and changes both the supported claim and the required computation.

\section{Concepts and terminology}

A \textbf{pose sequence} is a series of video frames represented by estimated body-landmark coordinates rather than by the original images. Each sequence contains 33 BlazePose landmarks, and each landmark has horizontal, vertical, and depth coordinates. A \textbf{source video} is the original video from which one or more pose sequences were extracted. Because several sequences can come from the same video, splitting sequences independently could put closely related material in training and testing.

An \textbf{encoder} converts the pose sequence into learned numeric features. A \textbf{read-out}, also called a probe, is a simpler model fitted on those features to predict the laterality target. The encoder follows a fixed, pre-registered training schedule and does not use an early-stopping validation set. The read-out uses inner cross-validation to select its ridge penalty.

\textbf{Cross-validation} rotates which data are held out. An \textbf{outer fold} measures final held-out performance. An \textbf{inner fold} makes a training-only choice without consulting the outer test set. \textbf{Stratification} keeps the source counts for the dataset annotations reasonably balanced. These annotations are used for balancing and a named confounding control; they are not diagnoses or primary prediction labels.

\section{Dataset inventory and cohort construction}

GAVD is the Gait Abnormality in Video Dataset \citep{ranjan2025gavd,gavdRepo2026}. Its official distribution supplies annotations and public video links rather than raw media files. This study operates on derived pose archives and does not redistribute source videos or identity-bearing frames.

The frozen inventory contains 666 annotation files describing 103 source videos. A matching pose archive is available for 642 annotations; 24 annotations have no matching archive. Pre-specified quality-control rules retain 625 pose sequences from 93 source videos and exclude 17 sequences. Exclusion can occur when the target cannot be computed, too few selected landmarks are valid, or too few complete four-frame model patches remain. Inclusion never depends on whether the target is positive, negative, large, or small.

Every retained sequence carries provenance information: the source annotation, pose-archive fingerprint, extraction version, pose-model identity, and visibility threshold. A SHA-256 content digest binds the cohort table and arrays together. Later stages reject content or provenance that does not match the frozen protocol.

\section{Coordinate-derived laterality target}

The target compares movement on the left and right sides of the pose representation. It uses five anatomical pairs: shoulders, knees, ankles, heels, and foot-index landmarks. The hips define the pelvis-centered coordinate system but are excluded from the target because pelvis centering makes their velocity magnitudes algebraically identical.

For each pair, the calculation keeps only \textbf{paired-valid transitions}: both landmarks must be observed at both ends of the same frame transition. Let $m_{L,k}$ and $m_{R,k}$ denote the median left and right speeds for pair $k$. The normalized contrast is
\[
c_k=\frac{m_{L,k}-m_{R,k}}{m_{L,k}+m_{R,k}+\epsilon},
\qquad y=\operatorname{mean}_k c_k.
\]
The small constant $\epsilon$ prevents division by zero. Every pair must provide at least eight shared valid transitions, and all five pairs must be usable. Positive and negative values indicate relatively greater left-side and right-side motion, respectively, under this formula. These are mathematical meanings, not clinical interpretations.

Short missing stretches may be interpolated for encoder input, but interpolated coordinates never define the target. Invalid coordinates have a validity mask and cannot contribute as numeric placeholders. Automated tests confirm that changing invalid placeholders leaves the target unchanged, mirroring negates the target, and mirroring twice restores the input.

\section{Training, validation, and testing design}

\subsection{Why the source video is the splitting unit}

All sequences derived from one source video are assigned together. Mirrored inputs and training augmentations inherit that assignment. No clip, mirror, or augmentation from an outer-test source can enter the corresponding outer-training set.

GAVD does not supply a persistent person identifier, and this project does not infer identity. Two different source videos could contain the same unidentified person. The split therefore supports held-out-source-video evaluation, not held-out-person evaluation.

\subsection{Five outer folds}

The 93 eligible source videos are divided into five stratified outer folds. Approximately four fifths of the sources train the encoder and fit the final read-out, while approximately one fifth provide the untouched test set. Sequence counts vary because source videos contribute different numbers of sequences.

This is not one permanent 80/20 split. Each source serves as an outer-test source exactly once and as an outer-training source in the other four folds. Combining out-of-fold predictions gives one held-out prediction for every eligible sequence.

\subsection{Four inner validation folds}

Each outer-training set is divided into four inner folds. In one inner round, 55--57 sources fit a candidate read-out and 18--19 sources validate its ridge penalty. The validation role rotates until every outer-training source has served as inner validation exactly once.

Because sources contribute different numbers of sequences, an inner fitting partition contains 266--450 sequences and an inner validation partition contains 80--197 sequences. These are rotating read-out partitions, not additional encoder-training or final-test sets. Exact memberships are stored in the split artifact.

These inner folds do not select an encoder checkpoint, stop encoder training, or change the registered encoder schedule. After penalty selection, the read-out is fitted again on all 74--75 outer-training sources and applied to the corresponding 18--19 outer-test sources. Feature scaling, target scale, neutral-band calibration, and other fitted preprocessing use outer-training data only.

\section{Fold-local representation training}

The representation model is an S-JEPA: a Skeleton Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture. It receives 64 time steps with 33 landmarks, groups time into four-frame patches, and uses a 96-dimensional embedding, a four-layer encoder, a two-layer predictor, and four attention heads. During self-supervised training, portions of the sequence representation are masked and the model predicts the hidden latent content. The laterality target and dataset annotation do not enter this objective.

For each outer fold, optimization seed, and training variant, a new encoder starts from a recorded seed-matched initialization and sees only that fold's outer-training sources. Sampling first chooses a source video uniformly and then one sequence from that source, preventing videos with many sequences from dominating.

The paper profile registers five seeds, numbered 42 through 46, and two variants. The \texttt{vanilla} variant never reflects a training sample. The \texttt{reflection\_augmented} variant reflects a sample with probability 0.5. Within a seed, the variants share initialization, source draws, masks, geometric views, optimizer schedule, and update count; reflection decisions use a separate random stream. The complete design contains $5$ outer folds $\times 5$ seeds $\times 2$ variants, or 50 fold-local encoders, each following the fixed 300-epoch schedule.

\section{Held-out evaluation}

For a given fold and seed, feature extraction uses the encoder trained without that fold's test sources. The primary lane fits a free linear ridge read-out to the learned representation. Comparison lanes include an identically structured untrained encoder, a target-component self-consistency oracle, pooled and missingness features, constructed one-pass and two-pass antisymmetric read-outs, and direct encoder-token reflection tests. The oracle verifies that saved pair contrasts reconstruct the target and is not a learned baseline.

The principal performance measure is source-balanced $R^2$, which gives every source video equal total weight. Mean absolute error, source-level sign accuracy, and mirror errors are secondary measures. Each checkpoint is evaluated separately. Metrics and squared mirror errors are computed within a checkpoint before registered seeds are averaged; signed errors cannot cancel across seeds.

An analytically antisymmetric wrapper and an empirically equivariant representation are different claims. Exact output oddness proves the wrapper's construction. Useful learned content additionally requires positive held-out predictive utility and improvement over the paired untrained initialization. A strict representation claim separately requires low direct token-equivariance error and improvement over initialization.

\section{Uncertainty and decision rules}

Optimization-seed variation and source-sampling uncertainty are kept separate. Five seeds show how training changes with registered randomized choices. Separately, 2,000 bootstrap repetitions resample source videos while preserving all sequences from a source together. The resulting intervals are conditional on the fitted cross-validation pipeline; they do not imply that the observed videos are a random sample of all people or recording conditions.

Decision thresholds are frozen in the protocol. Native probe behavior requires useful absolute held-out prediction, sufficiently low mirror error, and improvement over the paired untrained encoder. Strict representation behavior requires sufficiently low token-equivariance error and a favorable learned-minus-initialized comparison. A null or adverse result remains reportable and does not authorize post-result retuning.

\section{Limitations}

The design prevents source-video overlap but cannot guarantee that people are distinct across videos. The supported claim is post-development, within-GAVD cross-validated performance on held-out source videos. It is not a claim about unseen people, another dataset, clinical validity, prevalence, diagnosis, or treatment.

The target captures one signed left-versus-right motion contrast, not the full kinematic structure of gait, and it is not a validated clinical index. Learning claims remain conditional on the BlazePose schema, pose extraction, preprocessing, architecture, registered seeds, and measured controls. Unmeasured acquisition and missingness effects may remain.
