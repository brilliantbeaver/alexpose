Suggested caption: **Where laterality enters skeleton JEPA training.** Source-video groups determine all train/test membership; each video's clips and derived views retain its outer fold. Prepared pose coordinates have shape B × 64 × 33 × 3, with a separate validity mask. Four-frame joint patches become 528 tokens of width 96. Named left/right joints and anatomical target selection supply body structure. The student retains the complete token grid while zeroing hidden coordinate embeddings before adding positions, and the predictor estimates hidden features from a complete-input teacher. The teacher receives no gradients and follows an exponential moving average (EMA) of student weights. Validity and hidden locations follow the same permutation. This explicit penalty has retained synthetic demonstration evidence only. The signed coordinate-derived target enters the frozen readout and evaluation after pretraining; it does not supervise the encoder. Ridge fitting and penalty selection use training-source groups, and scores use held-out source videos. The initial S-JEPA control shares the learned pose extractor, anatomical schema, and preprocessing.

The saved workflow performs deterministic clip-local preparation before it writes source splits; it does not claim to cut original dataset clips after splitting. The raw-time target is calculated in a separate unfilled coordinate lane before model-only gap filling and temporal resizing, so it is not derived from the prepared tensor depicted here. Anatomical and motion mask choices change target eligibility or sampling while keeping the input schema intact. ## Detailed training pipeline for the supplement

[training_pipeline.svg](training_pipeline.svg) · [PNG preview](training_pipeline.png)

Suggested caption: **Where anatomical laterality enters skeleton JEPA training.** The input preserves a common 33-landmark schema and carries an observation-validity mask alongside the coordinates. Clip-local preparation produces 64 frames per clip; four-frame joint patches produce 528 tokens of width 96. Masking zeroes hidden coordinate embeddings before adding positional embeddings, while retaining the complete token grid. All clips and derived views from a source video share its outer fold, and only training sources supply encoder updates. The online encoder and predictor learn to predict the complete-input teacher's hidden features; the teacher receives no gradients and follows an exponential moving average of online weights. The objective also regularizes two geometric views. The completed reflection-augmentation, anatomical-target, and motion/region-mask comparisons are separate experiments. Notebook 09 adds an explicit loss comparing original and reflected tokens after the supplied left/right joint permutation, with identity action on feature channels; its retained execution is synthetic only. Neither dataset condition labels nor the signed movement target supervise encoder training. The frozen readout uses training-source labels and is scored on excluded source videos. Its initial-weight control retains the same learned pose extraction, anatomy, and preprocessing.

The illustration deliberately follows the implementation's order: accepted clips are prepared before source-fold assignment. Preparation uses each clip independently and fits no cross-clip statistics. The diagram does **not** claim that existing GAVD clips were cut after splitting. Training samples, target masks, and geometric views are generated only after the source groups are fixed. Future prediction experiments need their own rule separating an observed prefix before any input preparation that could use future coordinates.

The shape statement concerns the prepared tensor's axes and landmark identities. Interpolation, pelvis centering, body scaling, temporal resizing, and input masking modify coordinate values; they do not preserve the raw trajectory unchanged. Notebook 09's teaching execution uses 16 frames and embedding width 16, which are labelled separately from the full-scale settings.

## Reflection and the target

[reflection_and_target.svg](reflection_and_target.svg) · [PNG preview](reflection_and_target.png)

Suggested caption: **A known geometric relation supplies an audit of signed movement information.** Anatomical reflection negates the horizontal coordinate and exchanges every supplied left/right joint pair, including the corresponding validity and hidden-target locations, while retaining temporal order. For each of five bilateral landmark pairs, the target contrasts median speed over shared valid transitions. Exchanging the sides reverses its sign even when the original movement is asymmetric. The illustrated bodies and values are schematic; no depicted pose or value is presented as an observed recording. The joint-permutation relation for encoder tokens is distinct from an algebraic odd readout, which can enforce sign reversal before training. Constant nonzero tokens provide a failure control because they can satisfy token consistency while carrying no between-clip information.

The five target pairs are shoulders, knees, ankles, heels, and foot-index landmarks (labelled “toe” in the figure for readability). Each pair requires at least eight shared valid transitions, and all five must contribute. The raw-time target lane uses unfilled observations, valid pelvis centering, and a bilateral body scale. Its speeds are in image-derived normalized coordinate units per second when valid timestamps are available, rather than calibrated metric velocity; the ratio is dimensionless. A positive target means a positive average of the five normalized left-minus-right speed contrasts. Opposing pair differences may cancel; the sign does not identify the clinically impaired side.

## Latest learning results

[learning_results.svg](learning_results.svg) · [PNG preview](learning_results.png)

Suggested caption: **Learned clip correspondence and recoverable laterality give different outcomes.** All values come from the completed Notebook 18 grid with 625 clips from 93 source videos. (A) Large dots show mean pooled held-out seed scores; open dots show the five individual seed scores. Each seed's score pools its five held-out source folds and gives each source equal total weight. These dots are not confidence intervals. The motion-sensitive summary combines feature means, temporal variation, absolute feature changes, and observation support. Its paired initial encoder outperforms each trained teacher arm in every seed. The initial network still receives estimated poses from a learned detector. (B) Every trained diagnostic row has smaller hidden-feature error for its own clip than a valid mismatched clip from another source; the initial control shows mixed directions. The 375 trained rows are 125 encoder fits evaluated under three masking patterns. The 75 initial rows are deduplicated across the two experiment families. These rows repeatedly use the same source videos and are not independent samples. Only the sign of the within-predictor contrast is compared; teacher-feature error magnitudes cannot rank different models. (C) The three alternative-mask contrasts use their own count-matched random reference. The 95% intervals resample 93 source videos 2,000 times while retaining paired conditions and seed predictions, and condition on the fitted models. They include zero and do not establish equivalence.

This figure does not mix the Notebook 18 scores with earlier readout implementations. It includes no synthetic performance result and no newly trained encoder. The separate, post-hoc trained-versus-initial bootstrap computed during manuscript review is not plotted here, so all plotted intervals come directly from the retained grid.

The 10 September precision update rounds score labels and substantive differences to three decimal places. Nonzero differences below 0.001 retain one significant digit, so the two small effects remain −0.0008 and +0.0005 while the connected-region label becomes −0.009. Plotted coordinates and the full-precision source ledger are unchanged. This convention also applies to the print layout and is explained in v7's evaluation section.
