Auditing Predictive Context for Gait Anomaly Detection Anonymous submission Abstract Predicting a joint from the rest of the body seems a plausi- ble way to detect unusual gait, but an apparent gain over raw prediction error need not demonstrate learned movement re- lationships. We audit this idea with matched own-joint, local- interval and full-body completion tasks, coordinate and latent targets, and source-separated normal references. Real GAVD processing yields 8,950 eligible windows from 251 source videos, including 23 normal sources. The latent benefit pro- file reaches AUROC 0.641, while a calibrated full-error score reaches 0.673. The fixed primary comparison against raw er- ror gives a difference of 0.101 with a conditional 95% interval of [-0.035, 0.224]. On unchanged coordinate targets, a ran- dom encoder with fitted heads reaches 0.701 versus 0.686 for the learned encoder. Longer training does not establish an advantage. Normal prediction inadequacy, reference sen- sitivity and contextual teacher targets limit interpretation. All retained normal sources overlap earlier development, making this an exploratory audit. The contribution is a reproducible set of checks that prevents a favorable point estimate from be- coming an unsupported claim about learned or neurological relationships. Introduction An unusual walking pattern need not be difficult to predict. A repeated compensation may be highly regular, while or- dinary walking observed from an unfamiliar camera may produce substantial prediction error. This distinction moti- vates a different question: how much does the rest of the body help predict a hidden joint, compared with that joint’s own visible history? Consider a hidden left-ankle interval. Its prediction may change when the model additionally sees the opposite leg, arms and trunk. Either unusually weak or un- usually strong predictive help could distinguish an observed pattern from a normal reference. We investigate this question through matched skeleton- completion tasks (Figure 1). A normal-trained representation supports three information conditions, with restrictions en- forced before contextual encoding. Related work and biological motivation Skeleton S-JEPA learns action representations by predict- ing teacher embeddings from partial skeletal observations (Abdelfattah and Alahi 2024). MAMP instead emphasizes masked motion prediction (Mao et al. 2023). Both motivate the prediction task, but their motion-aware masking differs from our uniform selection. VICReg supplies agreement, variance and covariance regularization (Bardes, Ponce, and LeCun 2022). Normal-only gait reconstruction and predic- tion already appear in FSGait (Duan, Wan, and Zhao 2024). SeeKer estimates conditional keypoint densities and incorpo- rates keypoint-detector confidence (Delić, Grcić, and Šegvić 2025), while STG-NF models pose-sequence likelihoods us- ing graph-based normalizing flows (Hirschorn and Avidan 2023). GenGait also uses normal-only masked gait recon- struction and joint inconsistency scoring (Motta et al. 2026). Beyond Marginals separates marginal, temporal and inter- variable structure for multivariate anomaly detection (Roy, Boker, and Mili 2025). Our narrower question concerns the difference between matched information conditions and its regional normal-reference profile. The contribution is the matched-context audit and its diagnostic controls, not pri- ority for normal-only gait prediction, conditional skeleton modeling or anatomical anomaly localization. Clinical findings motivate examining relationships with- out determining their expected direction. Data and observation protocol We use the refreshed GAVD annotations, whose public- source videos provide observational gait categories, se- quence boundaries, views and bounding boxes (Ranjan et al. 2025, 2026). The binary endpoint is exactly the normal folder versus every other folder across all 12 categories. A positive label does not uniformly signify disease: the catalog includes nonpathological movement circumstances. Parkin- sons, stroke, cerebral palsy and myopathic labels additionally define a separate five-class task with normal gait. The annotation inventory contains 1,874 sequences from 348 source IDs, comprising 32 normal and 316 abnormal sources. Ingestion obtained 332 decodable videos, including 184 newly acquired videos. Pose-quality filtering retained 9,474 windows from 1,389 sequences and 311 sources, in- cluding 28 normal sources. The subsequent fixed profile- support gate retained 8,950 windows from 1,303 sequences and 251 sources: 23 normal and 228 abnormal. These are source-video identifiers, not verified independent peo- ple. Media failure and pose attrition are recorded rather than replaced with cached features. Table 1 shows substan- tial category-dependent selection. The target population is recordings meeting this observation and support rule, not every annotated recording. We extract projected two-dimensional BlazePose land- marks from the annotated person using sequence bounding boxes (Bazarevsky et al. 2020). We retain BlazePose indices 11–16 and 23–32, covering shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees, ankles, heels and foot indices. These 16 joints alone enter the model and masking sampler. Coordinates with con- fidence below 0.5 remain missing. Resampling to 15 Hz permits interpolation only between adjacent valid observa- tions and excludes videos below 15 frames per second. Each window contains 32 frames with stride 16. Coordinates are centered on the frame’s pelvis and scaled using valid torso lengths from the first half of the window. A token contains one joint’s two-dimensional coordinates over four frames, giving 128 tokens per window. A token is observed only when all eight coordinates are finite. Windows require at least 75% observed tokens overall and 50% in each half. Invalid patches are numerically zeroed at projection and excluded from attention keys, targets and pooling. Missing observations are not reconstructed as training labels. Before fold assignment, a deterministic bank of 16 uni- form masks establishes profile eligibility using only availabil- ity and annotated time identities. Each source must supply at least four distinct eligible target patches for every read- out joint. Readout uses 12 joints: elbows, wrists, knees, an- kles, heels and foot indices. All 16 joints still participate in training and masking. Support is deduplicated across over- lapping windows within each sequence; separate annotation sequences are not assumed to be independent physical ob- servations. Matched prediction experiment We first train a small skeleton JEPA using normal fitting sources only. Its encoder has two transformer blocks, four attention heads and width 64. Uniform sampling hides ex- actly half of the 128 tokens, independently of coordinates, velocity and motion intensity. A visible-context encoder pre- dicts centered, sharpened distributions from an exponential- moving-average teacher over all observed tokens in the com- plete window. A VICReg term regularizes agreement and representation variance/covariance across masked views. It discourages collapse and redundancy; it does not repel dis- ease classes. Missing targets remain excluded. With teacher representation z T , center c and predictor output u, the tar- get is q = softmax((z T − c)/0.06) and the prediction is p = softmax(u/0.1). Pretraining minimizes target cross- entropy plus 0.1 times VICReg. VICReg acts on projected, valid-token pooled online embeddings from two indepen- dently masked views; the second view also receives small coordinate noise. The teacher receives no gradient and is updated after the online optimizer step. The primary recipe uses 512 pretraining updates fol- lowed by 512 updates for each prediction head, with batch size 16, AdamW learning rate 0.0003, weight decay 0.05 and teacher momentum 0.996. Training first samples a nor- mal source uniformly and then a window from that source. Normal-development diagnostics are recorded every 64 up- dates. Head diagnostics reuse four fixed source-sampled minibatches with fixed joint and mask draws. Fixed budgets establish a reproducible computational setting, not conver- gence. The primary study uses neither stochastic positional noise nor a learned stochastic latent variable. After pretraining, we freeze the online view encoder for contexts and the EMA teacher and target center for latent tar- gets. Six independently fitted heads cross three context con- ditions with two target types. For target joint j and time patch t, own context contains visible valid patches of joint j; patch context adds visible joints in the target four-frame interval; full context contains every visible valid token. Allowed to- kens are gathered before the encoder’s first attention layer. Restricted branches never receive cached full-context embed- dings. A position query supplies the target identity without its measurements. Within each target type, heads share ini- tialization, sampled windows, masks and update budgets, but have separate fitted parameters. The coordinate target contains the eight normalized coor- dinates of the hidden patch, scored by mean squared error. The latent target is the frozen teacher’s distribution, scored by KL divergence from teacher to prediction. All branches re- ceive the same target. A requested target is eligible only when observed and accompanied by at least two visible valid own- joint patches. Eligibility filtering does not redraw masks. During head fitting, a joint is sampled uniformly from all 16 joints independently of visibility, and eligible hidden patches of that joint contribute to all six objectives. The same 16- mask bank is used for all sources and comparators at evalu- ation. These are completion tasks: visible context can occur af- ter the hidden patch. Shared pelvis and torso normalization already supplies a body reference to the own condition. The patch condition includes short motion as well as geometry. Moreover, the latent teacher can encode other joints in its target. Predictive benefit therefore means benefit for these fitted models under the declared body reference. It is nei- ther conditional mutual information nor a causal measure of inter-joint coupling. At the raw-input level, full context contains own context. For an ideal predictor that minimizes expected squared error, additional information therefore cannot increase the optimal risk. Our learners do not establish that bound: they separately re-encode each context through a frozen transformer, so their resulting memories need not be nested representations, and finite fitted heads may underfit or overfit. The observed loss difference measures model-dependent predictive gain, not mutual information, neural coupling or the intrinsic value of the added observations. Negative benefit on normal coordi- nate targets motivates a learning-adequacy check; it is not evidence that coordination or additional body information is biologically harmful. Normal-development coordinate losses showed worse full- context than own-context error in all four folds under the 512-head-update schedule. This training-adequacy diagnos- tic motivated a separately retained 4,096-head-update sensi- tivity with the same 512 pretraining updates. The decision used normal development observations rather than abnormal ranking outcomes. The original schedule remains primary, and the longer schedule cannot replace it based on a favorable test result. Regional normal-reference profiles For an eligible target, let ℓ o , ℓ p and ℓ f denote own, patch and full losses. We define signed predictive benefit as b = ℓ o −ℓ f . Positive values indicate lower error with full context; neg- ative values remain valid observations. For each joint, we first average all eligible mask-target exposures within a win- dow, then average windows within sequences and sequences within sources. Repeated mask exposures retain equal weight in the window mean; the support gate separately deduplicates annotated target patches. Each region then gives equal weight to its four joints: left knee/ankle/heel/foot, the corresponding right joints, and both elbows/wrists. For source s, the three regional benefits form b s . Five reserved normal sources estimate a reference mean µ g and sample variance v g for each region. With v̄ the mean regional variance, the anomaly score is 3 D(s) = 1 X (b sg − µ g ) 2 . 3 g=1 max(0.5v g + 0.5v̄, 10 −8 ) This two-sided distance accommodates unusually high or low predictive benefit. It is not a calibrated probability. The shrinkage weight 0.5 and variance floor were fixed for this run and were not tuned on test rankings. Primary latent-benefit sample variances range from 0.046 to 0.362 and coordinate- benefit variances from 1.63 × 10 −5 to 0.00606; the floor does not bind. Identically constructed distances from own, patch and full losses provide constituent controls. They are necessary because comparing D with raw full loss changes subtraction, regional preservation and reference standardiza- tion together. Patch-context losses are retained as a control for information near the hidden interval. Evaluation and controls Four outer folds hold out disjoint source IDs. Their test pop- ulations contain respectively 6, 6, 6 and 5 normal sources, plus 57 abnormal sources each. Five non-test normal sources per fold serve as development observations and the normal reference, leaving 12 or 13 normal fitting sources. Within each fold, fitting, reference and test sources are disjoint. Across folds, 20 reference assignments use 15 distinct normal sources, with pairwise reference overlaps ranging from zero to two sources. Fitting and reference sources recur, so the four models are not independent replications. Abnormal sources never fit the normal-only anomaly models or reference. They enter fitting only in separately identified supervised controls. The primary contrast is latent benefit distance versus raw full-context KL on identical readout support. We compute AUROC within each fold and average using test-normal counts as weights. Average precision is reported with ab- normal prevalence. Scores from independently fitted refer- ences are not pooled into one ROC curve. Supporting paired comparisons against full-distance and own-distance controls address whether subtraction contributes beyond constituent calibration; their multiplicity is explicit. A paired bootstrap resamples source IDs within fold and binary label, preserving pairing between scores and recomputing the same weighted AUROC difference over 1,000 replicates. Its interval is conditional on fitted mod- els, reference observations, fold assignments and observed source identities. It does not propagate retraining, reference- estimation or unknown participant dependence. With only five or six test normals per fold, its endpoints are coarse un- certainty summaries. Supporting intervals are unadjusted for multiplicity; intervals containing zero do not establish equal- ity or absence of benefit. Fold-specific results and leave-one- reference-out score changes accompany this interval. A wide interval is inconclusive, while a narrow interval concerns this fitted analysis rather than performance after new training or population sampling. Removing one reference source tests sensitivity of the reference calculation without retraining the predictors. The coordinate-target experiment tests whether find- ings extend beyond contextual teacher targets. A random- backbone control retains fitted heads and matched draws. Its coordinate targets remain comparable, whereas its la- tent target changes with the teacher. A fixed source-balanced ridge control uses 256 sampled windows/masks and regu- larization 10, with separate joint/time/context regressions. It shares information access and scoring support, but not neu- ral parameter sharing or optimization budget. Its minimum target-support check establishes estimability, not adequate learning or a strong linear optimum. A matched-budget sensitivity selects four distinct anno- tated target patches per readout joint using a fixed hash. It does not equalize mask exposure, contextual information, temporal coverage or precision. Source-level exposure counts remain part of the audit. Additional normal-only controls fit regularized Gaussian distances to 36 handcrafted motion features or joint-availability and camera-view features. A separately supervised Random Forest uses only the latter observation features. Every imputer, scaler and estimator is fitted using the appropriate training sources. The separately supervised five-class comparison uses a Random Forest on handcrafted features and a frozen-encoder probe with the same outer folds, excluding inner reference normals from fitting. Source-label weighting prevents multiple labels or many windows from increasing a source’s total evaluation weight. These classifiers receive abnormal labels and answer a different question from normal-only ranking. Development diagnostics apply relative-leg timing shifts, whole-body timing shifts and observation noise/dropout to normal fitting sources. They measure fitted-model sensi- tivity rather than held-out mechanism validation or virtual diagnoses. A four-factor teacher diagnostic crosses orig- inal/edited predictions with original/edited teacher targets on eight unchanged arm/right-leg joints. Its raw-KL predic- tor, teacher and interaction terms sum algebraically to total change. They do not decompose the nonlinear three-region score. The reproducibility record fixes manifest and pose hashes, model configuration, source roles, mask seed and checkpoint identity. Every reported comparison must reconcile its re- quired method, fold, target and observation-budget cells; a summary file alone is insufficient evidence that all controls completed. Missing cells remain missing rather than being replaced by synthetic results. Discussion and limitations This is exploratory internal evaluation on a cohort overlap- ping earlier development. All 23 retained normal sources and 58 abnormal sources occur in the earlier retained index; 170 abnormal sources do not. Absence from that index does not prove absence of prior inspection. New folds prevent direct fitting/test overlap but do not erase analyst adaptation. Newly scored abnormal recordings compared with reused normals are not an independent replication. Five reference sources and five or six test normals per fold severely constrain precision. Additional abnormal videos cannot replace normal diversity. Visibility selection, camera view, walking aids, repeated people and overlapping annota- tions may influence discrimination. Folder labels are obser- vational, projected pose is imperfect, and neither diagnoses nor physical forces are measured. Finally, no validated pub- lished anomaly baseline is included. The fixed ridge control and supervised Random Forest cannot establish superiority over SeeKer, STG-NF or other published methods. Conclu- sions must remain conditional on the tested predictors, ob- servation pipeline and source population. These results narrow the research question. A predictive- context profile can rank some non-normal recordings, but the present evidence does not attribute that ranking to learned inter-joint relationships. Reference calibration, the target rep- resentation, finite learning and observation coverage all re- quire separate checks. Failure of the fitted predictor to exploit more context should trigger a model-adequacy investigation before a biological explanation. A stronger follow-up should first test predictors on syn- thetic processes with known dependencies and a nested or residual architecture that can retain the own-context solution. It should then freeze choices before examining new normal participants, validate an established pose-anomaly baseline on exactly the same observation support, and collect blinded movement annotations. External recordings with participant identity, measured strength or electromyography would be needed to evaluate neurological or neuromuscular explana- tions. More abnormal web videos alone cannot answer those questions. Conclusion This real-video study audits a biologically motivated prediction-context diagnostic rather than claiming a new dis- ease detector. Matched constituent scores, a random encoder with fitted heads, longer training and reference perturbations prevent a favorable raw-error comparison from becoming an unsupported representation claim. On the current GAVD co- hort, the latent profile’s advantage remains inconclusive and its neurological interpretation unvalidated.
