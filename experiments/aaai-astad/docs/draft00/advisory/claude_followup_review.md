## (a) Where my first advice was wrong

I made four errors that matter, plus one I need to retract outright.

First, I treated the label structure incorrectly. I wrote about "healthy negatives" and implied the five-class study carved clean clinical categories out of a broader pool. You have corrected this: normal is the only normal folder, and everything else, including exercise, style, and pregnant, is abnormal for the binary task, without any claim of confirmed diagnosis. This matters because it reframes the 245 out-of-cohort sources. They are not a mixed or unlabeled reservoir. They are additional abnormal-category material with no new normal sources. My suggestion that they might serve as a confirmatory set was doubly wrong: they add no normal calibration diversity, and the normal count remains the hard ceiling it was.

Second, and most seriously, my central attribution instrument was statistically invalid as I stated it. I claimed that regressing the anomaly score on a pose-quality covariate and watching AUROC fall would prove quality caused the signal. It does not. Matching and residualization are observational. Real movement changes tracking quality, so conditioning on quality can remove genuine signal rather than nuisance, and I would not be able to tell the two apart. The honest instrument is a paired intervention on the same clip: take one clip, perturb only the observation quality while holding the movement fixed, and measure how the score moves. That supports a narrow measurement-effect claim and nothing broader. I also failed to say that any regression must be fit without test leakage. Residualization fit on the test scores is circular.

Third, I overclaimed when I wrote that collapsing the AUROC under residualization gives you the finding "regardless of absolute training quality." That sentence is wrong. An undertrained encoder produces a degenerate score, and residualizing a degenerate score tells you nothing about the mechanism of a properly trained one. Adequate training and the budget ladder remain prerequisites, not optional robustness checks. Residualization cannot rescue an undertrained result.

Fourth, I conflated two different comparisons and then drew a conclusion from the conflation. The current untrained-encoder result comes from a supervised probe trained on abnormal labels. The anomaly score comes from normal-only training. These are not the same experiment, so the untrained-encoder win is not evidence that the normal-only anomaly score loses to a normal-only untrained score. The only fair statement I can make is that a new normal-only detector has seen no abnormal categories at all, and that is a different object from a probe that saw four abnormal categories and was then tested on seven more.

Fifth, I was wrong to call five or six versus nineteen an order-of-magnitude shortfall, and wrong to float calibration as a near-complete fallback paper. The ratio is roughly three to one, not ten to one. The finite-sample threshold arithmetic is textbook and thin on its own. I retract the implication that either the calibration note or an inconclusive detection result would be publishable. Neither is promised.

On the references: I was told the coordinator opened all named 2026 works on primary sites. My lack of tool access does not make them fabricated, and I should not have implied global unverifiability. I will cite status cautiously but not call them hallucinated.

## (b) Which study to run

I recommend the cross-joint predictive-benefit study proposed by the other advisor, combined with broad unseen-abnormal-category transfer as the evaluation axis. I drop the quality-mechanism study from the critical path.

The quality-mechanism study goes because the only valid version of it is a paired same-clip intervention, and even done correctly it supports a narrow measurement-effect claim that does not carry a seven-page paper. It is useful only as a control inside another study, which is where I now put it.

The cross-joint study is the strongest single idea on the table because it specifies a mechanism that is intrinsic to gait and that the baselines do not capture. The claim is that a predictor using all visible joints gains predictive accuracy on a held-out target joint over a matched predictor using only that joint's own visible trajectory, trained on normal sources with identical uniform masks and identical target tokens. That accuracy difference is cross-joint predictive benefit. The abnormality hypothesis is that loss of this benefit separates some abnormal categories from normal. Raw latent discrepancy and normal-embedding distance remain baselines, because regular abnormal gait can still be perfectly predictable, so predictability alone is not the quantity of interest. The quantity of interest is the coordination-specific component.

The combination with unseen-category transfer is natural and costs little, because the labels already exist. Train the normal-only predictors, fix the benefit statistic, and evaluate separation across the five study categories first, then across the additional GAVD abnormal categories among the 245 sources that have usable media. This is where the label clarification pays off: those sources give abnormal-category breadth at no normal cost, so the transfer axis is about whether the coordination deficit generalizes to abnormal categories the detector never trained against, which it never did, since it is normal-only.

## (c) Exact contribution and the result required

Prior art boundary. Beyond Marginals (TMLR 2025, arXiv 2509.15033) already studies multivariate dependency anomalies, so generic multivariate-dependency novelty is spent. RobustGait (WACV 2026) already studies gait corruption and extractor bias, so a generic degradation table is spent. The contribution cannot be either of those.

The contribution that survives both is this: a coordination-specific anomaly statistic for skeletal gait, defined as the cross-joint predictive benefit under matched masks and targets, with an identity-level control that rules out the trivial explanation. The non-trivial design element the other advisor correctly insists on is that a shared full-context teacher target can itself encode other joints, so a full-context benefit could appear even for a local target. The study must therefore run an identical local-coordinate or local-patch target control, and claim coordination only if the benefit exceeds what the local-target control produces. Without that control the result is imprecise, not a finding.

The result required for a strong paper is a conjunction, stated before looking at confirmatory data. First, on normal held-out sources the all-joint predictor must beat the own-joint predictor on the target joint by a margin whose source-clustered interval excludes zero, and this margin must survive the local-target control. Second, the benefit-loss statistic must separate at least one abnormal category from normal with a source-level interval that excludes the prespecified minimum useful separation, and must do so better than both raw discrepancy and embedding distance under the same evaluation. Third, the controlled limb phase-shift intervention, which preserves each limb's marginal distribution while breaking inter-limb timing, must reduce the benefit statistic on normal clips, confirming the statistic responds to coordination rather than to per-limb shape. Phase shift is a diagnostic intervention on the statistic, not a simulated diagnosis, and must be described that way.

If the margin survives the local-target control and the phase-shift intervention moves the statistic as predicted but the abnormal separation interval includes the minimum useful value, that is an informative negative: the coordination signal is real and measurable but does not transfer to these abnormal categories. That is a publishable finding only if the first and third legs hold firmly, because then the paper reports a validated statistic that fails to separate, which is a precise scientific statement, not an undertrained null.

Stopping rule in one sentence. Abandon the coordination claim the moment the all-joint benefit fails to exceed the local-target control, and abandon the coordination-interpretation the moment phase shift fails to move the statistic, because past those two gates every downstream separation number is uninterpretable.

A last distinction between falsification and imprecision. The Nov 16 and Nov 17 gates are falsification points: failing them says the statistic is not what we claim. The Nov 18 gate is about precision: a separation interval that includes the minimum useful value does not falsify coordination, it fails to resolve transfer, and the paper must say exactly that rather than present it as evidence either way.
