THE GAIT STUDY Can the other leg help explain unusual walking? We test whether the opposite leg improves prediction and whether that improvement helps detect unusual gait. Walking measurements A controlled prediction Anomaly detection We follow joint positions over time in videos and laboratory recordings. We hide part of one leg and measure what the other leg adds. We then test whether the resulting score distinguishes unusual movement. The talk separates useful prediction from useful anomaly detection. Gait prediction | A guide to the measured study 22 / 43 METHOD EXPLANATION | ILLUSTRATION The three anomaly scores describe different quantities. Cross-leg benefit becomes an anomaly score only after comparison with separate normal reference recordings. Movement score M Cross-leg score R Combined score S M compares knee posture, ankle travel and trunk tilt with normal walking. R asks whether seeing the other leg helps prediction more or less than usual in normal walking. S adds M and R and divides by two. The weights stay fixed: S = (M + R) / 2. Both scores measure departure from a normal reference; neither is a probability of disease.
