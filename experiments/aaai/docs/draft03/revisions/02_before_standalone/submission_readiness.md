# Readiness of the positive interpretation

The positive framing is defensible as a measurement and gait-completion paper. It is not a positive anomaly-detection result. The title and abstract should keep that distinction clear.

The strongest evidence is the independent participant result: 5.2% lower healthy completion error, consistent fold means, improvement in 109 of 128 participants, and better performance than direct interpolation. The GAVD focused task provides a complementary video result, while the unchanged uniform task limits claims of broad masking superiority.

The principal novelty is the controlled empirical comparison and its observation-support checks. Residual fitting, anatomical masks, bilateral relationships and normative distances are established ideas. The work becomes useful by testing them together on real data and showing where successful completion stops supporting an anomaly interpretation. Its novelty is moderate rather than a new foundation-model architecture or physiological theory.

Compared with draft02, this version better explains what worked. It keeps the original negative primary detection outcome in the abstract and results, and explicitly labels the stronger emphasis on secondary completion results as post-outcome. It does not alter the frozen models, score weights, cohorts or evaluation banks.

The main unresolved issue for the positive claim is unequal effective capacity. A richer own-leg basis, tested in a new frozen study, could reduce the bilateral advantage. Other limits are the small previously explored GAVD normal sample, estimated pose targets, shared body references, artificial occlusion, one laboratory trial per participant, conditional-only intervals and failed paired-support timing adequacy.

The next strongest experiment would test a closer flexibility match and normal-only reliability calibration on untouched participants or repeated trials. It should retain local posture and amplitude rather than assume that regular movement is normal. No such improved detector has been executed here.

The manuscript and vector figures are generated from saved evidence. The [validation record](final_validation.json) gives the actual PDF, page, font, link, citation and numerical checks. The original 18 notebook executions and 137 passing tests are shared with [draft02](../draft02/submission_readiness.md); this editorial version does not pretend to be a new model run. Independent reviews and their resolutions are linked in [response_to_reviews.md](response_to_reviews.md).

A submission can honestly lead with useful completion and the conditions needed to interpret it. It should not claim state-of-the-art detection, neurological causality, clinical readiness or a guaranteed workshop outcome.
