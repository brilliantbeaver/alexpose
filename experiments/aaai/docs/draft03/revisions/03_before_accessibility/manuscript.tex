\begin{abstract}
Reliable gait anomaly detection requires distinguishing unusual movement from prediction errors caused by incomplete observations or an inadequate model. We introduce a controlled framework that measures how much opposite-leg observations improve prediction beyond a retained own-leg solution and an additional own-leg control. The predictors share observable targets, restricted inputs and fixed evaluation masks. Motion-independent uniform, joint-tube and anatomical-tube training have equal masking budgets and marginal token probabilities. An exploratory evaluation uses 304 GAVD recordings and a separate laboratory dataset of 178 participants. Relative to the own-leg correction, the uniform-trained bilateral predictor reduces normal completion error by 19.5\% on a distal-leg diagnostic and 5.2\% in the laboratory study, with a descriptive conditional 95\% interval of {[}3.6, 6.7{]}\%, and error decreases for 109 of 128 healthy participants. Anatomical versus uniform training lowers bilateral distal-task error by 7.7\%; the uniform-task advantage is uncertain. Anomaly scoring requires separate validation: local and combined scores reach AUROCs of 0.694 and 0.576 on the focal GAVD endpoint, and 0.958 and 0.892 in the laboratory study. Timing controls show that recording coverage alone does not establish prediction adequacy. The contribution is an observation-controlled method for constructing and assessing relational gait-anomaly evidence, supported by task-specific completion gains and explicit interpretation criteria.
\end{abstract}
\section{Introduction}

Gait anomaly detection must recognize unusual movement while accounting for the limits of the recording. A stable compensation can be regular and easy to predict. A normal gait can appear irregular when a joint is occluded or the camera moves. These cases require different interpretations of the same prediction error.

Relationships between the legs offer an additional source of evidence. Consider a short hidden interval of the left ankle trajectory. The visible left knee, heel and foot may reconstruct it through local geometry. The right leg may provide further information about the walking pattern. To use this relationship in anomaly detection, we need to measure what the opposite leg adds after accounting for the visible target leg, available observations and the fitted predictor.

We introduce a controlled prediction framework for this purpose. A normal-trained own-leg predictor supplies a retained baseline. Two corrections then receive either additional own-leg features or opposite-leg observations, with matched nominal input widths. Both predict the same hidden coordinates or angles. Input restrictions apply before feature construction, so a hidden value cannot return through an angle, interpolation feature or contextual embedding. A separate channel preserves projected posture, movement amplitude and raw timing.

The framework contributes an observable comparison of relational prediction, a controlled masking experiment, and an adequacy criterion for interpreting context perturbations. Uniform, joint-tube and anatomical-tube training have equal budgets and marginal token probabilities and are compared on fixed evaluation tasks. A fixed-answer timing test checks whether a predictor responds to aligned opposite-leg observations while retaining identical targets and permitted input support.

Experiments demonstrate useful completion on a focused GAVD task and independent laboratory participants. They also show why completion and anomaly scoring require separate evaluation. The method provides a reproducible basis for investigating relational gait abnormalities without treating reconstruction difficulty as a direct measure of neurological impairment.

\section{Related work and physiological motivation}

S-JEPA learns skeletal representations by predicting teacher features, while MAMP uses masked motion prediction \citep{abdelfattah2024sjepa, mao2023mamp}. Their motion-aware masking differs from our intentionally motion-independent reference. VideoMAE uses temporal tubes, and SLiM's semantic tubes already target anatomical reconstruction shortcuts \citep{tong2022videomae, do2026slim}. Our comparison controls token budgets, marginal probabilities and the downstream observation task; it does not claim those mask families are new. Stochastic positional queries are another intervention and remain separate from selecting hidden inputs \citep{bar2024stop}.

Normal-only gait methods already model posture, time and reconstruction. FSGait separates posture and temporal evidence; GenGait uses masked gait reconstruction and joint inconsistency \citep{duan2024fsgait, motta2026gengait}. SeeKer estimates conditional keypoint densities, and STG-NF learns pose likelihoods \citep{delic2025seeker, hirschorn2023stgnf}. These methods establish the value of normal-motion modeling and conditional prediction. Our framework addresses how to compare available context while keeping the observable target fixed, retaining a competent local solution and checking prediction adequacy on the evaluated observation support.

Post-stroke motor-module changes and weakness can alter sided movement and compensation \citep{clark2010modules, awad2020drive}. Parkinsonian movement size and arm coordination need not change together \citep{morris1994hypokinesia, sterling2015armswing}. Cerebral palsy combines varied motor-control and musculoskeletal constraints, while myopathic gait concerns muscle disease rather than a single brain disorder \citep{steele2015synergies, steele2012crouch, vandekerckhove2025dmd}. These findings motivate preserving amplitude, posture and timing as distinct observations. They do not establish that video predictability recovers neural synergies; mechanics and task constraints can also produce coordinated patterns \citep{kutch2012synergies}. A full movement world model would require control, mechanics, contact and observation components with multiple compatible explanations. We test only an observable completion component.

\section{Data and evaluation design}

\subsection{GAVD observation population}

We rebuild the inventory from the current condition CSVs, excluding the distribution folder. GAVD provides web recordings, sequence annotations and observed gait categories \citep{ranjan2025gavd, gavd2026catalog}. The inventory contains 1,874 sequences, 458,116 rows and 348 upload IDs. There are 332 decodable videos. Fixed pose-quality rules retain 311 sources; lower-limb readout support retains 304 sources, 1,381 sequences and 9,442 windows (Table \ref{tab:attrition}). The focal primary contrasts normal with Parkinsons, stroke, cerebral palsy and myopathic memberships: 28, 10, 15, 10 and 27 sources, respectively. The broad secondary includes every non-normal folder with unchanged scores. One source has two abnormal memberships and counts once in binary endpoints. Folder labels are not independently revalidated diagnoses; some broad categories describe circumstances rather than disease.

The GAVD analysis is exploratory because its 28 normal recordings informed protocol development. The evaluation population, endpoints, source roles, masks and score definitions were fixed before fitting the reported models. Four outer folds each reserve seven test normals and five separate reference normals; the other 16 normals fit models. Abnormal sources enter only outer evaluation, except in separately labeled supervised comparators. Three source-grouped internal folds select the small residual grid. Reference normals never select architecture, signs, stopping or fusion. Overlapping windows, sequences and edits remain inside their source role. Fits share sources across folds and are not independent replications.

\subsection{Preserved measurements and restricted inputs}

The ingestion pipeline crops each annotated person using the frame-specific GAVD box, detects BlazePose landmarks, and restores full-frame coordinates \citep{bazarevsky2020blazepose}. It returns the first pose in the crop and is not a validated temporal identity tracker. We use raw coordinates, decoded-frame flags and confidence. Values below 0.5 remain missing. Resampling to 15 Hz interpolates only between adjacent valid source observations. Windows contain 32 samples with stride 16; all four samples must be valid for a token. Windows require at least 75\% valid tokens overall and 50\% in each half.

Only indices 11--16 and 23--32 enter masking: shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees, ankles, heels and foot indices. Source readouts use the eight distal-leg joints, 25--32. The shared reference \(Q\) centers each frame on the pelvis and scales by valid first-half torso length. We do not rotate trunks upright, standardize each joint's excursion, or independently phase-align legs. Own-leg access is conditional on this shared body reference, which already contains body measurements. Projected angles and scaled image trajectories are not clinical three-dimensional angles, speed or ground clearance.

\section{Controlled prediction and masking}

Let \(O\) denote permitted visible own-leg tokens, \(C\) opposite-leg tokens, and \(Y\) the hidden four-frame XY target. Own-leg membership is hip, knee, ankle, heel and foot index. We apply masks and validity before constructing interpolation, temporal features or model inputs. Completion can use observations before and after the target; it is not future forecasting.

We fit a shared ridge baseline \(f\) on normal sources, freeze it, and compare

\begin{align}
\widehat Y_0 &= f(O;Q),\\
\widehat Y_o &= f(O;Q)+\alpha_o h(O;Q),\\
\widehat Y_b &= f(O;Q)+\alpha_b g(O,C;Q).
\end{align}

The local basis contains permitted interpolation estimates, support indicators, relative time and target identity. The own correction adds own-leg temporal moments; the bilateral correction adds opposite-leg features. The baseline has 77 features; each residual has 154 features, eight outputs and 1,240 coefficients including intercepts. Equal widths do not equalize numerical feature rank or inductive bias. Ranks count thresholded feature Gram-matrix eigenvalues, not ridge effective degrees of freedom. Recorded GAVD ranks include 111 for the own residual and 141 for the bilateral residual. A zero gate returns the saved baseline exactly, but does not guarantee held-out improvement or contain the separately fitted own correction.

Every fit uses 512 source-balanced window/mask presentations, with equal source/readout-joint loss weights. Internal validation chooses residual ridge penalty from \(\{0.1,1,10\}\) and multiplier from \(\{0,0.5,1\}\). A bilateral recipe passes only if it improves over both baseline and own correction in at least two of three internal folds and on their mean source risk. This operational rule is not a significance test. Internal selection averages source/joint-weighted requests; final scoring additionally merges repeated masks within physical window targets. We report this weighting difference. Separate generated dependency and conditional-independence fixtures, zero gates and hidden-input invariance test estimator behavior without pretending to simulate a disease.

\subsection{Equal-budget training, fixed evaluation}

A window has eight temporal patches and 16 joints, or 128 tokens. U uniformly hides 64 tokens. J uniformly selects one joint, hides its eight temporal tokens and uniformly fills 56 from the other 120. A chooses an anatomical group with probability equal to its width divided by 16. Four-joint groups are torso reference joints and either distal leg; two-joint groups are elbow and wrist on either side. Four-joint groups occupy two patches starting at 0, 2, 4 or 6; two-joint groups occupy four patches starting at 0 or 4. Starts are chosen uniformly from those listed. Each core has eight tokens. Uniformly filling 56 remaining tokens yields

\begin{equation}
P(\text{hidden})=\frac{1}{16}+\frac{15}{16}\frac{56}{120}=\frac12.
\end{equation}

All policies are motion-independent and share exact U-trained baseline weights. The coordinate target definition, nominal budget, unrelated RNG streams and source/window schedule are shared. Selected training targets and valid loss terms can differ. Matching marginals does not match higher-order dependence, difficulty or usable supervision. Masks are drawn before natural missingness, without favorable redrawing.

The primary U evaluation bank has 16 stored masks with seed 17. A hidden target must be valid and have at least two visible valid patches of the same joint. The separate E\_leg bank enumerates two distal sides and four starts twice, fills to 64 with seed 117, and scores the forced core only. Correction training and E\_leg require a valid target and at least two permitted tokens at distinct times on each leg. Every source requires four distinct physical target patches per readout. Repeated masks cannot satisfy that requirement. Each bank uses the same five reserved reference identities and its own fitted reference parameters. All 304 sources retain support on both banks. A versus U on the U bank is the primary masking contrast. E\_leg separately assesses completion when neighboring distal joints are hidden together.

\section{Scores and evaluation}

The local channel \(M\) uses 16 descriptors from full observed movement. Each leg supplies knee-angle median and interquartile range, ankle X/Y interquartile ranges and velocity median absolute deviations. Trunk inclination median/IQR and two sided differences complete the vector. We average windows within sequences and sequences within sources before normal-only imputation, robust scaling and shrinkage covariance fitting. \(M\) is squared Mahalanobis distance divided by 16. These full-observation descriptors never enter a restricted completion branch.

For identical target coordinates, define benefit

\begin{equation}
b=\operatorname{MSE}(\widehat Y_o,Y)-\operatorname{MSE}(\widehat Y_b,Y).
\end{equation}

Masks average within targets, targets within windows, windows within sequences and sequences within sources, retaining joint identity. Four joints average equally on each side. Five reserved normal references supply bilateral means \(\mu_k\) and sample variances \(s_k^2\). We use \(v_k=0.5s_k^2+0.5\operatorname{mean}(s^2)+10^{-8}\) and \(R=\frac12\sum_k(b_k-\mu_k)^2/v_k\). The frozen score is \(S=(M+R)/2\). A failed internal gate would set \(R=0\) for every source in that fold; none failed. Neither benefit nor \(R\) is mutual information or a health ranking. Dimension normalization does not make M and R equally calibrated probabilities, and R omits bilateral covariance.

Comparators retain the same sources: a richer 36-feature motion distance; base-to-own sham benefit; equally constructed constituent-error fusions; timing/waveform descriptors; Isolation Forest; availability-and-viewpoint distance; and a separately supervised nuisance classifier. A bounded STG-NF adapter uses its authors' implementation with a 16-joint graph and fitting-normal median imputation. Native complete cases retain only five normal sources and cannot support the same roles. The adapter is not a faithful published reproduction. Separate five-class Random Forest and learned/random JEPA probes use source-separated labels. Fresh encoders pretrain only on fitting normals with EMA and VICReg \citep{bardes2022vicreg}; these small probes do not establish representation-learning limits.

We summarize AUROC by within-fold values weighted by fixed normal-by-abnormal pair counts. Average precision is reported per fold and as an equal-fold mean with prevalence. Pooled out-of-fold ranks are secondary because separate references need not align score scales. Paired 1,000-replicate bootstraps resample whole recording groups within fold and binary-label stratum, retain all method scores together and preserve original fold weights. Intervals condition on the fitted models, split and selected protocol; they omit fitting uncertainty, unknown repeated-person dependence and adaptive redesign. Multiple contrasts are exploratory and are not multiplicity-adjusted.

\subsection{Fixed-answer timing diagnostic}

For a valid 48-frame parent, frames 8:40 supply the target interval. Distal opposite-leg observations come from offsets \(-4\) or \(+4\) frames, without wraparound, padding or crossing annotations. Each donor vector is relative to its permitted donor hip, divided by the original scale, and attached to the permitted opposite hip at the original time. Original and shifted inputs share target, own leg, reference and pair-specific permitted support. A hidden donor coordinate cannot reappear after shifting. Sixteen stored uniform parent masks hide 96 of 192 tokens, with seed 217, before original/donor joint-and-hip visibility is intersected.

The estimand is shifted minus aligned squared error from the frozen bilateral predictor on identical targets. Zero-offset, own-branch invariance, noise, missing-observation and whole-body crop controls accompany it. Shifting also changes instantaneous geometry and finite-interval distributions, so even a successful effect would not isolate neural timing. Adequacy is rechecked on the exact paired original inputs rather than assumed from denser training inputs.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Cross-leg prediction under distal occlusion}

The U-trained reference measures the benefit of opposite-leg observations without selecting a preferred structured training policy. On the fixed E\_leg challenge, its mean held-out normal MSE falls from 0.080769 for the own-leg augmentation to 0.065020 for the bilateral correction. This is a 19.5\% reduction as a ratio of source means. The absolute benefit is 0.015748, with conditional 95\% interval {[}0.010721, 0.021300{]}, across 28 normal recordings. These intervals use 5,000 within-fold source resamples of saved predictions.

All three policies have positive normal benefit intervals on E\_leg. On the ordinary U bank, U and A remain uncertain. The focused task hides nearby distal joints together, making local geometry less available. Its stronger benefit supports task-specific usefulness, not a claim that one task's larger loss or gain is intrinsically better. Targets remain pose estimates; artificially hiding an observed coordinate does not validate reconstruction of genuinely unobserved anatomy.

\subsection{Participant-separated laboratory evaluation}

We independently fit the same retained-prediction contrast to six simultaneous hip, knee and ankle flexion signals from a public laboratory dataset \citep{vancriekinge2023dataset}. One frozen walking trial per participant was selected before outcomes. All 188 selected trials parse; ten healthy trials fail unchanged observation support. The resulting 128 healthy and 50 stroke participants supply 433 windows. C3D angle declarations, degree units and common timestamps are verified. The first flexion component is not a global-axis rotation, and the signals are processed Plug-in Gait outputs with a shared body model.

The external representation uses 48 angle-patch tokens, half hidden, and the same gate logic. Each fold has 76 fitting healthy participants, 20 references and 32 healthy tests. Twelve angle median/IQR descriptors replace the image-specific M features. No limbs are independently cycle-aligned, no laboratory markers are relabeled as BlazePose joints, and no video weights are transferred.

All four internal gates pass. Bilateral prediction improves held-out healthy error over the own-capacity control by 4.78\% to 5.52\% across folds. A descriptive comparison on the same held-out normal targets gives mean participant MSEs of 309.838 for direct interpolation, 206.262 for f, 201.038 for f+h and 190.584 for f+g, in squared degrees. The same ordering holds in every fold. Equal residual width still does not equalize rank: the 72-feature own and bilateral bases have ranks 44 and 62. The observed improvement applies to these fitted predictor families; it does not isolate opposite-leg information from every difference in effective flexibility.

The aggregate own-to-both reduction is 10.454 squared degrees, conditional interval {[}7.154, 13.387{]}, or 5.2\%, {[}3.6, 6.7{]}\%. The bilateral model gives lower prediction error for 109 of 128 held-out healthy participants. Every fold has a positive mean reduction. Completion intervals use 1,000 paired participant resamples within the fixed folds. They are descriptive intervals calculated after inspection of the fitted results and exclude training and analysis-selection uncertainty.

\subsection{Effect of training-mask structure}

A paired comparison evaluates A against U while retaining exactly the same E\_leg target keys, mask draws, support and U-trained baseline. Normal bilateral MSE falls from 0.065020 to 0.060009, a 7.7\% reduction, conditional interval {[}6.4, 9.0{]}\%. All four fold means improve and 27 of 28 sources improve. E\_leg resembles part of A's training design, so this is evidence of task alignment rather than universal mask superiority.

The unchanged U bank provides the necessary boundary: A's reduction is only 0.8\%, interval {[}-1.0, 2.6{]}\%, with improvement in 16 of 28 sources and a worse mean in one fold. We therefore do not call A generally superior. Both banks were defined before fitting. The paired raw-error intervals are descriptive analyses computed after inspecting the fitted results, with no change to models or evaluation inputs.

\subsection{Prediction adequacy under timing edits}

The timing census retains 27 normal recordings, with 7, 7, 6 and 7 outer-test normals. It passes the operational coverage floor. However, all 24 outer fold/policy/offset combinations fail adequacy on their exact paired original inputs, as do all 72 corresponding internal combinations. Only about 1.75 editable distal tokens remain on average. U's minus-four-frame offset has a positive raw effect of 0.000393, interval {[}0.000177, 0.000618{]}; the plus-four-frame offset gives 0.000064, interval {[}-0.000232, 0.000368{]}. The minus-four-frame interval excludes zero, but compares two predictions from a model that is inadequate on the aligned paired inputs. It therefore does not establish a reliable coordination measurement (Figure \ref{fig:timing}). Software identity and visibility tests pass, but do not repair insufficient useful context.

\section{Measurement reliability and limitations}

We inspect prespecified high/low prediction-error examples in each focal label, with local videos, pose overlays and first/middle/last annotated frames. The ten cases show camera motion, assistance, split-screen footage, limb overlap and feet leaving the image. Figure \ref{fig:cases} illustrates measured skeleton traces without redistributing source photographs. Three sampled frames do not establish continuous identity correctness, and visible descriptions are not clinical relabeling.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/failure_cases.pdf}
\caption{Measured high-error normal and low-error Parkinsons-labeled examples. Sparse image traces are observation examples, not evidence that prediction error orders neurological severity. Source labels are retained without clinical reinterpretation.}
\label{fig:cases}
\end{figure}

A descriptive image-boundary check finds high-confidence distal points outside decoded images in frozen-window spans from 13/28 normal and 83/276 abnormal recordings. Pooled point fractions are approximately 0.43\% and 0.24\%; one requested frame fails decoding. Confidence therefore does not guarantee visible measurement, and the higher normal fraction cautions against attributing artifacts only to abnormal videos. No fits or exclusions change after this audit. Frame-aligned single-view strata lack enough normal support for a complete four-fold matched analysis; mixed views are not a matched viewpoint. Neither a global nuisance score nor the small camera review resolves these alternatives.

Five GAVD reference sources produce uncertain benefit scales. Saved leave-one-reference-out and variance-floor analyses expose that uncertainty. M and R also have different tails despite dimension normalization. In a descriptive check of the fitted score distributions, external test-normal R 99th percentiles range from 6.30 to 33.94 across folds, versus 2.45 to 3.99 for M. External reference left/right benefit correlations range from 0.54 to 0.82, which the diagonal R distance does not model. These observations do not identify the cause of fusion loss. The observed score-integration behavior may reflect calibration, reference estimation or representation choice as well as limited incremental movement information. We do not tune fusion after observing abnormal results. A new normal-only calibration study would require a separately frozen protocol and fresh evaluation.

The laboratory replication addresses participant separation and changes the observation system, but covers only healthy and stroke walking, excludes ten selected healthy trials and does not validate clinical deployment or video-weight transfer. GAVD remains an internal web-recording study with a small, development-informed normal sample. More abnormal windows do not replace independent normal participants. Conditional intervals omit training uncertainty, and limited ridge/JEPA budgets do not establish the best attainable model. The imputed STG-NF adapter is an informative bounded comparison, not a state-of-the-art benchmark.

\section{Conclusion}

Controlled cross-leg prediction makes relational gait evidence testable through retained local predictions, common observable targets and restricted inputs. Matched masking exposes task-specific completion gains, while participant-separated laboratory measurements confirm improved completion under the declared predictor families.

Using these relationships for anomaly detection requires adequate prediction on the actual available observations and separate validation of score calibration and combination. The framework makes these requirements explicit while preserving posture and movement amplitude as informative evidence.
