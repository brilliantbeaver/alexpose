The predeclared excess-error score also has a favorable ab-
normality association, while raw error ranks in the opposite
direction. Subtracting matched persistence error accounts for
a basic component of observed motion difficulty, but does not
isolate disease or neurological mechanism. Predictive depen-
dence can arise from mechanics, speed, camera geometry,
compensation, and correlated pose noise. The model has no
treatment input, longitudinal disease state, clinical reward,
or intervention outcome.
Generalization is limited by public-video selection, un-
certain participant overlap, annotation validity, acquisition
differences, and quality-dependent attrition. Source group-
ing addresses repeated windows from a video, but cannot
establish patient-disjoint or external-site evaluation. Pooled
out-of-fold AUROC also assumes comparable score scales
across separately fitted fold models; cross-fold offsets re-
main a limitation. Small normal-source support limits model
selection and uncertainty estimates. GAVD alone therefore
supports an exploratory observational benchmark, not de-
ployment claims. The highest-value extension is an exter-
nal cohort with verified identities, clinician assessments, re-
peated visits, and measured interventions.
Bilateral history improved normal-source forecasting over
persistence in this exploratory evaluation. The complete set
of primary comparisons does not establish a timing-specific
bilateral forecasting advantage. The reusable contribution
is a complete evidence chain from real video inference
to controlled source-level estimates. It makes forecast util-
ity, timing-specific information, and abnormality association
separately testable, which is necessary before a gait obser-
vation component can support stronger medical modeling
claims.
