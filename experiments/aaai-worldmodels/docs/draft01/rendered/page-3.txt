All methods use a common observed target support. Each
leg must have at least four observed joint-time points in
the first four future samples and eight over the full future;
each target joint must also have a prefix observation. Co-
ordinate errors are averaged within leg and window, then
legs equally, windows within sequence, sequences within
source, and sources equally. Training weights follow the same
source/sequence/window hierarchy with adjustment for ob-
served target counts. This prevents long videos or denser
pose support from determining the endpoint.
The primary endpoint is normal-source MSE averaged
over all eight future observations, nominally 0.1 to 0.8 sec-
onds, rather than only the final instant. Paired differences
compare bilateral prediction with persistence, own history,
shifted history, and static posture; positive values favor bi-
lateral prediction. Four-step results and shrinkage are sec-
ondary. We use 5,000 source resamples for error intervals
and 2,000 stratified source resamples for secondary AUROC
and average precision. These descriptive intervals condition
on fixed fits with overlapping training folds. They exclude
retraining and patient-population uncertainty and are not
multiplicity-adjusted confirmatory tests. Earlier GAVD out-
comes were exposed, so the new cross-fitting study is ex-
ploratory.

Results
Bilateral history improved normal-source forecasting over
persistence in this exploratory evaluation. Persistence and bi-
lateral normal-source MSE are 0.12126 and 0.08063. Their
paired difference is 0.04063 [0.02399, 0.05911], equivalent
to 33.5% of the persistence mean. Bilateral error is lower
on 23 of 29 normal sources. The gain over own history
is 0.00111 [-0.00253, 0.00524], over the separately trained
timing control 0.00591 [0.00141, 0.01134], and over static
posture -0.00900 [-0.01589, -0.00207]. The complete set of
primary comparisons does not establish a timing-specific bi-
lateral forecasting advantage.

Discussion and limitations
The positive forecasting gain shows usable information in this
observation contract. Correctly timed bilateral context also
outperforms its matched shifted control. However, the small
uncertain gain over own history and the stronger current-
posture-conditioned predictor prevent a broader claim of bi-
lateral temporal superiority. The static-context model learns
horizon-specific transitions from the current state; its per-
formance is a useful positive reference, not evidence that
the output is motionless. These findings favor testing a com-
pact current-state transition model before increasing tempo-
ral complexity.
