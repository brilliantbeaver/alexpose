Abstract
Medical world models need observation dynamics that re-
main useful under simple controls. We test whether correctly
timed opposite-leg history improves short-horizon gait fore-
casts beyond a leg’s own history. Full-duration processing of
selected GAVD videos yields 2,815 eligible windows from
456 sequences and 91 source videos, including 29 normal
sources. Normal-only ridge predictors forecast eight obser-
vations at a nominal 10 Hz using nested source-grouped
cross-validation. The primary bilateral forecast reduces mean
squared error by 33.5% relative to persistence, with paired dif-
ference 0.04063 [0.02399, 0.05911]. The corresponding own-
history and shifted-context differences are 0.00111 [-0.00253,
0.00524] and 0.00591 [0.00141, 0.01134]. A current-posture-
conditioned regression is stronger still, with MSE 0.07164
versus bilateral 0.08063. Bilateral history improved normal-
source forecasting over persistence in this exploratory eval-
uation. The complete set of primary comparisons does not
establish a timing-specific bilateral forecasting advantage. A
predeclared movement-referenced excess-error score reaches
abnormality AUROC 0.793, a secondary association. Inter-
vals resample sources conditional on fixed cross-validation
fits. The study is exploratory because earlier GAVD outcomes
were known. The contribution is a controlled evaluation of
a motor-observation layer, without claims about treatment,
disease progression, or neurological mechanism.
Introduction
Movement supplies an observable aspect of patient state. A
gait observation model could eventually support longitudi-
nal assessment, but low prediction error alone does not show
that a system has learned clinically meaningful dynamics.
Persistence may already predict a short future reasonably
well, while appearance, movement amplitude, and recording
conditions may distinguish dataset labels. We therefore sep-
arate useful forecasting, timing-sensitive information, and
abnormality ranking.
Neurological evidence motivates a relational question
without fixing its answer. Laboratory gait exhibits struc-
tured intersegmental covariation (Borghese, Bianchi, and
Lacquaniti 1996), and bilateral phase coordination is affected
in Parkinson’s disease (Plotnik, Giladi, and Hausdorff 2007).
Studies of stroke and cerebral palsy relate altered EMG mod-
ular organization to impaired movement (Clark et al. 2010;
Steele, Rozumalski, and Schwartz 2015). These measure-
ments differ from projected video coordinates. A predictable
compensation can still be abnormal, and no video forecast
score identifies a neural module or lesion.
We ask whether adding the opposite leg’s correctly timed
history improves prediction beyond the target leg’s own
history. Our contribution is a fixed-time, fuller-sequence,
source-grouped audit with matched timing controls and ex-
plicit negative-result conditions. Bilateral gait analysis and
normal-only prediction are established ideas. We aim to de-
termine what the available observations actually support be-
fore introducing a more complex medical model.
Related work
Skeleton S-JEPA learns action representations through la-
tent masked-observation prediction (Abdelfattah and Alahi
2024). This is distinct from a strict future-only task. MoCo-
DAD generates future skeletons for anomaly detection (Fla-
borea et al. 2023); GenGait reconstructs normative gait
(Motta et al. 2026); BioGait-VLM combines visual, lan-
guage, and biomechanical evidence (Chen et al. 2026).
These precedents preclude broad first-method claims. Strong
static baselines are particularly relevant because low motion-
prediction error need not require a learned motion model
(Martinez, Black, and Romero 2017).
Data and observation contract
The pinned local GAVD derivative contains 1,874 annota-
tion sequences and 348 source IDs. The requested normal,
Parkinson’s, stroke, cerebral palsy, and myopathic labels con-
tain 666 sequences from 103 sources (Ranjan et al. 2025).
Media checks retain 652 sequences from 98 sources. Two pre-
viously reviewed sources with person switches or scene cuts
are excluded, leaving 641 sequences from 96 sources for ex-
traction. Timing, person, and observation gates retain 2,818
windows from 456 sequences and 91 sources. The common
distal-target gate removes 3 windows, retaining 2,815 win-
dows from 456 sequences and 91 sources. These are video
groups, not verified independent people.
