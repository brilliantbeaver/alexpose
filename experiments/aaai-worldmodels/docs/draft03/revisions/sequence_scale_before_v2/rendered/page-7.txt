pre-score audit found uncertain person continuity when an
assistant overlaps one Parkinson’s participant. The fixed ex-

Nuisance checks constrain interpretation
Matching within fold and annotated view on prefix movement
and missingness retains 40 of 91 sources. Thirty-eight enter
four folds with adequate matched class support. The paired
candidate-minus-low-motion difference is -0.409 [-0.651, -
0.161]. There is no distance caliper, so matching does not
guarantee similar observations; pair distances and coverage
loss remain visible. Most sources have multiple views, and
single-view strata are small. These analyses cannot establish
camera invariance or remove unmeasured recording differ-
ences.
External native frame rates are associated with labels even
after sampling at 10 Hz. Age, reflective markers, clothing,
scene, and full-image person selection also differ. Some pre-
fix covariates leave the normal training range, and finite cal-
ibration tails can saturate. External ranking therefore mea-
sures transfer under documented observation shift rather than
isolated disease physiology. A source-identification diagnos-
tic on the normal training pool likewise supplies no evidence
that learned features remove recording identity relative to
random features.
Discussion and scope
The positive forecasting result identifies a compact, repro-
ducible capability: a normal-only model predicts relative leg
articulation better than repeating the last pose. The strongest
tested reference uses current posture rather than a long his-
tory. The benchmark also establishes why forecasting and
abnormality ranking need separate validation. Neither a
forecast-error reduction nor a favorable excess-error AUROC
establishes an incremental anomaly detector.
Several explanations remain for the conditional score’s
performance. Movement amount may carry annotation infor-
mation that the nuisance regression discounts. The normal
cohort may be too small for a stable conditional reference.
Disjoint training and calibration roles reduce fitting support,
while coarse tails lose ranking detail. Out-of-fit and refitted
forecasting models can also differ in error scale. The study
does not identify which explanation dominates or rule out
better conditional models with stronger independent normal
coverage.
The masking finding is similarly bounded to one compact
coordinate model and this sparse observation task. Anatom-
ical or latent masking may help with other representations
or larger populations. Here the policies do not earn an ad-
vantage over a matched uniform control. A useful next study
should add independent normal coverage and validated ob-
servations that resolve these ambiguities before increasing
model capacity.
GAVD supports a heterogeneous public-video benchmark,
but GAVD alone does not establish patient-disjoint, site-
disjoint, or prospective clinical generalization. Source IDs
are not patient IDs, and folder labels are not independently
confirmed diagnoses in this analysis. The external dataset
adds participant-coded transfer evidence, but diagnoses and
cross-dataset participant independence were not indepen-
dently reverified. Byte hashes exclude identical files rather
than reencoded copies or repeated people. Quality-dependent
retention also changes the evaluated population.
For medical world models, the contribution is a tested
movement-observation benchmark with useful forecasting
and explicit anomaly controls. Short gait forecasts do not
establish disease progression, treatment response, or action-
conditioned patient simulation. Verified independent par-
ticipants, comparable acquisition, and clinically grounded
movement measurements are needed before a claimed orga-
nizational effect can support stronger medical use.
