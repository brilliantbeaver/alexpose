A sequence is a meaningful movement observation, while
an original recording can contribute several sequences. We
preserve GAVD’s intended single-direction boundaries and
retain each unique sequence identifier. The annotation audit
finds 17 pairs of retained sequence IDs that share origi-
nal recording frames. Shared imagery can include different
people, and sequence identifiers alone do not establish par-
ticipant identity or disjoint footage. A relation table links
verified duplicates and known relationships, and all linked
observations stay together during evaluation. Unknown re-
peated people across recordings remain possible.
For transfer, we use the separately released KOA-PD-NM
video dataset (Kour, Gupta, and Arora 2020, 2022). The local
cache contains 189 originals from 95 dataset-coded partic-
ipants. Byte-identical copies in another folder are counted
once. Two files cannot be decoded. Processing all 187 de-
codable recordings with the fixed gates retains 773 windows
from 168 recordings and 90 coded participants: 27 normal,
49 osteoarthritis, and 14 Parkinson’s. All recordings from one
coded participant stay together. We report these counts sepa-
rately because original recordings and coded participants are
different grouping units; their names alone do not establish
independent people.
What the model observes
A pose is an estimated arrangement of body joints in an
image. We use Ambient’s shared RGB-image extraction in-
terface with a pinned BlazePose weight file. Sixteen positions
describe the shoulders, arms, hips, knees, ankles, heels, and
foot indices, which are landmarks near the fronts of the feet.
We subtract the midpoint between the hips, our pelvis refer-
ence, in each frame and divide by a torso scale estimated from
past observations only. The scale is the median valid distance
between the hip midpoint and shoulder midpoint in the prefix.
These coordinates describe relative two-dimensional articu-
lation in prefix-torso units. They are not physical walking
speed or measured three-dimensional joint angles. Both hips
enter the shared reference, including in an own-leg model.

Compact models and controlled masking
References before larger models
Persistence repeats each target joint’s latest observed po-
sition. It is both a short-horizon forecasting reference and
a measure of how far the future moves from that anchor.
A damped-velocity reference extrapolates half the latest
observed rate of change using actual time gaps. Learned
ridge regressions predict displacement from the same an-
chor. Ridge regularization limits coefficient magnitude in
the small training population. We compare own-leg history,
bilateral history, current bilateral posture, and a separately
fitted opposite-leg timing control. The timing control applies
a window-specific shift while retaining the anchor. It changes
alignment rather than simply renaming feature columns.
