Separation is costly when normal support is small. Each
calibration set has seven or eight recordings, and its forecaster
uses fifteen or sixteen. Smaller out-of-fit models supply the
nuisance regression’s training predictions, which may change
error scale. Empirical ranks are coarse and have no automatic
conformal coverage guarantee. We therefore report ranking
rather than a clinical operating threshold. Repeated windows
cannot increase independent calibration support.
Evaluation and frozen transfer
All sequences and windows from an original-recording re-
lation group stay in one outer fold. Five outer folds retain
the earlier recording partition; three inner normal-recording
folds choose ridge strength and training duration. Selection,
scaling, masks, and calibration do not use abnormal train-
ing labels. Nevertheless, earlier GAVD outcomes informed
the study, so every internal result remains exploratory. A
new method name or split cannot make reused recordings
untouched.
Errors average observed coordinate discrepancies within
leg, give legs equal window weight, average windows within
each gait sequence, and then average sequences within each
original recording. The primary mean gives recording groups
equal weight. A separately identified sensitivity gives se-
quences equal weight while retaining whole-recording re-
sampling. Training samples recording, sequence, and win-
dow hierarchically so long videos do not dominate. Mask
policies share the model, objective, windows, targets, three
seeds, and normal-selected duration. Neural predictions are
averaged across seeds before computing error. Seeds measure
optimization sensitivity rather than additional subjects.
The primary anomaly endpoint is the paired within-fold
AUROC difference between the conditional two-sided can-
didate and low motion. AUROC is the probability that an ab-
normal group ranks above a normal group, with half credit for
ties. Fold AUROCs are weighted by their normal-abnormal
pair counts. We also report equal-fold and pooled rankings.
Average precision summarizes precision across recall levels
and depends on abnormal prevalence. Neither metric alone
is clinical diagnostic accuracy. Secondary controls include
normal-only posture distance, motion/quality distance, prefix
movement, missingness, marginal calibration, and matched
posture calibration.
Primary 95% intervals use 5,000 paired bootstrap resam-
ples of groups within fold and binary label; the resampled
groups are original recordings, not individual sequences. Pri-
mary forecast intervals resample the 29 normal recording
components, which contain 141 sequences. These intervals
condition on saved overlapping fits and omit unknown par-
ticipant links and complete pipeline retraining variation. Two
additional grouped partitions repeat posture and posture-
plus-velocity fits as correlated sensitivities. Mask compar-
isons are exploratory; we make no confirmatory significance
claims across the family.
External transfer freezes all weights, transforms, nuisance
regressions, directions, and normal references. For each of
fifteen calibration components, we average windows within
recording and recordings within coded participant. Prefix co-
variates follow the same hierarchy. We then apply the com-
ponent’s normal-reference transform and average its partic-
ipant tail scores. Aggregation must precede this nonlinear
calibration. The change from recording videos to repeated
participant recordings may alter score variance. No exter-
nal participant fits or calibrates the model. Paired external
intervals use 5,000 participant bootstrap draws.
Results
Current posture supplies useful short-horizon
prediction
Across 141 normal gait sequences, with original record-
ings given equal weight, the current-posture transition has
MSE 0.07174, compared with 0.12126 for persistence (Table
2). Its paired absolute improvement is 0.04952, with condi-
tional 95% interval [0.03447, 0.06628], a relative reduction
of 40.8%. This establishes useful prediction relative to the
tested reference. It does not establish that the model has re-
covered a physiological state or needs the detailed order of
past movement.
The forecasting advantage also persists when each nor-
mal gait sequence receives equal weight. In this separately
declared post hoc sensitivity, MSE changes from 0.13850
to 0.08084, a 41.6% reduction. The paired improvement is
0.05766 with conditional 95% interval [0.04178, 0.07967].
The bootstrap carries all sequences from each selected
recording together within the original folds. This changes
the weighting of the same observations while retaining 29
normal recording groups; the original primary endpoint re-
mains unchanged.
Adding velocity and its support summaries gives MSE
0.07693. Its paired improvement over posture is -0.00519
[-0.01022, -0.00046], so this state does not earn its extra
information here. Bilateral history also improves on persis-
tence, but current posture is stronger. The bilateral advantage
over own-leg history has an interval spanning zero. Both al-
ternate partitions retain the direction of the posture compari-
son. These results make current posture a necessary reference
for any claim about learned temporal organization.
A model might improve on persistence merely by predict-
ing an average normal pose at each future query. To check that
simpler explanation, a separately declared post hoc diagnos-
tic adds two normal-training intercept priors, without tuning:
a mean pose for each future query and an anchor plus mean
displacement. They have MSE 0.10898 and 0.12235, respec-
tively. Posture improves on the mean-pose prior by 0.03724
[0.02539, 0.05150]. This supports the value of current po-
sitions beyond a constant prior within an outcome-exposed
exploratory comparison.
