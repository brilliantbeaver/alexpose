Structured masks do not improve the fixed task
Uniform masking gives MSE 0.07761, compared with
0.07962 for anatomical tubes and 0.07916 for motion bal-
ancing (the masking comparison). With improvement de-
fined as uniform error minus candidate error, the differences
are -0.00201 [-0.00426, -0.00002] and -0.00155 [-0.00311,
-0.00013]. These exploratory comparisons do not support a
benefit. The tube advantage over geometry-matched random
groups is 0.00042 [-0.00086, 0.00173], which also does not
establish an anatomical advantage. Every seed and policy
remains in the supplementary registry.
Shared prefix dropout, small coordinate noise, and
opposite-leg time shifts do not reverse the tube or motion-
balanced comparison with uniform masking. These pertur-
bations test observation dependence rather than neurologi-
cal interventions. Phase, learned-mask, latent-objective, and
stochastic-future extensions remain deferred under the de-
clared evidence gates. A broader search after these outcomes
would be a new study.
Forecast skill and anomaly ranking separate
Figure 3 focuses on one question: does using a learned fore-
cast improve abnormality ranking beyond measuring move-
ment alone? The movement-only control assigns a higher
score when the observed future contains less leg movement.
The forecast-based candidate assigns a higher score when
prediction is unusually easy or difficult relative to normal
walking, after allowing for past movement and missing joints.
Both scores require the future to be observed; neither is a
prefix-only early warning.
The forecast-based score has within-fold AUROC 0.462,
compared with 0.811 for movement alone. Their paired dif-
ference is -0.348 [-0.506, -0.191]. Matched posture calibra-
tion also performs weakly, and its paired comparison with
the larger state does not establish an advantage. Thus the
present normal reference does not rank annotation-defined
departures better than the declared movement comparator.

The bilateral excess score has pooled AUROC 0.793, while
low motion has 0.806. Their within-fold rankings differ be-
cause those compare only recordings evaluated by the same
fold model. The favorable excess-error association remains
compatible with a substantial contribution from movement
amount. This does not make low motion meaningless: re-
stricted movement can itself matter clinically. It shows that
the association alone does not demonstrate additional knowl-
edge of movement organization.
