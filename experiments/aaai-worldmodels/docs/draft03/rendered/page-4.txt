The new compact state contains current posture, a robust
recent velocity estimate, and uncertainty-related summaries
of that estimate. Velocity is the median of valid adjacent dif-
ferences over the latest five prefix intervals. Median absolute
deviation and valid-pair counts describe its variability and
support. This arm therefore adds both movement and obser-
vation information; its effect cannot be attributed to velocity
alone. A matched current-posture ridge is the mandatory sim-
ple alternative. It learns a mapping from the current joint po-
sitions to future positions, whereas persistence copies those
positions unchanged. An untrained random encoder with a
fitted coordinate head checks whether a trained nonlinear
representation is needed.
For masking, one multilayer perceptron has two hidden
layers of width 32 and a residual coordinate head, with 31,936
parameters. It receives visible prefix coordinates, visibility
indicators, latest observed anchors, and prefix times. Hidden
values are filled only after masking. The objective is future-
coordinate squared error with equal leg weighting. A learned
representation here is the numerical summary inside this
predictor. This experiment uses coordinates as targets rather
than a teacher encoder or latent-target objective.
A fixed task with different hidden past observations
Natural missingness, deliberate training masking, and future
queries are stored separately. The entire future stays hidden
in every arm. Additional prefix masks hide 40% of eligible
non-protected tokens, with deterministic rounding; a token
is one joint at one time. All observed reference joints and
each joint’s latest observed point are protected, including the
target anchors. The unmasked prefix baseline retains every
observed prefix token and therefore has more visible input.
It provides a full-input reference for the six budget-matched
corruption policies.
Uniform masking samples eligible tokens without replace-
ment. Time blocks prioritize contiguous intervals. Anatomi-
cal tubes prioritize connected limb groups over time. Random
joint groups match the anatomical policy’s group sizes, in-
tervals, and realized token counts. Motion-balanced masking
distributes the budget across low- and high-motion prefix to-
kens, using confidence-qualified differences and actual time
gaps. A motion-focused comparator favors higher-motion to-
kens. Both retain a uniform sampling component, so every
eligible region can be selected. Missing motion is not zero
motion, and ties have a deterministic rule.
Missing observations can prevent a complete block or tube
from reaching the exact budget. The frozen implementation
then truncates or completes its priority list. These are there-
fore budget-matched block and tube priorities rather than in-
variably complete tubes. Four fixed mask banks per window
make exposure auditable. A mask-pattern-only diagnostic re-
moves residual coordinate inputs to test the information in
the chosen locations; the shared persistence anchor is still
added at the output. Main evaluation uses the full observed
prefix for every policy, with shared corruptions reserved for
robustness.
From forecast error to an anomaly score
Why a forecast needs an explicit reference
Mean squared error, or MSE, averages squared differences
between predicted and observed coordinates. Lower error
means a closer forecast, in squared prefix-torso units. We
compare the learned forecast and persistence on exactly the
same future. Historical excess error is learned MSE minus
persistence MSE. A negative value favors the learned fore-
cast, while a higher value has the declared abnormality direc-
tion. This measures forecast gain but does not automatically
remove movement amount.
For one observed coordinate vector, let a be the latest pose,
y the future, and d the model’s predicted displacement from
a. The difference in squared errors is:
Ef −Ep = ∥d∥2 −2⟨d, y −a⟩.
(1)
Here E_f and E_p are learned and persistence errors.
The inner product measures alignment between predicted
displacement and observed displacement y minus a. The
identity shows that excess error can vary when movement
amount changes even if the predicted displacement stays
fixed. Common-weight averaging preserves that dependence.
We therefore retain negative persistence error as a low-
motion comparator: it assigns greater abnormality to less
displacement from the anchor. This diagnostic direction was
discovered in earlier outcome inspection and locked as a
baseline for the continuation. It does not gain preregistered
historical status.
A normal reference for movement difficulty
The prespecified candidate uses the posture-plus-velocity
ridge forecaster and asks whether a recording is easier or
harder to predict than expected from its past. First, we av-
erage future MSE within recording. Prefix movement uses
valid adjacent distal-joint differences divided by actual time
gaps. We also retain prefix missingness. A small ridge regres-
sion g predicts log forecast MSE from log prefix movement
and missingness using out-of-fit normal development pre-
dictions. Each such prediction belongs to a normal recording
excluded from the model that produced it. Subtracting this
expected log error gives a scalar residual. Separate normal
recordings define how unusual the residual is:
rs = log(Ef,s + ϵ) −g(zs),
As = |2Fcal(rs) −1|. (2)
The index s denotes a recording, E_f,s its hierarchical
forecast MSE, and epsilon the numerical floor 0.00000001.
The two values in z_s are log prefix movement and miss-
ingness. The function F_cal is the smoothed empirical cu-
mulative distribution of residuals from normal calibration
recordings. It indicates where a residual falls in that refer-
ence. The absolute-value transformation assigns a high score
A_s to unusually easy or unusually difficult prediction. This
scalar residual measures forecast difficulty; it is not a signed
coordinate residual or the historical excess score.
