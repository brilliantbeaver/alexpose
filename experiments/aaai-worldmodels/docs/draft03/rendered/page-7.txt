Nuisance checks constrain interpretation
Matching within fold and annotated view on prefix move-
ment and missingness retains 40 of 91 recordings. Thirty-
eight enter four folds with adequate matched class support.
The paired candidate-minus-low-motion difference is -0.409
[-0.651, -0.161]. There is no distance caliper, so matching
does not guarantee similar observations; pair distances and
coverage loss remain visible. Most recordings have multi-
ple views, and single-view strata are small. These analyses
cannot establish camera invariance or remove unmeasured
recording differences.
External native frame rates are associated with labels even
after sampling at 10 Hz. Age, reflective markers, clothing,
scene, and full-image person selection also differ. Some pre-
fix covariates leave the normal training range, and finite cal-
ibration tails can saturate. External ranking therefore mea-
sures transfer under documented observation shift rather than
isolated disease physiology. A recording-identification diag-
nostic on the normal training pool likewise supplies no evi-
dence that learned features remove recording identity relative
to random features.
Discussion and scope
The evaluation across hundreds of clinician-annotated walk-
ing sequences identifies a compact, reproducible capability:
a normal-only model predicts relative leg articulation bet-
ter than repeating the last pose. The strongest tested refer-
ence uses current posture rather than a long history. The
benchmark also establishes why forecasting and abnormality
ranking need separate validation. Neither a forecast-error re-
duction nor a favorable excess-error AUROC establishes an
incremental anomaly detector.
Several explanations remain for the conditional score’s
performance. Movement amount may carry annotation in-
formation that the nuisance regression discounts. Although
the cohort contains many gait sequences, the available 29 nor-
mal recording groups may be too few for a stable conditional
reference. Disjoint training and calibration roles reduce fit-
ting support, while coarse tails lose ranking detail. Out-of-fit
and refitted forecasting models can also differ in error scale.
The study does not identify which explanation dominates or
rule out better conditional models with stronger independent
normal coverage.
The masking finding is similarly bounded to one compact
coordinate model and this sparse observation task. Anatom-
ical or latent masking may help with other representations
or larger populations. Here the policies do not earn an ad-
vantage over a matched uniform control. A useful next study
should add independent normal coverage and validated ob-
servations that resolve these ambiguities before increasing
model capacity.
