All methods share observed future targets. We average co-
ordinate errors within a leg and window, the two legs equally,
then windows within sequences, sequences within sources,
and sources equally. Training weights follow this hierarchy.
Forecast evaluation uses 29 normal sources. Anomaly rank-
ing compares 62 abnormal and 29 normal sources. AUROC
is the probability that an abnormal source receives a higher
score than a normal source; average precision also depends
on prevalence.
The frozen analysis uses 5,000 source resamples for
forecast intervals and 2,000 stratified source resamples for
anomaly intervals. These intervals condition on fixed models
with overlapping training folds. They exclude retraining and
participant-population uncertainty. The study is exploratory
because GAVD outcomes were available during research de-
velopment.
This report adds an explicitly post hoc low-movement di-
agnostic: negative persistence error. It assigns larger scores
to smaller observed movements without changing any frozen
score direction. We compare it with excess error using
5,000 paired stratified source resamples. Within-fold rank-
ings avoid comparing scores from different fitted models.
These added analyses do not select a model, threshold, or
clinical rule.
Results
Normal-source mean squared error is 0.12126 for persistence
and 0.08063 for bilateral forecasting. The paired reduction
is 0.04063 [0.02399, 0.05911], or 33.5% of the persistence
mean. Bilateral forecasting improves error on 23 of 29 normal
sources. This establishes useful forecast information within
the observation contract.
The gain over own-leg history is 0.00111 [-0.00253,
0.00524], and the gain over the trained timing control is
0.00591 [0.00141, 0.01134]. However, current posture fore-
casts better, with error 0.07164, a 40.9% reduction from
persistence. Longer bilateral context is therefore not estab-
lished as necessary. Shrinkage yields bilateral error 0.08378
and does not improve the unshrunk model.
Prespecified excess error achieves AUROC 0.793 [0.680,
0.896] and average precision 0.865 [0.788, 0.938]. Raw fore-
cast error has AUROC 0.304 [0.178, 0.431], and persistence
error has AUROC 0.194 [0.096, 0.300]. Abnormal-source
prevalence is 0.681. These differences show that an error-
based ranking depends strongly on its reference and inter-
pretation.
The post hoc low-movement diagnostic has AUROC
0.806. The paired difference, excess error minus low move-
ment, is -0.013 [-0.111, 0.083]. This does not establish added
anomaly-ranking value from the learned forecast. Excess-
error AUROC within individual folds ranges from 0.653 to
0.931. Weighting those rankings by the number of normal-
abnormal source pairs gives 0.774, compared with 0.811 for
low movement. Their paired difference is -0.036 [-0.145,
0.067]. The association persists without cross-fold pairs,
while small source groups produce substantial variation.

Discussion and limitations
The study supplies a useful observation-model benchmark
and a concrete lesson for gait anomaly detection. A model
can forecast normal movement well while its raw error ranks
restricted abnormal movement as easier. Comparing fore-
casts with persistence exposes this distinction. Evaluating
persistence as an anomaly diagnostic then tests whether the
apparent benefit comes from learned behavior or movement
amount. This sequence of checks is the methodological con-
tribution.
The next model should demonstrate added information be-
yond current posture and movement-only scores. Promising
tests include normal-only calibration conditional on prefix
motion and view, and structured masks that hide a leg or a
continuous time interval. Such masks could encourage use
of coordination rather than nearby redundant samples. These
are proposed extensions that require matched budgets, fixed
targets, timing controls, and an untouched evaluation cohort.
GAVD supports exploratory evaluation across heteroge-
neous public-video sources, but does not establish clinical
generalizability. Participants may recur across uploads, a
video may contain several people, and local annotations do
not independently verify diagnoses. View, age, walking task,
assistive devices, acquisition quality, and unequal retention
can influence scores. More overlapping windows cannot re-
solve these limitations or enlarge the 29 retained normal-
source groups.
The model describes short-term articulation without treat-
ment inputs, repeated clinical visits, or measured inter-
vention outcomes. Patient-level decisions require external
participant-disjoint data and validated clinical measure-
ments. Within this scope, the benchmark separates forecast-
ing skill from anomaly ranking and defines clear tests for
stronger medical claims.
