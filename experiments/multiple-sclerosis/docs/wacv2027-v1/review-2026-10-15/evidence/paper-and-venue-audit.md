# Original-paper and venue review

Reviewed on 15 October 2026. The target is `docs/wacv2027-v1/wacv-v1.pdf`, titled *Evaluating Predictive Skeleton Learning for Video-Based Neurological Gait Analysis*. All nine PDF pages were read, and all pages, tables, and figures were inspected as rendered images. Page numbers below refer to that PDF, not subsequent drafts. This review supplies editorial and literature findings; the accompanying notebook and results audits establish the experimental facts and replacement numbers.

## Main editorial judgment

The paper has a useful workshop contribution: it tests whether features learned from a small collection of estimated skeleton sequences can support classification of previously unseen video sources, using a simple classifier after encoder training. Its strongest defensible account is an empirical comparison with the particular handcrafted angle-feature random forest that was evaluated. The current draft devotes disproportionate space to training diagnostics and the vocabulary of uncertainty, while explaining the scientific contribution only briefly.

Reorganize the narrative around the trained representation, the small training collection, and the held-out comparison. Preserve the mean-pose result and the distinction between collection labels and verified diagnoses. These are substantive aspects of the result, whereas repeated descriptions of confidence intervals and dependent folds can be condensed. A higher observed score is a finding that can be stated directly without claiming a universally superior algorithm or a demonstrated causal benefit of pretraining.

The results audit reports a corrected RF experiment that is newer than the RF scores in the supplied PDF. The comparison must be updated as a coherent experiment, with a brief provenance explanation, rather than substituting a more favorable RF score into selected sentences. Final replacement numbers must come from that audit. In particular, the PDF's RF score of 0.411 and S-JEPA–RF difference of 0.046 are historical values, not values to carry forward automatically.

## What can be simplified without weakening the paper

The main text needs the held-out unit, the rule used to select the checkpoint, the score definition, and a readable account of variability. It does not need three successive paragraphs explaining conditional intervals, overlapping training folds, and standard errors in §6.3. A short statement that the comparison is based on this small collection and varies across held-out groups preserves the practical interpretation, provided the numerical results and full uncertainty analysis remain available in supporting material.

Likewise, the effective-rank plot is not the strongest evidence for the requested contribution. A classifier using frozen features on unseen sources is the direct test of whether those features are useful for this task. The rank diagnostic can support the narrower observation that the network did not give the same representation to every input in the inspected batches. It cannot establish that disease-specific or temporally informative features were learned.

Use “trained with only 24 source videos per fold” only after the notebook audit confirms those partitions. Avoid “trained on 88 videos,” “41 independent participants,” “diagnostic accuracy,” “statistically proven,” “clinically validated,” “label-free classification,” or “the first S-JEPA for neurological gait” without additional evidence. “Self-supervised” applies to encoder training; the final classifier and checkpoint selection use the collection labels.

## Figure and layout findings

All original figures are readable at page scale, with no observed clipping or collisions. Figure 1 provides a useful overview and Figure 2 makes landmark selection visible. Figure 3 dominates the opening of the results despite its secondary evidential role; reducing or relocating it would improve the scientific emphasis. Figure 4 should be regenerated from the coherent updated comparison, and its method markers should differ by shape or line style as well as color. Discrete fold groups are easier to interpret as grouped points or columns than connected lines; if lines are retained, the caption's statement that fold order is not a time trend remains helpful.

Table 3 is the natural main results table. Its primary-score column should be labeled in readable terms such as “Equal weight per source video,” with macro-F1 defined in the caption or heading. Keep the alternative equal-clip score available so that source weighting is transparent. A compact source-level weighting illustration would be more useful for a general research audience than a second long paragraph of equations.

## Remaining author-level facts

The current PDF gives no actual consent/permission basis or ethical-review decision for collecting health-labeled public videos. The authors need to supply those facts and the intended data-sharing policy. The review cannot infer an exemption, licenses for the source footage, clinically verified diagnoses, or participant-disjoint evaluation from filenames. These are provenance questions, not reasons to weaken the measured algorithm comparison.

The document also cannot claim that a random-encoder or temporal-order control has been completed unless such an experiment appears in the verified results. A positive paper can state the higher observed score over the evaluated handcrafted baseline and explain the small training setting while leaving the mechanism of that advantage as the next research question.

## Additional close prior work to include

The original bibliography omits two particularly relevant studies. Add a short comparison at the end of §2.2 on page 2, before §2.3. This strengthens the paper's clinical and methodological context without asserting priority for learned neurological gait analysis.

Kaur et al.'s [primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36126031/) describes multi-view video, estimated 3D joints, MS/PD/matched older-adult groups, and evaluations across people and walking tasks. The [authors' research repository](https://github.com/kaurrachneet6/Vision-Based-Gait-Analysis-Framework-for-Predicting-Multiple-Sclerosis) confirms OpenPose extraction, calibrated 3D reconstruction, and group-separated evaluation procedures. IEEE full-text access was blocked, so this review does not claim to have inspected the full journal manuscript. The proposed bounded description below is supported by the primary abstract and author documentation. The author's project webpage contains material copied from a different treadmill paper and should not be used as the citation authority.

The full [GaitForeMer author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.00106) was checked, especially Figure 1 and §§2.1, 3.1–3.2, and 4.1. Its pretraining combines motion forecasting with supervised activity classification; calling the entire procedure label-free would be inaccurate. It subsequently predicts clinician-rated PD gait-impairment severity from estimated 3D skeletons. That task differs from the current paper's three collection labels. Its published scores should not be placed alongside the present results as a direct benchmark.

```latex
Kaur et al. used multi-view video and estimated 3D joints to classify gait from people with MS, PD, and matched older adults, including evaluations across participants and walking tasks~\cite{kaur2023}. GaitForeMer combined motion forecasting with activity classification during pretraining and then adapted its representations to PD gait-impairment severity~\cite{gaitforemer}. “A small collection” is preferable to an unqualified “a smaller collection”: this study has fewer videos than GaitForeMer's external pretraining dataset, but video counts cannot be equated with the participant counts in either clinical study.
```
